
### Amendment 23, __NOW__ (after the trial of Amendment 22 and before its lives are tested; before any code of this one, before any trial, before any life of these arms): her body brings its own logarithm and its own e-to-the-power. A move, for her home

**What was found** (Friday 11/27, while her home was built; `second/src/samemath.mjs`, `second/test/t0-same-math.mjs`). Her
body uses two pieces of the machine's own arithmetic in her draw: e to the power of a score (for her chances), and a
logarithm (for the jitter each hand adds). Node on the MSI and Node on GitHub's machine give the same values for both. The
Workers runtime, where her home will run, does not: about one value in fifteen differs in its last place. So the same body
lived a different life in her home than in the lab from her 54th visit on. Her nightly replay would have failed on her
first day. This is what the check "the same marks in her home as in the lab" (B5) is for.

**What her home does about it today.** Her Worker puts a copy of the two functions, written out in plain JavaScript from
the C library that Node carries (fdlibm), in place of the runtime's own before her body runs. With that her life is the
same in Node and in the Workers runtime (lives 91 to 93, 2,000 rooms each). It works, but it sits around her body, and a
missed swap would not be seen until the replay failed.

**What moves into her body.** Her body's own code does the two calculations itself, with the plain-JavaScript functions
(a new file in `core/`), and no longer asks the machine. They use only adding, taking away, multiplying and dividing,
which every machine does the same way. Her RULE page says kind 8 for such a body; no new number on it. Her Worker then
swaps nothing.

**The move. It has no luck in it.** Four arms that differ from Amendment 22's only in where the two calculations come
from: O-P-home, N-P-home, O-P-homes and O-P-home-x25, against O-P-wide, N-P-wide, O-P-wides and O-P-wide-x25. Lives 1 to
20 of each on the MSI: exactly the same record of every visit, the same flips at every save point, the same probe G, the
same learned weights and the same usual in every cell at the end, the same count of every cell's stream, the same visit
count, and the same flips in 100 test rooms with learning off.
- If all 80 lives are the same, her own arithmetic gives on the MSI exactly what the machine's gave, for every value her
  lives met, and every earlier result holds for the body she is born with.
- If a life differs, Node's own functions are not what the plain copy says for some value, and it is found before
  anything is said.
**And in her home's runtime:** life 91, 2,000 rooms, lived in the Workers runtime with nothing swapped: the same marks as
in Node at every visit (her home's own test, run again on the new body).

**Two runs, as a record and for the second machine.** Freeze `home`: lives 1 to 10 of the four arms with the frozen lives
1 to 10 of O-P-wide, N-P-wide, O-P-wide-x25 and O-B-step-start. Freeze `home2`: lives 11 to 20 likewise. Each names its own
later beacon round. The second machine's replay of these two tests is then a replay with her own arithmetic.

**My guesses (blind: written before any code of this amendment and before any trial).**
1. All 80 lives are the same on the MSI (90%). The plain copy matched Node on 33 million values in her home's test.
2. In the Workers runtime, with nothing swapped, life 91 has the same marks as in Node at all 2,000 visits (92%).

**What it would mean.** If both hold, the body she is born with is the arm O-P-home, and it lives the same life on the
MSI, on the second machine and in her home. That is what her nightly replay rests on.
