Hi, and thanks for jumping in. This is a design job, not a research sweep. ## Your main job

## What Jaron wants covered (his ideas are sparks; test them, don't assume them)
1. **People in positions.** Hackers already sitting inside networks: China's Volt Typhoon pre-positioned in US power/water/transport, per US agencies; Salt Typhoon in telecoms. North Korean fake IT workers inside US companies. Reported spy recruitment: the NYT reported China's MSS recruited a woman who had an affair with Nvidia's CEO, and he resigned 11/12. How should the desk track "who is sitting where" and what they could do?
2. **Who meets whom.** A meetings/visits ledger: leaders, ministers, military chiefs, envoys, and what happens before and after.
3. **What happens alongside events.** Co-occurrence: outages, attacks or market moves that line up with summits, deadlines or other crises. This must be measured against a baseline, so coincidence gets a fair chance.
4. **A modern escalation ladder.** Herman Kahn's 44-rung ladder (1965) is beautiful systems thinking, but it is missing what changed since: cyber pre-positioning and disruption, information ops and false flags, economic pressure (tariffs, export controls, rare earths, chip supply), AI/chips, space (satellites, jamming), undersea cables, proxies and deniable attacks. Propose how these get their own rungs or tracks next to Kahn's. Read each pair (US-China, China-Taiwan, Russia-NATO, US-Iran, North Korea) AND the Russia-China-Iran-North Korea core as one side. Log both views.
5. **His hypotheses to test fairly** (each next to its competitors, including plain coincidence): (a) small disruptions could be distractions that keep the US busy or panicked; (b) an attack could be made to look like it came from Iran when it came from China (misattribution), so the public is slow to back a response against China; (c) many moves now look like positioning for an eventual China-Taiwan crisis; (d) reshoring (tariffs, chip fabs like Intel) is partly about the US surviving a big war without collapsing.

## The honesty rules the desk must have built in (these are what make it worth running)
- Jaron's principle P-0166: "Words about the unknown are spent credibility." Never claim a connection the evidence doesn't show, and never wave one away either. Every link gets a label: ESTABLISHED (with source), REPORTED (claim, named source), NOT ESTABLISHED EITHER WAY, or RULED OUT (with source). Never write "no evidence".
- Prefer methods that stop us fitting stories after the fact: indicators written in ADVANCE for each scenario (what we would expect to see first if X were coming), competing hypotheses scored against the same evidence, a baseline for "how often does this happen anyway", and a red-team seat whose job is to break each claimed link.
- It must not become a fear machine. A recent X video stacked 67 headlines on a globe as if everything were connected. The desk is the calm, checked version.
- Satya alone sets the safety level. The desk feeds Satya; it doesn't set levels.
