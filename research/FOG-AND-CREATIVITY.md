# Fog and creativity (Jaron's research, started 11/6/2026)

**His idea (9:55 PM):** daily stress and other people consuming your time cover creativity. When that clears, what comes out is beyond what he can explain. He doesn't think it's special to him: people everywhere are fogged by survival work, so AI taking over that work could bring more meaning, not less.

**Test 1, his own court week.** He called it BEFORE it happened, which is what makes it worth counting.
- Fog week: Wed 11/4 to Tue 11/10 (court). Clear week: Wed 11/11 to Tue 11/17.
- Count each week: new ideas or plans added to projects/strategy/INDEX.md, new principles in projects/principles/LEDGER.md, new builds he starts, his messages per day (state/usage-ledger.json, human_messages). Plus his own word on how it felt.
- Honest limits: one person, one week, and a dam can break just from ideas saved up. Repeat it around other stressful dates (5 to 10 of them) before calling it a pattern.

**What outside research already says** (from memory, re-check before publishing):
- Backs the fog: Mani, Mullainathan, Shafir, Zhao, Science 2013, "Poverty Impedes Cognitive Function". The same sugarcane farmers tested better after harvest pay than before it, and money worries lowered test scores in a lab. Book: Scarcity (2013). Some later studies did not fully repeat it.
- Pushes back on the meaning part: people who lose jobs involuntarily tend to have lower wellbeing, even past the lost income. That may be about status, money and structure rather than the work itself, and it comes from a world where losing a job is a crisis. Whether meaning grows when survival is covered is not established; guaranteed-income trials are the closest evidence.

**Fog is per person (Jaron, 11/6 10:06 PM):** what counts as fog depends on what someone is going through. The same event can be fog for one person and nothing for another, or change for the same person when their life changes. Some fog comes from wants people create for themselves, not from needs. Things that remove people and demands can bring clarity, even in places nobody would choose. So court is only one layer, and the clear week may not be fully clear: other things stay on him after it. Count that in the comparison. Personal details are in the private day log.

**Same load, different fog over time (Jaron, 11/7 11:20 AM):** one obligation can be almost no fog at first and heavy fog later, once debt piles up, a deadline appears and a record starts counting against you. Fog grows from consequence and time pressure, not from the obligation existing. That is why an outsider can call it a contradiction to be calm about something early and stressed about it later.

**Mood shift data point (11/8 morning, details in the private day log):** in the fog week, he noticed a clear shift from reacting to caring and a much more positive day, after a morning that held several changes at once (a walk, a long values talk, writing and posting something caring, plus one personal change logged privately). One morning cannot separate them; worth noting each time it happens so a pattern can show.

**Hopelessness spending (Jaron 11/9):** when saving for a life-changing goal feels unreachable, people spend on numbing (subscriptions, entertainment) instead. This is a fog loop: the fog makes the goal feel impossible, the numbing keeps the fog. Matches research on scarcity and present bias (Mullainathan/Shafir) and on low expected future gains reducing saving. See P-0133.

**Doorway pressure (Jaron 11/10, data point):** frustration builds while getting ready to go outside, peaking as tasks get added; it vanishes the moment he steps out. Likely nicotine craving plus a place that feels unwelcoming (fog from the living situation), plus overheating. Worth logging with the watch data later (heart rate before vs after stepping out).

**Where his thinking works (Jaron 11/10):** outside, moving (pacing), feeling welcome, with no one he knows watching; voice-to-text only when alone. Indoors with others his voice and flow shut off. Fits research on walking and creative thinking (Oppezzo and Schwartz 2014, Stanford: walking raised creative idea output about 60% over sitting). A fog factor: being watched or unwelcome.
