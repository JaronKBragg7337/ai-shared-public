- **My biggest change to the usual design: "move" is level 4, not level 5.** By the time collapse is plainly here, the road
  is the dangerous place. 5 means "be where you are going to be, and hold."
- **The scale does not care about the cause.** It measures what stopped working, how close, and for how long. An ice storm
  that kills the power for a week in January is a 4 without any enemy at all, and it is the most likely 4 Fort Wayne will see.

## The idea in one picture

Most threat scales measure how scary the news is. This one measures **how many of the things life runs on have stopped
working, how close to us, and whether more is coming.**

**The nine things life runs on ("functions"):**
power, water, fuel, food and pharmacy, payments (cards, ATMs, EBT, banks), phones and internet, emergency help (911,
hospitals), roads and air, and order (police answer calls, stores are not being looted).

**Four rings (how close):**
- **Home** - within about 50 miles of where a household sleeps. Each household has its own home ring; anyone in the network
  who lives elsewhere gets their own.
- **Region** - within about 250 miles (Chicago, Indianapolis, Detroit, Toledo, Columbus, Cincinnati, Peoria), OR anything
  we are wired to no matter the miles: our power grid operator, the refineries and pipelines that feed Indiana, the systems
  that carry our payments.
- **Nation** and **World** - everything else.

**Open or closed (is more coming?):** an event is **closed** only when all four are true: the cause is known from evidence
(not just a statement), whoever did it is caught or it was an accident with the fault found, it was one place and one
function, and the thing works again. Anything else is **open.**

Those three ideas (functions, rings, open/closed) are the whole machine. Everything below is them applied.

## 1. The scale

Each level: what is true (any ONE box is enough) and what the network does. The number shown is always the highest box
that is checked.

### 0 - Quiet
**True:** nothing open anywhere that touches a function. Nothing down.
**Do:** live. Keep the standing kit (two weeks of water, food and medicine; a half tank or better; some small bills; the
household card that says where you go and who you call).

### 1 - Heads up
**True (any one):**
- An open event somewhere in the Nation or World that touches a function, one place, not spreading.
- A closed event in the Region.
- A serious report (plot, attack, failure) that nothing checkable backs up yet.
- An old event newly reported.
- A storm or cold watch for Home.

**Do:** know about it. Nothing else. No buzz; it shows on the page.

### 2 - Top off
**True (any one):**
- An open event in the Region (attack, or a failure nobody can explain), one place.
- A function failed nationwide for 2+ hours and the cause is still open, even if it is working again.
- Two open events in different functions inside 7 days, anywhere in the Nation.
- A plot report that IS backed by one costly action (see section 2: flights restricted, schedules cancelled, embassies
  emptied, airlines pulling routes).
- One function shaky at Home: storm warning plus a grid-operator alert; more than 1 in 10 gas stations dry in a Region
  city; cards or EBT down statewide 4+ hours; local hospitals turning ambulances away.
- A known flashpoint date (disputed election result, a verdict, a shooting video) AND stores in a Region city boarding up
  or the Guard being pre-positioned.

**Do:** the things that cost nothing if it turns out to be nothing, all doable in an hour: fill the tank, get cash, refill
prescriptions, charge everything, check the kit, tell your people where you are sleeping tonight. One buzz, daytime only.

### 3 - Get set
**True (any one; needs two hard sources, or one member's own eyes plus one hard source):**
- One function down at Home with no fix time, or a fix time over 24 hours.
- Two functions down across the Region, or across several states anywhere, 6+ hours, cause open.
- Three open events in different functions inside 72 hours anywhere in the Nation, or two at once in the Region. (This is
  the "opening move" pattern.)
- A foreign state confirmed to have actually knocked out a function on US soil (not "got into a system": knocked it out).
- An open attack at Home (whoever did it is still out there).
- Banks anywhere in the country limiting withdrawals or closing; or card networks down nationwide 4+ hours.
- Two nights running of looting or riots in a Region city.
- A multi-state grid failure anywhere in the country with no fix time, even far away. (The trucks, the payments and the
  panic-buying reach us in a day or two. We get set while the shelves are still full.)

**Do:** pack the vehicles. Bring the household together. No trips you don't need. Check in morning and night. Decide stay
or go NOW while it is calm, so level 4 is one word, not a discussion. Households on the early list (section 3, rule 7) act
as if it is 4. This buzz wakes people.

### 4 - Move
**True (any one; same proof rule as 3):**
- Two or more functions down at Home with no fix time, or a fix time over 48 hours. (In freezing weather, power counts as
  two: power and heat.)
- Power down across the Region with no fix time, whether or not our own lights are still on.
- The far-away grid or payment failure from level 3 has reached us: local stations dry, shelves stripped, cards failing,
  stores closing early. Or it has run 72 hours with no fix.
- Cards, ATMs or EBT down nationwide over 24 hours; or banks in Indiana limiting withdrawals.
- More than half the gas stations at Home dry with no resupply date.
- Looting or riots at Home spreading past one neighborhood; or in three or more Region cities at once; or a curfew at Home.
- Two or more attacks in the Region inside 72 hours with nobody caught; or a foreign state knocking out a function in the
  Region.
- A state military strike on US soil.

**Do:** run the plan today, in daylight. Go to the place your household already picked, or lock in at home if home IS the
place. Hours matter here because the roads, the pumps and the stores still mostly work, and tomorrow they may not.
**"Move" means "do your plan," and for many households the right plan is to stay.**

### 5 - Hold
**True:**
- Three or more functions down at Home with no fix date, and one of them is order or emergency help (stores being looted
  and nobody stopping it, 911 not answering, police not coming); OR
- Level 4 has been true at Home for 72+ hours and no outside help is arriving.

**Do:** be where you are and stay there. Travel only to get away from direct danger. Households group up. Check-ins at set
times by the backup method. Guard water, heat and medicine. **Stay away from the flashpoints: grocery stores, pharmacies,
gas stations, gun shops, crowds.** That is where Jaron's picture of collapse actually happens, and the record of past
disasters agrees with him on the places even where it disagrees on the people (see "Where I argue with Jaron").

### How often each level should happen (the cry-wolf budget)
| Level | Expected | If it happens more than this, the boxes are too loose |
|---|---|---|
| 0-1 | most days | |
| 2 | a handful of times a year, days at a time | more than about 12 times a year |
| 3 | once a year or less | more than twice a year |
| 4 | about once a decade | |
| 5 | hopefully never | |

### Checked against history (what the scale would have said, for the people who lived there)
| Event | Level | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 9/11, seen from Fort Wayne | 3 | foreign attack, open, air travel down nationwide. Not 4: everything at home worked |
| 2003 blackout, Detroit/Toledo/Cleveland | 4 | power down across a region, no fix time. Fort Wayne (lights on): 3 |
| 2008 bank crisis | 2, a few days of 3 | banks failing, but cards and ATMs kept working |
| March 2020 | 3 | food and pharmacy shaky everywhere, cause open, weeks long |
| CrowdStrike outage, July 2024 | 2 for a day, then 1 | air, banks, some 911 down nationwide; cause known in hours = closed |
| Texas freeze 2021, in Texas | 4, some places 5 | power, heat and water down for days |
| Katrina, New Orleans | 5 | every function gone, order gone, help not arriving |
| Helene 2024, western NC | 5 | roads, power, water, phones gone for a week or more |
| 2020 unrest, in the cities hit | 3-4 | order failing locally, everything else working |
| Colonial Pipeline 2021, Southeast | 3 | fuel down across a region. Fort Wayne: 1 |

That table is the real test. If a level 4 here does not feel like Detroit in August 2003 or Texas in February 2021, the
scale is being read wrong.

## 2. What I would measure that most people don't

The rule for picking a signal: **someone had to spend money or take a risk to produce it.** Talk is free. Closing an
airport is not.

**Meters (is a function actually down?)**
- Power: poweroutage.us by county; the I&M outage map; the grid operator's public emergency notices (PJM, which Fort Wayne
  sits on, and MISO next door). The operator's cold-weather and hot-weather alerts come a day or more ahead.
- Phones and internet: Downdetector, but only when several carriers or banks spike at once; Cloudflare Radar and Georgia
  Tech's IODA for whole-region drops.
- Air: the FAA's live status page (ground stops) and its flight restriction list. A sudden security restriction is an
  action someone had to order.
- Fuel: GasBuddy's station-outage tracker; the government's Wednesday fuel stock report for the Midwest (diesel and
  gasoline in our own region matter far more than the national crude reserve); pipeline company shipper notices.
- Payments: the card networks' and the Fed's public status pages; **EBT outages and missed benefit dates.** When benefits
  don't load, the stores that depend on them see trouble inside days. It is the earliest plain sign of the collapse path
  Jaron describes, and almost nobody watches it.
- Emergency help: FCC 911 outage notices; hospital diversion status; the Indiana DHS county travel-status map.
- Space weather: NOAA's storm alerts (a severe solar storm is a grid threat with hours to days of warning, free).

**Costly actions by people who know more than we do**
- State Department ordering staff out of a country; embassies closing.
- Airlines dropping routes; ship insurers raising war prices; ships rerouting.
- Governors' Guard call-ups (public orders). The federal cyber agency's emergency directives.
- Utilities calling in crews from other states BEFORE a storm.
- Big retailers closing early or boarding windows. Banks posting withdrawal limits.

**Money plumbing, not prices**
- Whether banks will lend to each other overnight (the published overnight rate jumping), a bad Treasury auction, the
  Friday bank-failure list. Prices fall all the time. Plumbing rarely breaks, and when it does, cards stop.

**The cheap local ones nobody uses**
- **Listener counts on the Fort Wayne / Allen County police and fire scanner feeds.** When something real is happening
  locally, the count jumps before any news story exists.
- Google search spikes by metro for "power outage," "cell service down," "gas shortage."
- **The network itself.** Fifty people are fifty sensors, and some of them work in exactly the right places (stores,
  hospitals, trucks, utilities). One tap: "shelves / gas line / ATM / power / police." Two members reporting the same thing
  independently counts as a hard source. This is the best signal on the list and it costs nothing.

**Popular signals I would NOT use** (they cry wolf): crypto price, stock index drops by themselves, government-plane
tracking (they fly training runs daily), "pizza orders near the Pentagon," hobby DEFCON sites, viral threads with no place,
time or second source.

## 3. Rules

1. **Highest box wins.** Levels don't add. The only thing that stacks is the cluster rule (two or three open events in
   different functions close together), because that pattern is the thing we most need to not miss.
2. **Statements can raise the number. They can never lower it.** "Zero evidence of cyber" does not close an event. What
   closes it: the thing works again, the fault is shown, and time passes with nothing else.
3. **But silence is evidence too, on a clock.** "No evidence isn't safety" can turn into a trap where every unexplained
   failure is a hidden attack forever. So every open event carries a clock. An "opening move" with no second move in 30
   days was not an opening move. Most big failures are accidents (the 2023 nationwide ground stop was a contractor
   deleting files; CrowdStrike was a bad update). Unexplained holds at 2. It does not get to 3 without company.
4. **Distance is connection, not just miles.** Far events cap at 2. A far event reaches 3 only as a pattern (cluster) or
   as a system we depend on failing. **Nothing reaches 4 on news alone:** 4 needs a function actually down in the Home or
   Region ring, or a strike on US soil.
5. **Proof.** A report with nothing behind it caps at 1. One costly action behind it lifts the cap to 2. Level 3 and up
   needs two hard sources (meters, orders, maps) or a member's own eyes plus one. Social posts are welcome as the first
   alarm; they start the checking, they don't set the number.
6. **Rounding.** In the first 24 hours of an open event, a tie goes UP. After 24 hours with no new facts, a tie goes
   DOWN. A thin cushion in the same function (rule 9) also rounds up.
7. **The early list.** Some households act one level ahead of the number: anyone who needs power for medical gear or
   cold medicine, late pregnancy, newborns, people who can't move fast, anyone living alone far out.
8. **Decay.** Up can jump several levels at once. Down goes one level at a time, at most once a day.
   - Closed event: down one level after 24 hours, gone after 72.
   - Open event: holds 72 hours, then down one level for each 7 quiet days.
   - Open event with a foreign state named: still counts toward clusters for 30 days after it leaves the number.
   - A function that failed: no drop until it has worked for 48 hours straight.
9. **Thin cushions (slow background facts).** Low fuel reserves, a grid operator's winter shortage warning, a drug
   shortage, a state shown to be inside water systems. These never raise the number by themselves. They mean a hit to
   that function becomes a failure faster, so events in that function round up. They are listed on the page under the
   number.
10. **Date of the event, not date of the headline.** An attack in July reported in November counts as July. Otherwise a
    busy news week manufactures a cluster out of old stories.
11. **Base rates.** If a thing fails by itself often, one more failure means less. (My search shows at least two other
    FAA communication failures this year, in August and September, both without an attacker. That is a reason the FAA stop
    sits at 2, and also a reason to watch whether the next one is bigger.)
12. **Domestic or foreign: the real question is "open or closed, and aimed at what?"** See the argument below. In rule
    form: closed events decay fast whoever did them; open events don't; attacks aimed at functions rank above attacks
    aimed at attention; a foreign state is always open.
13. **One number, one arrow, one word.** Shown as "2 ↑ partly": the level, which way it is leaning, and how solid the
    proof is (solid / partly / thin). Thin proof can't go above 2.
14. **Buzz rules.** 1 never buzzes. 2 buzzes once, in the daytime. 3 and up buzz at any hour. Going down is a quiet note.
    Never two buzzes for the same step.
15. **Orders and your own eyes beat the number.** If officials order your address out, go. If you see level-4 things
    out your window, act and report; don't wait for the page.
16. **The silence rule.** The signal travels over phones and internet, which are two of the nine functions. So: the page
    shows when it was last checked, and a check older than 6 hours displays as "STALE," never as calm. And every
    household knows the rule: **power and phones both out for 6 hours and you can't reach the signal = treat it as 4 and
    run your plan.** Meeting place and time are agreed ahead.
17. **No approve button, but a hard limit.** The AIs set 0-3 straight from the boxes. 4 and 5 need the boxes met AND a
    second, different AI reading the same sources and getting the same level, or a member's own-eyes report. That is a
    limit in the script, not a person in the way.
18. **Keep score in public.** Every call of 2 or higher is logged with its reason and graded 30 days later: right, early,
    or false alarm. The false-alarm count sits on the page. A signal people can audit is one they keep listening to.

### Where I argue with Jaron

**"Collapse is the top."** Agree, and it is why the whole scale is built on functions instead of attacks. Two additions.
First, **the move has to come before the top**: the window to move is while roads and pumps work, so move is 4 and the top
is "hold." Second, the record of real disasters (Katrina, Helene, the Texas freeze, the 2003 blackout) says most people
help each other for the first days, and the violence gathers at particular places: stores, pharmacies, fuel, old
grudges. It spreads with **time** and with **no help coming.** His picture is right for a long failure with no rescue. It
is too dark for a three-day one. So the scale makes duration and "is help arriving" the line between 4 and 5, and tells
people to avoid the flashpoints rather than fear their neighbors.

**"No evidence isn't safety."** Agree, and rule 2 builds it in. But it needs rule 3 beside it or the scale can never come
back down and people stop listening.

**"Domestic with a motive is lower; foreign or unexplained is higher."** Mostly right, but nationality is the wrong
handle. Three cases break it. (a) A domestic bomber who has not been caught can strike again; known motive or not, that
is open. (b) Domestic groups have gone after power substations more than once; that is a function attack and ranks high
no matter who does it. (c) The domestic "statement" events (a verdict, a disputed election) are the ones that split
people into sides, and that is his own collapse path. A foreign strike tends to pull a country together for a while; a
domestic split does the opposite. So the scale asks: is it over, and was it aimed at something life runs on?

**"Far raises awareness, near or systemic means move."** Agree completely; it is rule 4. One sharpening: "near" should
mean "connected." A refinery fire 150 miles away that feeds our pumps is nearer than a riot 60 miles away.

## What the brief missed

1. **Weather and accidents.** The most likely 3 or 4 for Fort Wayne is an ice storm, a deep freeze or a derecho taking the
   power, or a rail chemical spill forcing an evacuation. Winter starts now. The scale handles them because it is blind to
   cause, but the feeds (weather service, grid operator, state travel map) need wiring in.
2. **"Move" where?** The number is useless unless each household already has a one-page card: stay or go, where, which
   road, who rides with whom, where to meet if phones are dead. For many households the right answer is stay.
3. **The signal dies when it is needed most** (rule 16). There needs to be a no-phone fallback and a rule everyone knows.
4. **Not everyone lives in Fort Wayne.** The number has to be worked out per household ring.
5. **Coming back down.** A scale that only goes up is a scale people mute. Decay and the public scorecard are as important
   as the triggers.
6. **Slow collapse has no first day.** An economy doesn't break at 2:14 PM. The function count (shelves, pumps, cards,
   benefits) catches slow failure the same way it catches fast failure; an event-driven scale would not.
7. **Fake reports.** If member reports can raise the number, they must come from known members, and two must agree.
8. **The early list** (rule 7). A single number for fifty people ignores that some need a head start.

Written after reading `SCALE-REVIEW-DRAFT.md` (his notes, proposals v2 and v3, the 3:51 AM key rule), `tools/safety_signal.py`

### First, the numbers don't mean the same thing
The three versions use the same digits for different actions. Compared by what people DO:

| What the network does | Live script | Team v2 | Mine |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top off gas, check supplies | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| Gas full, cash, test message | 3 | 3 | 2 |
| Packed, vehicles ready, checked in | 4 | 4 | 3 |
| Run the plan | 5 | 5 | 4 |
| Hold in place, off the roads | (none) | (none) | 5 |

**Tonight, by action:** live script says 4 (packed, vehicles ready). Team v2 says 3 and I say 2, and those are the SAME
action: top off, cash, check in. Two designs built apart landed on the same thing to do, one step cooler than what is
live. Jaron's own "closer to a 3" on the old numbering agrees.

### Where mine is better
1. **It would not have called Peoria a 5.** The live box "an attack, alert or evacuation order for Indiana or the
   Midwest = MOVE" told 50 people to run the transportation plan over one site 230 miles away. Nobody moved, and nobody
   should have. That is the exact cry-wolf the scale exists to prevent. Mine needs two functions down at home, or a
   whole region dark, before "move."
2. **It would not have gone 3 to 4 on a four-month-old attack.** The water-system report raised the live signal because
   the headline was new. Rule 10 (date of the event, not the headline) stops that.
3. **It comes back down.** The live rules are "highest box" plus "unsure = higher" with no decay, so it only ratchets:
   3, 4, 5, 4 in two days and it has sat at "vehicles ready" since. v3's 72-hour decay fixes most of this; my
   open/closed clock is finer (a caught bomber clears in a day, an unsolved one doesn't).
4. **"An attack on US soil anywhere" is too loose a box for a high level** (live 4, v2 4). The country has several of
   those most years. Each one would put the network at "vehicles ready." Mine asks how close, is it over, and was it aimed
   at something life runs on.
5. **v2's level 5 has two loose boxes:** "anything ordered within 150 miles" (a chemical-spill evacuation in another
   city would fire MOVE) and "a national emergency order" (dozens are in force at any time). Function boxes are harder to
   trip by accident.
6. **No wasted level.** The team's 2 and 3 are nearly the same action (top off gas vs gas full plus cash). I spend that
   number on "hold," which is a different instruction from "move" and the one that keeps people off the road at the worst
   time.
7. **Cause-blind.** Every live box is about an enemy. An ice storm, the likeliest way Fort Wayne loses power for a week,
   is not on the live scale at all.
8. **Checked against history** (the table in section 1), plus a budget for how often each level may fire and a public
   scorecard.

### Where theirs is better
1. **It knows there is a war on. Mine didn't.** Levels 1-3 of the live script are a ladder for a conflict abroad
   (new targets, new places, land infrastructure, a fuel shock). That is real early warning and my scale has almost none
   of it: war abroad sits at 1 for me until something breaks here.
2. **Nuclear.** They have a box for threats moving from words to steps. I left it out entirely. That is a hole.
3. **The free DHS alert feed is already wired in and runs by itself.** I listed a dozen feeds and missed the one that
   works today. (I'd score it lower than 4, though: bulletins were in force for most of 2021-2023. Bulletin = 2, an
   "elevated" alert = 3, an "imminent" alert naming our region = 4.)
4. **Simpler.** Satya runs this four times a day. One table of boxes can be applied the same way every time. My 18 rules
   would drift in a bot's hands unless they are boiled down into the script.
5. **Two dials (v3).** Showing THREAT and STABILITY side by side says at a glance WHY the number is what it is. Mine
   folds threat into the fine print (open/closed, clusters). His field-of-view need (note 3) is better served by theirs.
6. **The baseline rule** (weeks of the same attacks in the same zone are background; only NEW counts) is my base-rate
   rule said better.
7. **Pattern labels** (causal chain / shared cause / same time only). My cluster rule counts events; theirs makes you say
   how they are related before counting. That is the better habit.
8. **Stability signs I didn't have:** police sick-outs, insurers pausing policies, jail incidents, "everyone is going"
   talk in local groups.
9. **Jaron's 3:51 AM rule** (the signal's real job is the days BEFORE; printed card; a trigger that needs no signal). I
   reached the same place from the roads side (move at 4, silence rule). His is stated more plainly and came from him.

### What I would steal, and what I'd change in mine now
- **A wartime rule (new, from context I lacked):** while the US is trading fire with a state, an open attack on a
  function site in the Region counts one level higher until it is shown to be one site with no second device. Under it,
  Peoria was a 3 on my scale (packed, ready) while the evacuation was live, and 2 again once it lifted with nothing else
  found. That matches Jaron's "4" on the old numbering. **Tonight stays 2 ↑** (three separate level-2 boxes checked: open
  Region event, unexplained nationwide failure, officials moved to protection; no level-3 box). Proof word moves from
  "partly" to "solid."
- **Their war ladder as my levels 1-2:** US forces trading fire = 1. Something new (new place, land infrastructure hit,
  national fuel shock) = 2. Nuclear threat moving to steps = 3. Nuclear use anywhere, or a strike on US soil = 4.
- **Two dials on the page, one number on the buzz.**
- **Pattern labels on every cluster.**
- **Their extra stability signs,** added to section 2.
- **Their backtest:** run my boxes over the 48 past readings and count how many days each version would have spent at
  each level. That one table settles most of this argument with data.

### The merge I would put in front of Jaron
Keep the team's shape (one table, two dials, the war ladder low on the scale, decay, confidence tag). Change four things:
1. **4 = move, 5 = hold.** It follows from his own 3:51 AM rule.
2. **Stability boxes decide 3 and up; threat boxes alone stop at 3.** (v3 says this for 5. I'd say it for 4 too.)
