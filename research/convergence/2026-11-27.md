## China and Taiwan

Taiwan's Defense Ministry recorded 1 PLA aircraft sortie and 7 navy ships from 6 a.m. Thursday to 6 a.m. Friday (UTC+8). It said that one sortie entered the southwestern and eastern ADIZ. The morning text does not list an official ship, does not list balloons, and lists no rocket launch. Taiwan sent aircraft, ships, and coastal missiles. Taiwan News, on that morning report, described the sortie as a drone and put the month's running total at 267 aircraft and 242 ships. The ministry's own English text says aircraft, not drone. The air count is down from 2 sorties in the previous window. The navy count around the island is up from 6 to 7. No source said that overnight count was timed to the grain-ship claim, to Kuwait, or to Saturday's vote. This window ends at 6 a.m. It is not part of the patrol below, and the two counts do not add.

From 9:25 a.m. Friday the same ministry reported a separate wave. Its English post said 30 sorties were detected, including J-10, J-16, and Su-30 fighters, H-6K bombers, and a KJ-500 early-warning aircraft. It said 26 of the 30 crossed the median line into the northern, central, southwestern, and eastern ADIZ, then went on to the western Pacific for joint air-sea training and long-distance flights. It said the armed forces monitored the activity and responded. The post does not give a ship count. Lianhe Zaobao and Free Malaysia Today, citing the ministry, called the same activity a joint combat readiness patrol with warships and included drones in the mix. The X post's aircraft list does not use the word drone. China's Defense Ministry did not immediately comment. Free Malaysia Today said that on Thursday the ministry had called reunification an unstoppable trend and had said Taiwan's people could feel safer with a "heroic people's army." That is Thursday's line. It is not a Friday order. The last comparable patrol the ministry published was 21 November: 21 sorties, 15 of them across the median line. Friday's air count is higher. No source said the flights were ordered to move Saturday's vote.

Reuters reported Friday that China has been running a weeks-long amphibious landing drill at Dacheng Bay in Fujian, about 280 km from Taiwan. Geospatial firm ingeniSPACE first detected it on 1 November. Its analysis of images from 1 to 16 November showed vessels forming and dismantling a floating bridge about 800 metres long. Eight civilian vessels took part, four of them barges, two semi-submersible and two powered, with four landing craft, smaller support craft, and Chinese navy ships nearby. No purpose-built bridging gear was seen. Jason Wang, the firm's chief operating officer, told Reuters the PLA appeared to be rehearsing low-signature methods to move supplies for forces that could be used in a first-island-chain invasion, and that civilian ships used as a bridge from deeper water would add landing places along the Taiwan coast. A senior security official, speaking without a name, confirmed the details and said the drill was to "practise the swift seizure of small islands," that it was "very targeted" at the first island chain from Japan through Taiwan to the Philippines, and that it appeared to be part of this year's All-Military Strategic Exercise, routine but later than in past years. The same official said there is still "some distance between innovation and fantasy," and that the barges would be live targets in a war. China's Defense Ministry did not answer Reuters. On Thursday it had called its training in the region "entirely legitimate and reasonable." Taiwan's Defense Ministry said it knew of the activity at Dacheng Bay, including the civilian vessels, and was assessing what it means for the strait. The images are from the first half of November. The publication, and Taipei's public assessment, are Friday. No source said the drill was ordered for the election, for Ukraine, or for Hormuz.

Local elections are Saturday, 28 November, one day out. Voters choose mayors, magistrates, councils, and other local offices across 22 cities and counties. CNA put the ballot at 11,051 posts and nearly 20 million voters. Polls run from 8 a.m. to 4 p.m., with results expected in the evening. The Kuomintang is defending 14 of the 22 top seats. The Democratic Progressive Party holds five. In Taipei, Mayor Chiang Wan-an of the Kuomintang rallied on Ketagalan Boulevard. CNA said the crowd reached past Jingfu Gate, and named Legislative Speaker Han Kuo-yu, Taichung Mayor Lu Shiow-yen, New Taipei Mayor Hou Yu-ih, Eric Chu, and Hau Lung-bin, plus guests from the Taiwan People's Party. DPP candidate Puma Shen, whom Chinese police want on a separatism charge, cancelled the Ren'ai Road rally over traffic and went online. Focus Taiwan said the livestream drew more than 330,000 concurrent viewers on his campaign's YouTube channel. Reuters said it drew more than 500,000 viewers. Those are two different counts. Shen told Reuters earlier this month that Chinese police had never tried to contact him. Reuters said the campaigns have stayed on local issues such as transport and the environment. No new US chip-export rule was published Friday. No source in the ministry's posts ties the 30 sorties, or the Dacheng Bay bridge, to a ballot instruction.

## Russia and Ukraine

Ukraine's Air Force said that from 18:00 on Thursday, Russia launched 155 Shahed-type attack drones, 83 of them jet-powered, plus Gerbera drones and Parodiya decoys. Launch sites named were Oryol, Millerovo, Kursk, Shatalovo, Primorsko-Akhtarsk, occupied Donetsk, and Hvardiiske in occupied Crimea. As of 08:00 Friday the Air Force said 110 had been shot down or jammed, 51 of them jet-powered, with hits at 27 locations and debris at 4. Several drones were still airborne and the attack was ongoing. Those are Ukrainian Air Force figures, carried by Interfax. The drone wave is larger than Thursday morning's, which the Air Force had put at 113.

Prime Minister Serhii Koretskyi said the night's strikes hit railways, the energy system, civilian businesses, and homes in nine regions, and that one person was killed and at least six were injured. He said railway stations in Kryvyi Rih and Kamianske, and two coal mines, were hit in Dnipropetrovsk oblast, and that diesel locomotives were damaged. He said substations, including ones that feed the railway, were hit in Vinnytsia and Kirovohrad. He said production sites and warehouses were hit in Zaporizhzhia, Kyiv region, and Mykolaiv. He said a drone set an apartment building on fire in Izium, in Kharkiv oblast, injuring five people, that a private house was damaged in Odesa oblast, and that a dormitory and a residential building were hit in Sumy oblast. Those are his figures. Ukrinform said that by morning, consumers were without electricity in six regions, with the widest cuts in Dnipropetrovsk and Kherson. First deputy energy minister Artem Nekrasov said the same six oblasts, and named those two as the hardest hit.

A second compilation covers a longer day and does not match the prime minister's overnight toll. Kyiv Independent, citing local authorities, put the past day at 6 dead and 25 injured. It listed an 86-year-old woman killed in Kharkiv oblast, where an educational facility was damaged, one dead in Kherson, one dead and four injured in Zaporizhzhia on 26 November, one dead in Donetsk, one dead in Dnipropetrovsk, and a 70-year-old man killed in Odesa on 26 November, plus injuries in Zhytomyr and no casualties in Vinnytsia. The Kharkiv death and the educational facility match the university strike already logged Thursday. The 6 and Koretskyi's 1 are different clocks. Later Friday, Ukrinform reported a strike on an enterprise in Nizhyn that killed two and injured four, and a drone strike on Kremenchuk that injured two. Those came after the morning counts. The day's toll was still open. No Russian Defense Ministry statement on the Izium building or the Nizhyn enterprise was found in these reports.

On the ground, Ukraine's General Staff said Russian forces fought 136 engagements on Thursday, launched 81 air strikes, dropped 219 guided bombs, used 7,111 kamikaze drones, and conducted 1,888 shellings, 21 of them with multiple-launch rockets. The heaviest axis was Pokrovsk, with 16 attacks. Kostiantynivka had 14. Lyman had 6. Sloviansk and Huliaipole had 5 each. The engagement count is down from 167 in the previous morning report. Pokrovsk is down from 21. Kostiantynivka is up from 7. Oleksandrivka, Orikhiv, and the Dnipro axis recorded no assaults. The General Staff estimated 2,262 Russian personnel losses in the day and about 1,648,161 since 24 February 2022. Those are Ukrainian figures. The 7,111 is the battlefield drone count. It is not the long-range wave above. Ukrainian aircraft, missile units, and artillery struck 15 Russian targets in the day: eight clusters of personnel and equipment, six drone-control points, and one artillery position.

Ukraine claimed a strike the other way the same night. The Special Operations Forces said deep-strike drones hit the Yefremov Synthetic Rubber Plant in Tula oblast, that several drones reached the target, and that the strike was run with the Black Spark group inside Russia. The plant is described as Russia's only producer of high-molecular-weight polyisobutylene, used in solid rocket propellant and plastic explosives, including fuel for Iskander missiles. That is the Ukrainian military's account. Residents and the Astra channel reported a fire and damage at the town's combined heat and power plant, which supplies the factory, and said heating was cut. Tula governor Dmitry Milyaev said air defense shot down four drones, that windows broke in three houses, and that no infrastructure was damaged. His account and the Ukrainian account do not describe the same result. Kyiv Post said Moscow claimed 318 Ukrainian drones destroyed overnight across the country. That national claim and the governor's count of four over Tula are not the same figure, and Euronews-style verification of the 318 was not in the reports read here.

## Iran war

Two Italian technicians were killed in Kuwait on Thursday when debris from intercepted drones fell on a site Kuwait and Italy both blame on Iran. Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani told the Senate that several drones of Iranian origin hit Kuwaiti military facilities around 2 p.m. local time, including an air-defense detection station where two employees of Rheinmetall Italia were maintaining the antennas of a Skyguard system. Kuwait's Defense Ministry first said four people were hurt by falling debris at a "vital facility" in what it called an Iranian attack, one Kuwaiti, two Italians, and one Pakistani, and later confirmed the two Italians had died. Italian reports named them as Antonio Saccoccio, 65, and Emanuele Lo Russo, 36. Al Jazeera called them the first European civilians killed in this war. Iran's armed forces spokesman Abolfazl Shekarchi said no Iranian offensive or military operation had taken place in the region. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi called the incident "suspicious" and a "precooked recipe for war," denied any Iranian role in calls with Tajani and with Kuwait's foreign minister, and offered a joint investigation with Kuwait and Italy. Tajani later said the drone was made in Iran, and he noted that pro-Iranian militias operate in the region. That is his statement. It is not an Iranian admission. Kuwait's Foreign Ministry condemned the attack, cited Article 51 of the UN Charter and Security Council Resolution 2817, and reserved a right of self-defense. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said pressure on Iran has to increase. Those are the two accounts. They do not agree on who fired.

The Red Sea side of the same war produced a confirmed hit the same day. Saudi Arabia's Energy Ministry said a hostile aerial projectile struck Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery on Thursday, killed a Nepali worker, wounded several others, started a fire that was later put out, and forced a temporary halt to some operations. The refinery is built to process up to 400,000 barrels a day. The ministry did not name the attacker. The Saudi-led coalition did. Spokesman Turki al-Malki said the Iran-aligned Houthis fired the missile, and that the coalition destroyed the launch platform in Hodeidah that it says was used in the strike. He said attacks on civilians and civilian sites will draw a firm military response. This is a different event from Wednesday's Houthi claim on Ras Tanura, which Saudi authorities had not confirmed. Houthi officials, as carried by the Indian Express, said Saudi strikes then killed four people in Taiz province, one of them a child. Houthi media also reported strikes on Sanaa. AP did not verify that Sanaa report. The Jazan death is the Saudi ministry's account. The Yemen death toll is the Houthi account.

Iran's navy added a claim on Friday that no US office answered. Rear Admiral Shahram Irani said, in an account TASS took from Fars and that Anadolu and Al Jazeera also carried, that US forces had seized two Iranian tankers and that navy special forces boarded them "while the Americans were asleep" and took them back. He also said US forces collect fees to escort ships through the strait and then take no responsibility when those ships are hit. No hull, place, or date was given. No US confirmation or denial was located as of the GlobalSecurity cutoff at 05:00 ET Friday. CENTCOM's standing line is that recognized transit routes have been cleared of mines by US forces. The boarding story is an Iranian claim.

Oil moved with the week's strikes. GlobalSecurity, citing Pakistan Observer, put Brent at $96.61 a barrel at 04:50 GMT Friday, up 4 cents, and WTI at $85.78, up $2.10 from Wednesday's close. It said Brent climbed nearly $2 on Thursday to the highest settlement since 6 November, about 33 percent above the $72.48 settlement of 27 February. Reuters' Thursday midday print, already logged, was $94.96. The two clocks are not the same quote. ANZ analysts said Brent could pass $100 even without a further escalation. That is a bank view.

A navy is moving closer, and Tehran answered it. Reuters reported Friday that Araghchi told South Korea's foreign minister, Cho Hyun, on Thursday that a foreign military presence in the Gulf would raise tensions and "expand the scope of conflict," rather than bring peace. The Iranian embassy in Seoul posted that account on Friday. Cho told him Seoul does not intend to join any war or send troops to the Gulf. South Korea's foreign ministry said Cho described the mission as protection of Korean nationals and of the country's economic interests. Parliament is reviewing a motion to extend the Cheonghae anti-piracy unit, based in the Gulf of Aden since 2009, into waters south of Oman, outside the Gulf. President Lee Jae Myung has said the Strait of Hormuz and the waters next to it are excluded. The National Assembly defense committee has approved a revised text that bars the unit from other countries' wars. Extra assets discussed include a maritime patrol aircraft, an explosive-ordnance team, and unmanned mine-clearing gear. A plenary vote is expected in December. Seoul's text is a wider patrol area that stops short of the strait. Araghchi's text is a warning that foreign forces widen the war.

President Donald Trump, on a Thanksgiving call with US troops in the UAE, in Saudi Arabia, and aboard USS Portland, said "We have victory at hand" in Iran and that it will happen whenever he wants it to, and he played down Chinese and Russian support for Tehran. Gulf News carried those lines, as logged by GlobalSecurity. They are his claims. No US or Israeli strike on Iranian territory was acknowledged in the window that closed at 05:00 ET Friday. No new US military death was reported. The Pentagon toll stays at 23. Two US carrier strike groups, led by USS George H.W. Bush and USS Theodore Roosevelt, remain in the Central Command area. No new public position was published for either, or for USS George Washington. No source showed a missile stock moving from the Gulf to Europe or the Pacific, or the reverse. No Chinese or Russian arms delivery to Iran was announced Friday.
