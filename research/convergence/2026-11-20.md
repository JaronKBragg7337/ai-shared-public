## China and Taiwan

Taiwan's Defense Ministry recorded 8 PLA aircraft sorties, 7 navy ships, and 2 official ships from 6 a.m. Thursday to 6 a.m. Friday (UTC+8). It said 6 of the 8 sorties crossed the median line into the northern and southwestern ADIZ, and that 4 PRC balloons were detected in the same window. Taiwan News said all four balloons crossed the median line. Taiwan sent aircraft, ships, and coastal missiles. Taiwan News put the month's running total at 228 aircraft and 180 ships. The daily air count is up from 1 sortie in the previous window. The navy count around the island is unchanged at 7. The official-ship count is unchanged at 2. No source today said the rise was timed to the Ukraine strike, the objects that came down in Poland, or the Iran oil moves.

Mainland Affairs Council Minister Chiu Chui-cheng said Friday that Chinese state-media reports of what Trump told Xi on Taiwan were "not true." Xinhua's Thursday account of the Shenzhen bilateral says Xi told Trump the US side must keep doing the right thing on Taiwan to avoid major setbacks, and that Trump said the United States does not support "Taiwan independence." Chiu said Taipei had checked that account with the foreign ministry. He said CCP media routinely tell other parties' stories after important meetings, and that Washington should clarify the facts. Those are his statements. The White House still has not issued its own readout of the bilateral. The four-point declaration the three leaders issued Thursday does not mention Taiwan. Xinhua's sentences and Chiu's rejection do not match, and neither is a US transcript.

On chips, Reuters reported, citing two people familiar with the matter, that TSMC has told affected customers to halt orders at 16 nanometres and below unless they hold a US export licence. The same people said TSMC guidance generally limits those Chinese designers to 28-nanometre and 40-nanometre processes, and only if the chips do not use high-bandwidth memory or advanced packaging. The people said the foundry is also checking operating locations, parent companies, major shareholders, packaging suppliers, and shipping routes, including orders placed through overseas subsidiaries. Those are the two people's account of TSMC's instructions, not a TSMC press release. The restrictions follow US Commerce Department rules that took effect Thursday and closed a route that had let TSMC judge some Chinese designs as outside the controls without an individual licence. The Financial Times reported Thursday that TSMC and Samsung had suspended some shipments to Chinese customers after those rules. No Chinese commerce-ministry countermeasure dated Friday was found.

Taiwan's Taiex fell 2,393.60 points, or 6.23 percent, on Friday to 36,033.29. Focus Taiwan said that is the third-largest one-day point drop on record and took the week's loss to 7,641.83 points, or 17.5 percent. Capital Investment Management general manager Fan Chen-hung attributed the drop to US midterm results, the Nvidia leadership uncertainty, and the tighter US controls on AI-chip exports toward China. That is his attribution.

## Russia and Ukraine

Russia launched one of the largest missile and drone attacks of the war overnight, after Putin said in Shenzhen on Thursday that there would be no Black Sea or energy truce and that the response to the Crimean Bridge strikes would be far more painful. Reuters and Al Jazeera reported the barrage as that response. Ukraine's Air Force spokesman Yurii Ihnat said bridges and energy sites have been Russian targets for a long time and that "there can be no question of this being some kind of response." The wire framing and Ihnat's line do not match. Both are on the record. Neither is an independent finding that the strike was, or was not, ordered because of the bridge.

Ukraine's Air Force said the attack began at 18:00 on Thursday. It listed Iskander-M/S-400 ballistic missiles and Zircon anti-ship missiles from Rostov, Bryansk, and Kursk, without giving a count; 84 Kh-101, Kalibr, and Iskander-K cruise missiles from Vologda and the Black Sea; and 404 Shahed-type drones, 123 of them jet-powered, plus Gerbera and Parodiya decoys, from Oryol, Kursk, Millerovo, Primorsko-Akhtarsk, occupied Donetsk, and Hvardiiske and Chauda in occupied Crimea. As of 10:30 Friday it said 462 targets had been shot down or suppressed: 3 ballistic missiles, 79 cruise missiles, and 380 drones, 102 of them jet-powered. It recorded hits at 23 locations and debris at 10. Those are Ukrainian Air Force figures. Ihnat, speaking earlier, put the salvo at up to 80 cruise missiles, up to 20 ballistic missiles, and about 400 drones, about 120 of them jet-powered. President Zelensky said more than 400 drones and over 110 missiles, 27 of them ballistic. AeroTime quoted Polish General Ireneusz Nowak putting the launch at 26 Kalibr missiles, 72 Kh-101 missiles, and 503 drones, 202 of them jet-powered. The Ukrainian and Polish counts do not match.

Zelensky said the targets were civilian infrastructure that keeps people alive: energy sites, bridges across the Dnipro, railway, and substations and power lines that nuclear plants depend on and that tie Ukraine's grid to the EU. He said missiles arrived in waves about 20 to 30 minutes apart. He said eight people were dead and nearly 30 injured as of his morning account, and that Ukraine struck three Russian ballistic launchers overnight, destroying one, with damage to the other two still being checked. Those are his claims. Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said ballistic missiles damaged the Podilsko-Voskresenskyi, Darnytskyi, and Pivdennyi (Southern) bridges and told people to stay off the roads. By afternoon he put the Kyiv toll at 7 killed and 17 injured, 16 of them in hospital. Reuters's earlier nationwide count was 8 dead, of whom Prime Minister Serhii Koretskyi said 6 were in Kyiv, plus 2 at a railway station in Lviv. The city and national counts were still moving and do not collapse into one figure. First Deputy Energy Minister Artem Nekrasov said nuclear plants cut output and that outages hit Kyiv and 10 regions. Russia's Defense Ministry said the Kyiv road and rail bridges it struck are used to move weapons and equipment to Russian forces in Donbas, and that it also hit defense plants, including sites it said produce Flamingo cruise missiles and drones, plus an arms plant and a military-industry substation in Lviv. That is Moscow's account of the targets. The two governments agree bridges in Kyiv were hit. They do not agree what those bridges are for.

Poland said three objects from the attack entered its airspace and came down in the southeast. Defense Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz said one was a Kh-101 cruise missile, near Nowy Lipowiec, about 60 km from the Ukrainian border; a second was also a missile, near Rudnik nad Sanem; the third was most likely a jet-powered drone, near Molodycz. He said no one was hurt and no critical-infrastructure damage had been reported. A Polish Armed Forces spokesman said 11 objects threatened to enter Polish airspace and 3 did. dpa said Poland scrambled two F-16s and that two Swedish Gripens were also up, and that ground-based air defenses were activated. Prime Minister Donald Tusk said there was no doubt the objects were Russian and called it an intentional provocation. Kosiniak-Kamysz said the timing with Coalition of the Willing exercises in Poland, and with defense ministers and chiefs of staff in the country, was no accident. Those are Polish government claims about intent. Tusk said NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte would visit Warsaw on Saturday, and that Poland would ask NATO for more help on the eastern flank. Ihnat had said earlier that one missile may have crossed into Poland and that the report was being checked. Warsaw's later account is three objects, not one.

On the ground, Ukraine's General Staff morning report, carried by EMPR and in the General Staff's own briefing, said Russian forces fought 278 engagements over the past day, launched 3 missile strikes and 72 air strikes, dropped 236 guided bombs, used 5,120 kamikaze drones, and conducted 2,106 shellings. It estimated 2,150 Russian personnel losses. Those are Ukrainian figures. The 5,120 is the battlefield drone count. It is not the long-range wave above.

Ukraine's General Staff said that on Thursday its forces hit the Kaleykino oil pumping station near Almetyevsk in Tatarstan, a node on the Druzhba pipeline more than 1,000 km from the front, and damaged three 50,000-cubic-meter tanks, one of which caught fire. That is Kyiv's account, confirmed by the General Staff on Friday. The Kyiv Independent said it was not clear how far the station's operations were disrupted. Russia's Defense Ministry said it intercepted 310 Ukrainian drones overnight. Smolensk Governor Vasily Anokhin said a drone attack damaged an industrial facility he did not name. Those are Russian claims. The commander of Ukraine's Unmanned Systems Forces said 40 Russian oil and defense sites had been hit in 50 days. That is a Ukrainian command claim.

The talks track split in public on Friday. On Thursday in Berlin, Jared Kushner said he and Steve Witkoff had gotten the Russians comfortable with a security guarantee for Ukraine based on NATO's Article 5, and that Trump is open to an Article 5-like guarantee if it serves US interests. On Friday, Deputy Foreign Minister Sergei Ryabkov told Interfax that any formula for "quasi-membership" of NATO for Ukraine is unacceptable, and that security guarantees can be discussed only after Ukraine's exclusion from NATO is accepted as irrevocable. He also said a visit to Moscow by representatives of Britain, Germany, and France is being worked on. Kushner's account of what Moscow accepts and Ryabkov's account do not match. Zelensky said Friday that cooperation among Ukraine, the United States, and Europe on negotiations is getting closer, and that he wants Washington to call a first multilateral meeting with the Europeans soon. That is his claim.

## Iran war

No new Iranian strike on a US base, dated to Friday, was found. No new public position for USS George Washington, USS Theodore Roosevelt, or USS George H.W. Bush was published today. The last Navy statement, logged yesterday, still has the Japan-based Washington under 7th Fleet in the Asia-Pacific and leaves Roosevelt and George H.W. Bush as the two carriers on the Iran war. The IRGC's Thursday claim that two unnamed supertankers hit mines in Hormuz is still unverified. No independent identification of those ships was published today.

The new fact is oil moving under the blockade, not a confirmed breakout. United Against Nuclear Iran said that on Monday the sanctioned Suezmax Olym (IMO 9212759) transferred about 200,000 barrels of Iranian crude to Hao Fan (IMO 9258167) south of Kharg Island. UANI said Hao Fan is not on the US sanctions list and is spoofing its position near Shinas, Oman. UANI adviser Charlie Brown called it the first observed ship-to-ship transfer of Iranian crude since the blockade was reinstated in mid-July, and said the volume is too small for an economic export voyage. He called the timing against Wednesday's blockade notice a coincidence. UANI also said it has tracked no Iranian crude tanker that has left the Gulf of Oman and avoided US enforcement since 12 July, and that at least 55 tankers laden with Iranian oil are along the Iranian coast. About 12 small LPG tankers have passed the line since July. Those are UANI's tracking claims.

Separately, Al Jazeera reported that Reuters, citing TankerTrackers.com co-founder Samir Madani, said a tanker was loading Iranian crude by ship-to-ship transfer in the Gulf on Thursday. Madani did not name the ship. He said it is not on the Treasury sanctions list, and that if it gets out it could be the first successful seaborne crude export since the blockade was reimposed on 14 July. There is no Iranian confirmation. A loading is not a completed export. UANI's "none have cleared" line and Madani's "could be the first if it clears" line describe different stages. They are not a report that the blockade has been broken.

Euronews Persian reported Friday, citing satellite imagery and ship-tracking data, that more than 14 tankers at Kharg Island have been given crews and Iranian flags and that a convoy is expected to head for the Sea of Oman in the coming days. It said TankerTrackers' data confirmed Iran was resuming oil exports. It also said Israel's Channel 14 and Sabereen News reported dozens of Iranian tankers preparing to head toward China. The "dozens to China" line is those outlets' claim. Euronews also quoted Mohammad Enayati Najafabadi, whom it described as a journalist with ties to the IRGC, warning in his own post that an attack on the tankers would mean explosions at oil facilities in the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman, and Bahrain. He called that his personal analysis, attached to a Fars commentary. It is not an IRGC order.

CENTCOM's Wednesday advisory remains the rule in force. It says a ship that takes cargo at sea from a vessel loaded at an Iranian port or coastline may be treated as evading the blockade even if it never enters Iranian waters, and may be stopped, boarded, diverted, seized, or sent back to the Iranian port of loading. Ships that do not comply can face force. USNI News, writing Thursday, said the United States has two carrier strike groups in the region, Theodore Roosevelt and George H.W. Bush, and counted 16 attacks on shipping so far in November. US Energy Secretary Chris Wright said Thursday on Fox News that the United States has been moving more oil through Hormuz for several weeks and exporting more gasoline, diesel, and jet fuel. That is his claim. It does not say the strait is back to pre-war traffic.
