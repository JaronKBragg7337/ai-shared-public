# Convergence 2026-11-18

## China and Taiwan

Taiwan's Defense Ministry recorded 9 PLA aircraft sorties, 6 navy ships, and 2 official ships from 6 a.m. Tuesday to 6 a.m. Wednesday (UTC+8). Taiwan News, citing the ministry, said 8 of the 9 sorties crossed the median line into the central, southwestern, and eastern ADIZ. Taiwan sent aircraft, ships, and coastal missiles. Taiwan News said the month's running total is now 219 aircraft and 162 ships. The daily air count is up from 5 sorties in the previous window. The ship count around the island is unchanged. No new public total for the wider unannounced Pacific deployment was published today. No source today said the 9 sorties were timed to APEC or to the Iran war.

APEC's leaders' meeting opened Wednesday in Shenzhen. Xi Jinping told the CEO summit the Asia-Pacific needs more open trade and warned that unilateralism and protectionism are rampant. Putin, speaking after him, called Western powers "former hegemons" and said sanctions, tariffs, and export restrictions have "outlived their usefulness." He said anti-Russian sanctions imposed after the invasion of Ukraine had backfired and made Russia stronger. Those are their speeches. Reuters framed Xi's lines as following Monday's US rule on remote access to advanced chips. On Tuesday, Foreign Ministry spokesman Guo Jiakun called that rule typical economic coercion and long-arm jurisdiction and said China would take necessary measures. He did not, in that Xinhua account, name a countermeasure. TSMC said Tuesday it is reviewing the rule and will comply. Beijing's chip complaint and Xi's protectionism speech are on the record going into Thursday's meeting with Trump. Neither statement says the PLA flights were part of that argument.

Xi and Putin then met at the Qilin Shanzhuang guesthouse. China's Foreign Ministry readout does not mention Ukraine, Iran, or nuclear weapons. Xi said China-Russia relations have "stood rock-solid" and give "greater stability and certainty to a fluid and turbulent world," and that the two sides should use exchanges between governments, legislatures, militaries, and parties. Putin, in that Chinese account, said trade had reached a new high and that Russia is ready for "back-to-back" strategic coordination and deeper work on trade, energy, and education, plus a "multipolar" order. bne IntelliNews, citing TASS, said the talks ran more than two hours, continued in a smaller format with Sergei Lavrov and Yuri Ushakov, and were described as focused on economic cooperation. Putin said trade has held well above $200 billion and should hit that again in 2026. Those are the leaders' claims about their own meeting. The Chinese text and the Kremlin's later account do not describe the same subjects. That split is under Links.

Trump landed in Shenzhen on Wednesday afternoon. Reuters and CBC reported that Xi met him at the airport, the first time Xi had done that for any visiting leader since becoming president, and that the two shared a brief hug. They were due at the APEC banquet with Putin the same evening. The trilateral is still Thursday. Reuters said that meeting could take up Russia's war in Ukraine and the US war in Iran, plus trade, AI, and nuclear weapons. That is Reuters's list of what is potentially on the table, drawn from what Washington has said. Beijing's public line for the three-way meeting is still an exchange on world peace and strategic stability.

## Russia and Ukraine

Ukraine's General Staff morning report, carried by DSnews, said Russian forces fought 165 engagements on 17 November, launched 2 missile strikes and 87 airstrikes, dropped 220 guided bombs, used 6,990 kamikaze drones, and conducted 2,418 shelling attacks. A same-morning account carried by OGO said 18 of the assaults were on the Pokrovsk axis. Ukrainian aircraft and artillery struck one Russian troop concentration. Those are Ukrainian figures. The 6,990 is the General Staff's battlefield drone count. It is not the long-range wave below.

Ukraine's Air Force said Russia launched 141 long-range drones overnight on 17-18 November, Shahed, Gerbera, and Parodiya types, 73 of them jet-powered, from Oryol, Millerovo, and from Chauda and Hvardiiske in occupied Crimea. By 8 a.m. it said 126 had been destroyed or jammed, 59 of them jet-powered, with drones still airborne and hits at 7 places. The Kyiv Independent, compiling local authorities for that past day, put the civilian toll at 5 killed and 15 injured. Governor Vadym Filashkin said 4 of the dead were in Donetsk Oblast. Governor Oleh Syniehubov said 1 person was killed and 5 injured in Kharkiv Oblast. Energy sites were hit in Kyiv, Mykolaiv, and Rivne oblasts. Those are Ukrainian local accounts of that window. Later on Wednesday, separate strikes were still being counted. Al Jazeera and AFP reported at least 4 killed and 13 wounded in Odesa, which Zelensky called a brutal and deliberate attack, including on first responders. The Kyiv Independent reported a Kharkiv Oblast market strike that injured at least 11, including a child, and an afternoon drone attack in Kyiv that injured 3, with debris hitting a 28-story building in Pechersk. A Kyiv Oblast police account said 4 people, including a 12-year-old girl, were injured in Vyshhorod district. These later figures are not added to the morning total. They are further Ukrainian reports from the same day.

The road gap on the Crimean Bridge still stood while Putin was in Shenzhen. Ukrainska Pravda said close-up footage from occupied Crimea showed the road section destroyed and the railway still operating. The Kyiv Independent said train traffic had resumed. That is the status of Tuesday's strike, not a new attack. Ukraine's Defense Ministry tied Wednesday's refinery strikes to that bridge hit and called both a response to Russian strikes. That is Kyiv's framing.

Ukraine's General Staff said that on 17 November and overnight into 18 November it struck the Ilsky and Afipsky refineries in Krasnodar Krai. It said storage tanks were hit at Ilsky and, on preliminary information, a processing unit at Afipsky. It put their combined capacity at about 13 million tonnes a year, more than 4 percent of Russian refining, and called them short fuel routes to Russian forces. Those are Ukrainian military claims. Krasnodar's operational headquarters said fires at two enterprises were caused by falling drone debris. Russia's Defense Ministry said it intercepted 184 Ukrainian fixed-wing drones over Belgorod, Rostov, Krasnodar, occupied Crimea, and the Sea of Azov. Those are Russian accounts. They do not confirm the General Staff's description of what was hit. The General Staff also said Ukrainian forces struck a road bridge over the Siverskyi Donets near occupied Stanytsia Luhanska, used to move ammunition and fuel, and a repair base near Khomutovka in Kursk. It did not say whether the bridge was still usable. It separately confirmed that a 10 November strike on the 123rd Aircraft Repair Plant damaged three Russian military transport aircraft, one Il-76 seriously. That aircraft claim is about an earlier strike, confirmed today.

At Tuesday's Ukraine Defense Contact Group in Brussels, Pentagon policy chief Elbridge Colby said deep strikes inside Russia "have not been effective in driving an end to the war" and do not blunt Russian advances, and that strikes on civilian sites such as the retailer Wildberries had probably hardened Russian resolve. German Defense Minister Boris Pistorius called that criticism "quite a stupid thing" and said the strikes in question were on strategic military and military-used targets. UK Defense Secretary Wes Streeting said it is not for allies to tell Ukraine how to fight, and that deep strikes have degraded Russia's war machine. Ukraine's defense minister said Kyiv will answer Russian strikes and has the right to defend itself. Colby's assessment, and the German and British rejections, are claims. CNBC reported the split as sitting ahead of Thursday's Trump-Putin-Xi meeting. The published aid list from that Brussels meeting includes Belgium restating a plan to deliver 10 F-16s by the end of 2026, and France two Mirage 2000-5F jets. Zelensky asked for 40 more F-16s, saying jets already in Ukraine spend too much time in maintenance. Those are pledges and a request, not aircraft that arrived today.

German Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul said Wednesday that Europe has to be in talks on Ukraine's future, or a settlement will not last. He also said any conversation among the three leaders is a good one. A US official told Bloomberg, in an account carried by UNN, that Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner are expected to meet Ukrainian and European officials in Berlin on Thursday on postwar security guarantees. That is the same day as the Shenzhen trilateral. The Berlin date is that official's statement, as relayed.

## Iran war

Iran widened its strikes to Kuwait before dawn Wednesday, after a US strike on Qeshm Island on Tuesday. Iran's military, in accounts carried by UPI and state media, said combat and kamikaze drones targeted US Patriot systems and drone and missile launch platforms at Ali Al Salem Air Base. It gave no evidence of damage. Kuwait's army said its air defenses were countering hostile drones and that explosions residents heard were interceptions. It did not name the attacker, name the base, or report damage. Kuwait called the launch into its airspace a flagrant violation of sovereignty. Bahrain's defense force said it intercepted a number of Iranian air attacks on Wednesday, called them part of a continuing campaign, and later issued an all-clear. Tuesday's Iranian claim of a drone strike on a US radar at Sheikh Isa, and Bahrain's claim that the drones were intercepted with no US injury or damage, were already on the record. Wednesday is a further round, and the first reported Iranian attack on Kuwait in about two months.

The US strike those Wednesday attacks followed is still an official's account, not a CENTCOM communique. The Associated Press and CBS, citing a US official, said a US fighter jet struck an IRGC missile launch site on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday with precision munitions, in response to Iranian attacks on US personnel and assets in Bahrain. A local Iranian official, Ahmad Nafisi, said that strike caused no damage to residential or commercial areas or to infrastructure. Iranian state media said the US hit Qeshm and did not cause serious damage. CENTCOM, asked earlier, told Reuters it had no updates. The US account and the Iranian account agree a strike was reported. They do not agree on the result, and CENTCOM has not published its own statement of the strike.

Two ship casualties were reported from Tuesday's traffic in the strait. ADNOC Logistics and Services said Wednesday that one of its vessels was attacked in the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday, that 11 crew were injured, and that one crew member was still missing. The company did not name the ship or the weapon. SeaTrade, citing the security firm Vanguard, identified it as the Liberian-flagged product tanker Navig8 Messi, about 13 nautical miles north of Khasab. That identification is the firm's, not the company's. Separately, Oman said it rescued 24 crew from the Panama-flagged LPG tanker Tara Gas and that the search for one missing person continued, with firefighting still under way. Reuters's maritime sources said Tara Gas was abandoned after a projectile hit. It is not established that Tara Gas and the ADNOC ship are the same vessel. Iran's Tuesday claim that an LNG carrier and oil tankers caught fire after striking mines was already on the record and was not independently verified. ADNOC did not say a mine caused its casualty.

CENTCOM posted, late Tuesday UTC, imagery dated 17 November of USS Theodore Roosevelt sailing with USS George Washington in the Arabian Sea. That is the command's own picture of the carrier whose arrival US officials described on Sunday and which yesterday's note still had as unconfirmed by CENTCOM in public. The post shows two carriers. It does not show USS George H.W. Bush, and it does not say how long the overlap lasts. CBS, citing the US military, said Monday's confirmation was that three carrier strike groups are in the region and that the overlap is expected to be temporary. An INSS map cited by CBS places most of the US warships in the Arabian Sea, east of Hormuz, rather than inside the Persian Gulf, as of Wednesday. That map is the think tank's, not a CENTCOM plot. No source today said a carrier left Europe or the Pacific, or that missile stocks moved from one theater to another. The new fact is the published picture of two carriers already reported in this war, together, outside the strait, on the day the fighting in Kuwait and Bahrain widened.

## Links today

The concrete link is diplomatic, and the two sides do not describe it the same way.

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov told RIA Novosti that Putin and Xi discussed an end to the war in Ukraine during Wednesday's Shenzhen meeting, and that Putin briefed Xi on his talks in Moscow with Witkoff and Kushner. China's Foreign Ministry readout of the same meeting does not mention Ukraine, Iran, or the envoys. It speaks of military and party exchanges, energy and trade, and "back-to-back" coordination, which is Putin's phrase in the Chinese text. Peskov's account and Beijing's account are both claims. They agree the meeting happened. They do not agree, in public, on whether the Ukraine war was in the room. No Chinese or Russian statement today said that conversation was tied to the 9 PLA sorties, the Kuban refinery strikes, or the drones fired at Kuwait.

Thursday's three-way meeting is still the place Washington has said both wars can be discussed. Reuters said the sideline session with Trump could cover Ukraine and the Iran war. Beijing has not adopted that agenda in its own words. Peskov said Putin and Trump would see each other at a formal reception in Shenzhen. That is the banquet, not confirmation that a separate bilateral has been scheduled. German Foreign Minister Wadephul said Europe must be in any talks on Ukraine's future. A US official's statement, carried from Bloomberg by UNN, puts Witkoff and Kushner in Berlin on Thursday, the same day as the trilateral and without Ukraine in the Shenzhen room. That is a calendar fact about two tracks. It is not evidence of a deal.

CNBC reported the Colby-Pistorius-Streeting split on Ukrainian deep strikes as coming ahead of that same Thursday meeting. The split is real and sourced: the Pentagon policy chief said the deep-strike campaign is not ending the war, and Germany and Britain rejected that. Ukraine struck two more refineries overnight anyway. No source said Colby's remarks were delivered at China's request, or that the refinery strikes were ordered for the summit. Putin's CEO-summit line that sanctions and export restrictions have "outlived their usefulness" names the Ukraine sanctions and, in the words he used, export restrictions. Guo had already called the US chip rule coercion. Reuters set Xi's protectionism speech next to that chip rule. Neither leader, in the texts used here, said the chip rule, the Russia sanctions, and the Iran war were one package.

The Gulf escalation sits on the same day as Trump's arrival. Iran's Kuwait and Bahrain claims, the US official's account of the Qeshm strike, the ADNOC and Tara Gas casualties, and CENTCOM's photo of Roosevelt with Washington are all dated to this window. No source tied those attacks to the PLA count, to the Russian aircraft flight already logged on Tuesday, or to the Kerch and Kuban strikes. No source said the carriers were moved because China or Russia had moved. No source today showed a transfer of missile stocks among the Gulf, Europe, and the Pacific.

No source today showed a PLA move, a Russian battlefield move, and an Iranian military move ordered as one action.
