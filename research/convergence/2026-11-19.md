# Convergence 2026-11-19

## China and Taiwan

Taiwan's Defense Ministry recorded 1 PLA aircraft sortie, 7 navy ships, and 2 official ships from 6 a.m. Wednesday to 6 a.m. Thursday (UTC+8). The ministry said that one sortie crossed the median line into the southwestern ADIZ. Taiwan sent aircraft, ships, and coastal missiles. Taiwan News said the month's running total is now 220 aircraft and 171 ships. The daily air count is down from 9 sorties in the previous window. The navy count around the island is up from 6 to 7. The official-ship count is unchanged. No source today said the drop in sorties was timed to the Shenzhen meetings, or to the Iran war.

The same morning, Taiwan's Defense Ministry said two Russian Tu-95 sorties entered the ADIZ from the south, flew through airspace east of Taiwan, and exited northeast of Keelung. It said it monitored the track and responded, and it released a photo it said was taken from a Taiwanese F-16. This is the second Russian flight Taipei has logged this week. Tuesday's aircraft were not typed in the ministry statement. Thursday's were. Japan's Joint Staff said that from morning into the afternoon, two Russian Tu-95 bombers came from the Pacific, passed between Yonaguni Island and Taiwan, crossed the Tsushima Strait, and flew toward the continent. It said two Russian Tu-142 patrol aircraft came from the Sea of Japan, crossed the Tsushima Strait, joined those bombers over the East China Sea, and then returned toward the continent through the strait. Japan scrambled fighters. Reuters placed the Taiwan detection on the day Putin was in Shenzhen for the trilateral with Xi and Trump. No Russian Defense Ministry statement on Thursday's flight was found. No source said Moscow ordered the track for the meeting, or for the single PLA sortie.

In Shenzhen, Xi and Trump held a bilateral before the three-way meeting. Xinhua's account says Xi told Trump that on Taiwan the US side should do the right thing and avoid major disruptions to China-US relations, and that Trump said the United States does not support "Taiwan independence." Those lines are Xinhua's. Focus Taiwan, filing after a Mainland Affairs Council briefing, said the White House had not issued its own readout and that Taipei had not responded. At that briefing, MAC deputy head Liang Wen-chieh said that from what Taipei knew, Taiwan was not the focus, that the talks were expected to center on a Ukraine ceasefire and Iran's nuclear program, and that any mention of Taiwan would be peripheral. He later said he did not think Taiwan would be discussed. Xinhua's account of the bilateral and Liang's pre-result assessment do not match. The four-point declaration the three leaders then issued does not mention Taiwan.

After the leaders' meeting, Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi declined to say how Japan would respond to a Taiwan military contingency. She said Japan's position is unchanged from the 1972 Japan-China Joint Communique. She would not answer a hypothetical about military intervention. That is her statement. It does not retract, and does not repeat, her November 2025 remark that a Chinese blockade of Taiwan followed by US intervention and further force could be a survival-threatening situation for Japan.

## Russia and Ukraine

Ukraine's General Staff morning figures, carried by EMPR, said Russian forces fought 211 engagements over the past day, launched 71 air strikes, dropped 239 guided bombs, used 4,582 kamikaze drones, and conducted 2,027 shelling attacks. Ukrinform headlined the same engagement count and said the heaviest Russian attacks were on the Pokrovsk and Kostiantynivka axes. Ukrinform also carried a General Staff estimate of 1,970 Russian personnel losses for the day. Those are Ukrainian figures. The 4,582 is the General Staff's battlefield drone count. It is not the long-range wave below.

Ukraine's Air Force said that from 18:00 on 18 November, Russia launched Oniks anti-ship missiles from Crimea and 98 attack drones, 61 of them jet-powered, plus Gerbera and Parodiya decoys, from Oryol, Kursk, Shatalovo, Millerovo, occupied Donetsk, and Hvardiiske in occupied Crimea. By 08:00 it said 69 drones had been shot down or suppressed, 48 of them jet-powered, with drones still airborne and hits at 18 locations. Those are Ukrainian Air Force figures. Later on Thursday, Kyiv city authorities told Ukrinform that a man of 26 was killed in the Podilskyi district and that four people were injured, three of them hospitalized, as of 16:20. A later Suspilne account, citing Mayor Vitali Klitschko around 17:05, put the injured at five and reported damage at the Dutch embassy, St. Volodymyr's Cathedral, and the grounds of the Foreign Ministry. Those are Ukrainian local accounts. The toll was still being updated.

Ukraine struck the Crimean Bridge again overnight, the third strike in a week, while Putin was in Shenzhen. The General Staff said Ukrainian Navy Neptune coastal missiles hit the bridge, halted traffic, and damaged the railway track. It called the bridge the main land route for fuel, ammunition, and equipment to Russian forces in the south. That is Kyiv's account. Russia's Transport Ministry said rail traffic was suspended after an overnight drone attack and that no one was hurt. Moscow-installed governor Sergei Aksyonov said trains were due to resume from around 16:00. The two sides agree the bridge was attacked and rail traffic stopped. They do not agree on the weapon. Kyiv Post said the extent of the new damage had not been independently verified.

Speaking in Shenzhen after the trilateral, Putin said he had discussed a halt to strikes at sea and on energy sites with Russia's Security Council and had hoped some agreement could be reached. He then said that after the strikes on the Crimean Bridge there would be no such agreements, and that Russia's response would be far more painful than what Kyiv did to the bridge. He said the bridge is only a civilian facility and is not used for military equipment or petroleum products. He said the road section suffered serious damage and would be restored. The refusal of a moratorium, the threat, and the civilian-only description are his claims. Reuters reported them as delivered after the talks with Trump and Xi. Meduza's account of the same press conference says he also claimed attempts to pressure Russia on territory are pointless, and that Russia has invited the Europeans to Moscow to clarify their position before deciding how talks continue. Those are also his claims.

In Berlin the same day, the Coalition of the Willing met with US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner. AFP reported more than 25 leaders there, with the EU and NATO, on winter aid and on ending the war. Kushner wrote that he and Witkoff updated Chancellor Friedrich Merz on Trump's discussions in China and on efforts toward a peace in Ukraine, and that the Berlin work was to align positions and prepare the next round. That is Kushner's account of a briefing. It does not quote what Trump said in the room in Shenzhen.

## Iran war

The new military fact in this theater is a carrier leaving, not a new strike. US Pacific Fleet spokesperson Cmdr. Katie Koenig said the Japan-based USS George Washington is now under 7th Fleet control and conducting routine operations in the Asia-Pacific. Stars and Stripes reported that Washington left the Middle East earlier this week after USS Theodore Roosevelt arrived, and that Roosevelt and USS George H.W. Bush are the two carriers still there, with at least 15 other Navy warships in the Arabian Sea. The three-carrier overlap logged yesterday is over. Koenig did not say the ship is going home to Yokosuka. Bloomberg, citing two US officials it did not name, said the strike group may be sailing for that home port. That destination is those officials' account, not the fleet spokesperson's.

No new Iranian attack on a US base, dated to Thursday, was found beyond Wednesday's already logged strikes on Kuwait and Bahrain and the US official's account of the Tuesday strike on an IRGC site on Qeshm. Stars and Stripes, writing Thursday, still treated those attacks as the live backdrop. It also reported that the New York Times, citing US and Israeli officials it did not name, said Trump is weighing a joint four- to seven-day air campaign with Israel against Iran's missile and drone stocks and energy sites. The White House had not answered questions about that report. Separately, Trump has said it was highly unlikely he would order new major strikes and that the war is militarily effectively over. The unnamed-officials account and Trump's own public line do not match. Neither is a strike order.

The three leaders' declaration, released by Xinhua and carried by the Kremlin, says they plan to increase the production and supply of refined petroleum products and reaffirmed stable energy flows. The text does not name Iran, the Strait of Hormuz, or the blockade. ThePrint read that paragraph against fuel prices since the Iran war and the disruption of Hormuz. That is ThePrint's connection. It is not a sentence in the declaration.

Xinhua and China Daily published the China-Russia-US Declaration toward Peace after the Shenzhen meeting. The four points are UN reform, more refined petroleum and stable energy flows, respect for state consent and official immunity with a line that the International Criminal Court and similar bodies must not abuse their jurisdiction, and a pledge that a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought, plus a dialogue on nuclear risks and proliferation. The document does not name Ukraine, Iran, Taiwan, or Hormuz. Reuters noted that the ICC has issued an arrest warrant for Putin and five other Russian officials. The declaration does not mention that warrant. dpa noted the text is silent on the Ukraine war, which Trump had said was on his agenda, and does not explicitly mention the war with Iran. Trump left without a public comment, Reuters reported. Beijing and Moscow released the text. A White House text of the same declaration was not found.
