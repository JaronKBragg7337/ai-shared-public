# Convergence 2026-11-24

## China and Taiwan

Taiwan's Defense Ministry recorded 3 PLA aircraft sorties and 9 navy ships from 6 a.m. Monday to 6 a.m. Tuesday (UTC+8). It said all 3 sorties crossed the median line into the southwestern and eastern ADIZ. The morning text does not list an official ship and does not list balloons. Taiwan sent aircraft, ships, and coastal missiles. Taiwan News put the month's running total, on that morning report, at 263 aircraft and 220 ships. The air count is down from 6 sorties and 5 crossings in the previous window. The navy count around the island is down from 12 to 9. No source said the drop was timed to the NATO aircraft decision, to the strike Zelensky confirmed in the Gulf of Finland, or to the Hormuz claims.

Local elections are Saturday, 28 November, four days out. Voters choose mayors and county chiefs, and a nuclear-policy referendum is on the same ballot. Reuters reported Tuesday that the vote is not directly about China, and that Beijing has treated the Democratic Progressive Party's Taipei candidate, Puma Shen, as the issue. China calls Shen a traitor and says he is playing up the China threat to win votes. Chinese police have issued a wanted notice for him on a separatism charge. In October, Taipei prosecutors indicted four people for putting monitoring devices on a scooter outside his home and issued arrest warrants for two people from Hong Kong. The same month, CCTV said Shen was seeking votes by talking about outside interference. China last month added sanctions on his father's business in China. Mainland Affairs Council head Chiu Chui-cheng told Reuters that the Chinese Communist Party ties its Taiwan policy to Taiwan's elections, so interference before a vote is inevitable, and that the interference will deepen resentment. Those are Taipei's account and Beijing's account. They agree that Beijing is speaking about this candidate. They do not agree on what that speech is for.

DPP legislative caucus leaders Fan Yun and Chen Pei-yu said Tuesday, citing the National Security Bureau and the Mainland Affairs Council, that Chinese agencies worked with travel and airline companies to offer subsidized or free flights home to Taiwanese business people in China and their families, so they would vote for specified candidates. Chen also named airport perks and coordinated sea and air seats through the Kinmen and Matsu links. Fan cited illegal campaign money, the Chou Hsiao-yun case already logged yesterday, disinformation, hacks of election systems, selective farm purchases, and threats against candidates. Those are DPP claims drawn from the security agencies. They are not a court finding published today, and no Beijing statement answering the flight allegation was found.

No new US chip-export rule was published today. No source tied the sortie count to Hormuz or to Ukraine.

## Russia and Ukraine

Ukraine's Air Force said that from 18:00 on Monday, Russia launched 96 attack drones, 64 of them jet-powered, of Shahed, Gerbera, and Parodya types, from Oryol, Bryansk, Primorsko-Akhtarsk, occupied Donetsk, and Hvardiiske in occupied Crimea. As of 08:00 Tuesday it said 73 had been shot down or jammed. Ukrainska Pravda, citing the same command, said 45 of those 73 were jet-powered, that several drones were still airborne, and that the attack was ongoing. Hits were recorded at 13 locations and debris at 2. Those are Ukrainian Air Force figures. The wave is smaller than Monday morning's, which the Air Force had put at 153 drones.

The strike with a published governor count was in the city of Zaporizhzhia. Regional governor Mykhailo Semikin said a Russian attack killed two people and wounded 40, including four children, the youngest under one year old. A private home was destroyed and a five-story apartment building was partly destroyed. Other homes, cars, and transport infrastructure were damaged. Reuters carried that count. Kyiv Independent's compilation of local authorities put the day's toll across Ukraine at 9 dead, with the injured figure published first at 81 and then at 77, and put the Zaporizhzhia injury count higher than the 40 Semikin gave Reuters. Those published totals do not match. In Donetsk, regional head Vadym Filashkin said Russian forces shelled the region 28 times over the past day, killing two people and injuring five in the Kramatorsk district, including strikes by FPV drones and an Italmas drone. Ukrinform said a drone hit Korosten railway station in Zhytomyr region, killing one person, with the injury count later at three, and that a municipal hospital building in Odesa was damaged in an afternoon strike. No Russian Defense Ministry statement on the Zaporizhzhia building was found. Interfax, quoting that ministry, said Russian forces hit drone-control sites, Starlink terminals, and other military assets in Sumy region and killed servicemen. That is a Russian claim about Sumy. It is not an account of the apartment building.

On the ground, Ukraine's General Staff said Russian forces fought 161 engagements on Monday, launched 73 air strikes, dropped 250 guided bombs, used 7,475 kamikaze drones, and conducted 1,865 shellings, 17 of them with multiple-launch rockets. The heaviest axis was Kostiantynivka, with 18 attacks. Pokrovsk had 15. Huliaipole had 13. Kramatorsk, Kupiansk, Orikhiv, and the Dnipro axis recorded no assaults. The engagement count is up from 149 in the previous morning report. An evening snapshot on Monday had stood at 123. The morning figure is the closed count. The General Staff estimated 1,848 Russian personnel losses in the day and about 1,641,489 since 24 February 2022, and added one ship or boat, to 36. Those are Ukrainian figures. The 7,475 is the battlefield drone count. It is not the long-range wave above. The added hull is a line in the loss table. The General Staff did not name it.

Kyiv confirmed the Baltic strike it had not claimed. In a Bloomberg interview given late Monday and published Tuesday, President Volodymyr Zelensky said Ukraine hit the Russian cargo ship Sparta II with a drone in the Gulf of Finland on Saturday, 21 November. He said the ship was carrying weapons and was a legitimate target, and that any Russian-flagged vessel carrying weapons or sanctioned cargo is a legitimate target in Russian territorial waters or in neutral waters. He said the strike was in Russian territorial waters, the ship then drifted, and "surely there are questions." He said some European partners are worried when something happens in the Baltic Sea, and that he told ministers and the military to give partners more detail so there are no surprises. Bloomberg said it could not verify the weapons cargo. The weapons line is his claim. Reuters said the ship, owned by Oboronlogistika, is now anchored in St. Petersburg after a Russian vessel towed it out of Finnish waters on Sunday. Finland's military had said the strike was likely Ukraine's and had placed it in international waters west of Gogland. Zelensky's account and that earlier Finnish account still do not describe the same water. They now agree that Ukraine struck the ship.

Norway answered that confirmation the same day. Defence Minister Tore Sandvik, receiving Ukrainian Defence Minister Yevhenii Khmara in Oslo, said all such attacks would stop if Russia stopped the war, and that Ukraine must act inside the rules of international law. He said Oslo has a dialogue with Kyiv on "a need for stability in our near areas." The two ministers signed an agreement to widen cooperation on an anti-drone system. Those are his statements. They are not a finding that the Sparta II strike broke the law.

Italy answered Sunday's Libya strike. Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani told Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha that he was concerned about military operations in eastern Libya for which Ukraine has claimed responsibility, and that Italy is working to keep the war with Russia from spreading into the Mediterranean. Italy said Libya's sovereignty, stability, and unity have to be respected, and that it still supports Ukraine's defense and a just peace. That is the Italian foreign ministry's account of the call. It does not mention Hormuz or Taiwan.

NATO's answer to Friday's missiles is now a deployment order. The North Atlantic Council met Monday in Brussels. Deputy Defence Minister Paweł Zalewski said afterward that an AWACS early-warning aircraft will be at Poland's disposal, called it an immediate allied response, and said Russia has moved up the escalation ladder with NATO. That last line is his characterization. NATO spokesperson Allison Hart said the Friday violations were a dangerous incident, that Russia bears full responsibility, that Supreme Allied Commander Europe General Alexus Grynkewich has authorized the forward deployment of a NATO AWACS, and that he is examining further assets while he reviews the deterrence posture in Poland and elsewhere. She said all allies stand in full solidarity with Poland. She did not name a base or a length of stay. dpa, citing the alliance's military headquarters in Mons, said the approval concerns moving an AWACS to Lithuania. That is dpa's account of where the aircraft goes. It is not in Hart's public post. Kyiv Post said other Polish requests, including more air defense, were put off to a meeting of NATO foreign ministers next week.

The same night, Poland's airspace was crossed again. The Operational Command of the Polish Armed Forces said a Russian unmanned aircraft briefly entered Polish airspace during the attack on western Ukraine. Radars tracked it. Authorization was given to shoot it down. After it turned east, the command held fire because of the risk to civilians and the risk of damage on the ground. No one was hurt. The official statement does not give the type, the distance, or the time inside Poland. RMF, citing unnamed sources, said it was a jet-powered drone that flew about 20 kilometres in and stayed about nine minutes, and that fighters were not scrambled because of the weather. PAP, citing sources close to the defence ministry, described a jet-powered reconnaissance drone with cameras that entered near Dorohusk. Those press accounts are not the military's published statement, and they do not match each other on distance and time. On Tuesday morning Zalewski told RMF that it is hard to imagine this was an accident and that Warsaw assumes it was deliberate. That is his judgment. Russia has not commented on the Friday missiles or on this drone.

Ukrainian Navy spokesperson Dmytro Pletenchuk said the closure of the Kerch Strait has removed the remaining route by which the Black Sea Fleet could leave that sea, and that the fleet's activity is now limited to shifting berths and local defense. He said debris blocks the Kerch-Yenikale channel. That is his assessment of damage already claimed from the 16-19 November strikes on the bridge. It is not a new strike dated Tuesday.

Britain added launchers on the same day. Defence Secretary Wes Streeting visited Kharkiv, and the British Ministry of Defence said he is the first UK defence secretary to do so. He announced further RapidRanger launchers, which the ministry said Ukraine has already used against propeller-driven and jet-powered Shahed drones. The number of launchers was not given. That is a British delivery announcement. It does not mention Poland's drone or the Gulf.

## Iran war

Iran's army chief, Major General Amir Hatami, told army commanders at the Command and Staff University on Tuesday that the Strait of Hormuz will stay closed until Iran's seven conditions are met, and that control of it will get tighter. Press TV quoted him: the strait "will not be reopened. It will become increasingly closed, with stronger management exercised over it." Al Jazeera, citing Iranian television, quoted him saying Iran must tighten control, manage the strait more strongly, and make conditions more difficult for the enemy. He also said that if adversaries start another war, the response will be more severe and without a geographic limit. Those are his statements. They name no date for a new strike, and they do not mention Taiwan, Poland, or Ukraine.

The mine claims published around that speech do not agree on a number, and none names a ship. Iran's army navy said two tankers that had turned off their identification systems entered a prohibited zone east of the strait early Tuesday, hit mines, caught fire, and cannot move. Tasnim carried that statement, and Press TV and Al Jazeera reported it. The IRGC navy, in statements carried by Mehr and IRNA, said three "violating" tankers suffered explosions and fires after hitting mines, one off the southern coast and two east of the strait, while under what it called extensive US air escort, and that the escort failed. Iran International, as logged by GlobalSecurity, carried a further IRGC version: one tanker exploded early on 24 November, and two accompanying tankers turned back after a radio warning. Mehr later on Tuesday carried an IRGC report of a fourth tanker exploding east of the strait. No hull, flag, or casualty was named. No UKMTO confirmation of these Tuesday claims was found. No CENTCOM statement on them was found. Reuters said traders largely dismissed the claims. The Iranian statements are claims. They are not a verified count of ships on fire.

What UKMTO did confirm on Tuesday was older. It issued a time-late report that a bulk carrier, anchored at Dibba about 23 nautical miles northeast of Khor Fakkan in the UAE, was hit on the port side by an unknown projectile. A fire broke out. No casualties were reported. Mehr carried the UKMTO text. Accounts differ on the day of the hit: Iran International, via GlobalSecurity, places it on 22 November, and a Reuters brief places it on Monday. The agency did not name the attacker.

Al Jazeera, using Marine Traffic, said 226 vessels entered or left the Gulf from 1 to 7 November and 125 did so from 17 to 23 November, a drop of more than 40 percent. Its AIS count for the past 24 hours was 28 ships, 12 in and 16 out, carrying an estimated 4.18 million barrels, and it said ships with transponders off are missing from that count. It said Sunday was the month's low, with one vessel out and two in. Brent was quoted at $88.71 a barrel at 03:10 GMT on Tuesday, down 66 cents, per Reuters via Arab News as carried by GlobalSecurity. Al Jazeera quoted Brent at $88.35. The strait is not back to the pre-war 125 to 140 ships a day. Iranian state media and officials denied on 23 and 24 November that Iran was preparing a tanker convoy to run the blockade. No breakout sailing was confirmed.

The US Treasury on Monday sanctioned more than two dozen Iranian exchange houses, front companies, and people, including firms in the UAE, Turkey, and Hong Kong. Secretary Scott Bessent said Iran's economy is underwater and its leaders are panicking, and that the shadow-banking system is the regime's last financial lifeline. FinCEN, in a separate analysis the same day, identified about $14.7 billion of activity from January 2025 to August 2026 that it said was potentially linked to Iranian shadow banking. The largest flagged route in that analysis was from mainland China-based banks to UAE-based banks, about $2.6 billion. That is a US description of past flagged flows. It is not a finding that Beijing ordered them, and it is not a flow dated Tuesday.

IRGC navy chief Ali Ozmaei told ISNA, as carried by Al Jazeera, that the strait is not a place to test unauthorized routes. Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei's Monday line, that Tehran will use all available means against the blockade, is the political claim Hatami's order sits on. It was already logged yesterday. In a letter to the UN Security Council on Monday, Iran accused Bahrain of facilitating US attacks by opening its territory, airspace, and bases, and demanded full reparations. That is Iran's claim. Bahrain's claim remains that Saturday's fire was at a civilian plant. No CENTCOM confirmation of a destroyed Patriot radar was found. No new attack on Bahrain or Kuwait with a casualty count was located for this window.

The Wall Street Journal reported, per Reuters as carried by GlobalSecurity, that the United States is investigating whether Iran was behind cyberattacks on as many as 50 energy tankers. That is an investigation. It is not a finding.

Bush strike groups in the Central Command area. No source showed a missile stock moving from the Gulf to Europe or the Pacific, or the reverse.
