## China and Taiwan

Taiwan's Defense Ministry recorded 2 PLA aircraft sorties, 6 navy ships, and 1 official ship from 6 a.m. Friday to 6 a.m. Saturday (UTC+8). It said both sorties entered the southwestern and eastern ADIZ, and that 4 PRC balloons were detected in the same window. Taiwan News said all four balloons crossed the median line. Taiwan sent aircraft, ships, and coastal missiles. Taiwan News put the month's running total, on that morning report, at 230 aircraft and 187 ships. The daily air count is down from 8 sorties in the previous window. The navy count around the island is down from 7 to 6. The official-ship count is down from 2 to 1. No source said the overnight drop was timed to the Ukraine strikes, the objects that came down in Poland, or the Hormuz attacks.

The same morning, after that 6 a.m. count, the ministry posted a separate alert. It said that from 09:38 it detected 21 sorties of J-10, J-16, Su-30, H-6K, KJ-500, and Y-20U aircraft, and that 15 of the 21 crossed the median line into the central and southwestern ADIZ to conduct joint air-sea training. The armed forces said they monitored and responded. That post is timed 04:44 UTC, which is 12:44 in Taipei, so it sits outside the 6 a.m. daily window. The H-6K is a bomber and the Y-20U is a tanker. The ministry did not say how many ships were with the aircraft, and it did not call the flight a named exercise. The 21 are not inside the morning total of 230. No source tied the daytime wave to Poland, to the Kryvyi Rih strike, or to the tankers off Khasab.

China's Defense Ministry said representatives of the Chinese and US militaries held the second working-group meeting and the annual plenary of the Military Maritime Consultative Agreement in Shanghai from 18 to 20 November. It said the sides reviewed the 2026 rules for air and maritime encounters, discussed measures on military maritime security, and agreed the agenda for the 2027 working-group meetings. It framed the meetings as delivering on the consensus of the two heads of state. That is Beijing's account of a completed staff channel. It is not a new operation around Taiwan, and it does not mention Hormuz, Ukraine, or the daytime sorties.

On the market, the Financial Supervisory Commission said Saturday it will ban short sales below the previous close, for both margin shorts and borrowed shares, and will raise the minimum short-sale margin from 130 percent to 150 percent. Securities and futures firms hedging for their own business are exempt. The commission will also stop accepting applications for new put warrants, and it asked lenders to suspend share lending and to recall shares already lent or let those loans expire. The rules take effect Monday. The commission said this is to keep trading orderly after recent losses, and Focus Taiwan said it is the first blanket restriction of this kind since 2015. It tightens Thursday's rule, which had barred shorting a stock only after a fall of 3.5 percent or more. Premier Cho Jung-tai met the economic and financial agencies Saturday morning and told them to produce measures to stabilize the market. The Taiex had closed Friday down 2,393.60 points, or 6.23 percent, at 36,033.29. The commission attributed the drop to volatility in AI-related stocks. Bloomberg reported that the NT$500 billion National Financial Stabilization Fund said it has entered the market. Focus Taiwan's account is narrower: the fund's committee has been authorized to intervene since 6 November. Those are the two descriptions. Neither source tied the curb to the PLA sorties, the Iran war, or the Ukraine strikes.

## Russia and Ukraine

Ukraine's Air Force said that from 18:00 on Friday, Russia launched 3 ballistic missiles, typed as Iskander-M, KN-23, or S-400, from Bryansk and occupied Crimea, and 148 attack drones, 87 of them jet-powered Shaheds, plus Gerbera drones and Parodiya decoys, from Oryol, Bryansk, Primorsko-Akhtarsk, Millerovo, occupied Donetsk, and Hvardiiske in occupied Crimea. As of 08:00 Saturday it said 117 drones had been shot down or suppressed, 67 of them jet-powered, with hits at 19 locations and debris at 9. Ukrinform said about 10 drones were still airborne and the attack was ongoing. The Air Force did not give a count of ballistic missiles shot down. Those are Ukrainian Air Force figures. The wave is much smaller than Friday's, which the Air Force had put at 84 cruise missiles and 404 drones plus ballistic and Zircon missiles.

The toll that rose overnight was from Friday evening in Kryvyi Rih, not from a second mass salvo. Local officials said two ballistic missiles hit the center of the city, near a restaurant, and that drones then struck the same place while rescuers were working. Oleksandr Vilkul, head of the city defense council, called that a follow-up strike on rescuers, medics, and utility workers. A boy of 7 died in hospital of head injuries. A 20-year-old ambulance paramedic also died. The injured count did not settle. The Kyiv Independent, updating Saturday morning, reported 52 injured. bne IntelliNews quoted Vilkul at 08:42 putting the injured at 58, six of them children, with 28 still in hospital. Associated Press, citing regional head Oleksandr Hanzha, reported 60 wounded, six of them children. The dead are the same two people across those accounts. The injured totals do not match because they were still moving. Vilkul said the boy had been on the restaurant playground. No Russian Defense Ministry statement on Kryvyi Rih was found. The double-tap description is the Ukrainian officials' account.

Kyiv was hit again before dawn. The city military administration said two private houses caught fire in Podil after an enemy attack. Kyiv Post reported a blast at about 5:47 a.m. local time, shortly after the Air Force warned of a ballistic missile from the north. Mayor Vitali Klitschko said two people were injured and one was hospitalized. City police named the injured as men of 63 and 67, and said debris damaged private houses in Podil, Holosiivskyi, and Solomianskyi. Ukrinform said falling debris started the Podil fire and that debris fell in an open area in Solomianskyi. Kyivvodokanal reported lower pressure or outages in Holosiivskyi, Sviatoshynskyi, Solomianskyi, Shevchenkivskyi, and Podil. No death in Saturday's Kyiv strike was reported. Friday's Kyiv toll, 7 killed and 17 injured, stands as logged yesterday. Saturday's strike is a separate, smaller attack.

On the ground, Ukraine's General Staff morning report, carried by Ukrinform, said Russian forces fought 203 engagements on Friday, launched 3 missile strikes and 78 air strikes, dropped 242 guided bombs, used 6,224 kamikaze drones, and conducted 1,976 shellings. The heaviest axis was Pokrovsk, where Ukrainian units stopped 26 attacks. A General Staff summary carried in Ukrainian put Russian personnel losses for the day at 1,730. Those are Ukrainian figures. The 6,224 is the battlefield drone count. It is not the long-range wave above. The engagement count is down from 278 in the previous morning report.

The General Staff said that on Friday and overnight its forces hit an ammunition depot near Pervomaiske in occupied Crimea, command posts near Pryshyb in Zaporizhzhia region and near Ukrainsk in Donetsk region, and a road bridge over the Siverskyi Donets near Luhansk. It said the bridge is used to move fuel, ammunition, and equipment, and that the damage was still being assessed. That is Kyiv's account. No Russian confirmation of those four strikes was found.

NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte met Prime Minister Donald Tusk in Warsaw on Saturday, the visit Tusk had said was coming after Friday's impacts. Reuters, citing the Polish Press Agency, quoted Rutte: "I can guarantee that NATO will defend every inch of Polish territory." He said General Alexus Grynkewich, NATO's commander in Europe, would identify any gaps in defense and deterrence and make adjustments if needed. PAP also reported that the North Atlantic Council will meet in Brussels on Monday to discuss the violation. TVP World quoted Rutte telling TVP Info that all allies stand in full solidarity with Poland, that the response by Polish F-16s with NATO support was how the alliance should work, and that Polish authorities were still assessing what happened. He called the incident dangerous and unacceptable. Asked whether the timing with the Coalition of the Willing drills was a coincidence, he would not say. Those are his statements in Warsaw. They do not add a new object on Polish ground. Friday's identification stands: a Kh-101 near Nowy Lipowiec, a second suspected missile near Rudnik nad Sanem, and a suspected jet-powered drone near Molodycz. No one was hurt in Poland. No Russian comment on the impacts was found.

## Iran war

UKMTO logged two attacks on outbound tankers in the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday morning, both near Khasab on Oman's Musandam Peninsula. Warning 215-26, at 05:00 UTC, said a tanker was hit on the stern by an unknown projectile about 11 nautical miles north of Khasab. Warning 216-26, at 05:50 UTC, said a tanker was hit in the engine room by an unknown projectile about 17 nautical miles east of Khasab. UKMTO reported no casualties in either case and said the environmental impact was unconfirmed. It did not name the ships, and it did not say the two reports were two hulls. Reuters said it was not immediately clear whether they were the same vessel. These sit on top of warning 214-26, at 15:00 UTC Friday, when UKMTO said a tanker outbound was hit on the port bow about 8 nautical miles north of Khasab and a fire was reported, with no casualties. dpa counted three projectile attacks since Friday afternoon, striking an engine room, a stern, and a port bow, and said it was unclear whether three tankers were involved. UKMTO did not name an attacker.

Iran's navy said Saturday, in a statement carried by state media, that an oil tanker hit a naval mine in a restricted area east of the strait and caught fire. Reuters said it could not verify that account and could not establish whether it referred to either UKMTO incident. State media also said the military carried out its most "intense and varied" operations yet in the strait overnight to stop vessels, and that the aim was to foil US escorts. That is Tehran's description of intent. It is not an admission that Iran fired the projectiles UKMTO recorded, and it is not an independent count of ships hit.

The same day, Iranian state television aired an underground Revolutionary Guard site where the Guard unveiled a launcher it said can fire two liquid-fueled missiles. Associated Press, carried by the Las Vegas Sun, said the launcher appears to be a step past an earlier dual launcher that fired two solid-fuel missiles, and that liquid fuel lets operators adjust speed and trajectory. The site's location was not shown. Brig. Gen. Seyyed Majid Mousavi, commander of the Guard's Aerospace Force, said Iran's capabilities have expanded significantly and that "surprises" and highly effective assets have been built into the combat structure. Those are his claims. The broadcast was aimed at US statements that the missile program has been destroyed. Washington's destruction claim and Mousavi's expansion claim do not match. Neither is a count of missiles left.

Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said Saturday that Tehran was still in contact with Qatari mediators who visited Iran on Friday, to see whether another chance can be given to diplomacy. That is his statement. It is not a report that talks have resumed, and it does not mention the tankers.

No new public position for USS George Washington, USS Theodore Roosevelt, or USS George H.W. Bush was published today. The last Navy statement, logged on 19 November, still has the Japan-based Washington under 7th Fleet in the Asia-Pacific and leaves Roosevelt and George H.W. Bush as the two carriers on the Iran war. No source today showed a transfer of missile stocks among the Gulf, Europe, and the Pacific.
