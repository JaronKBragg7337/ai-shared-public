## China and Taiwan

Taiwan's Defense Ministry recorded 2 PLA aircraft sorties and 6 navy ships up to 6 a.m. Thursday (UTC+8). It said 1 of the 2 sorties entered the southwestern ADIZ. The English post does not list an official ship and does not list balloons. Taiwan News said Taiwan sent aircraft, ships, and coastal missiles. Taiwan News put the month's running total, on that morning report, at 266 aircraft and 235 ships. The air count is up from 1 sortie in the previous window. The navy count around the island is down from 9 to 6. That previous window's single sortie had entered the eastern ADIZ. No source said the lower ship count was timed to the grain-ship claim near Odesa, to the mine claims in Hormuz, or to Saturday's vote.

The launch that Taipei had flagged on Tuesday flew Thursday evening. China's state broadcaster CGTN said a Long March-2D lifted the Yaogan-49 01 group from the Xichang Satellite Launch Center in Sichuan at 5:38 p.m. Beijing time. It called the flight the 681st of the Long March series. It described the satellite as a remote-sensing craft for scientific experiments, land surveys, crop estimates, and disaster work. That is China's description of the payload. Taiwan's Central News Agency, United Daily News, and Liberty Times, each citing the Defense Ministry, put the launch at 5:38 p.m. and said the path crossed the southwestern ADIZ toward the western Pacific, above the atmosphere, with no harm to Taiwan. The ministry's English-language post put the time at 7:50 p.m. The two clocks do not match. The ministry said the armed forces tracked the flight and stayed ready. No source said the launch was ordered for the election, for Ukraine, or for Hormuz. Tuesday's notice had cited a US Federal Aviation Administration advisory.

Local elections are Saturday, 28 November, two days out. Voters choose mayors, magistrates, councils, and other local offices, with a nuclear-policy referendum on the same ballot. Premier Cho Jung-tai told the weekly Cabinet meeting that the Ministry of Digital Affairs, the Central Election Commission, and local election commissions should stay in contact against cyberattacks through the vote, and that Justice and Interior should protect candidates and keep order. Cabinet spokesperson Michelle Lee gave that account. Later Thursday, Mainland Affairs Council deputy head Liang Wen-chieh said National Security Bureau data put recent cyberattacks from China at about 2.58 million a day. That is his citation of the bureau. It is not a count of one intrusion dated Thursday, and no Beijing statement answering it was found.

The same briefing was about Shanghai. Xinhua reported Tuesday that Politburo member and Shanghai party chief Chen Jining is under investigation for suspected serious violations of discipline and law. Liang called that a major event and said the council is watching it because the Taipei-Shanghai Twin-City Forum is still expected in Taipei by the end of the year. He declined to say whether a tighter hold by Xi Jinping would change the situation in the strait. He said the government's line on lawful cross-strait exchanges, on equal footing, is unchanged. Those are his statements. They are not a finding that the investigation was timed to the election.

In Taipei, the two leading mayoral candidates pulled in-person rallies after a fight over road closures. DPP candidate Puma Shen said Wednesday night he would tear down the stage for Friday's rally on Ren'ai Road because the lane closures were choking traffic. Mayor Chiang Wan-an of the Kuomintang offered City Hall Road for Friday and moved his own election-night rally online. His campaign manager confirmed that cancellation Thursday morning. No source tied the rally fight to a PLA order.

On trade, the US Trade Representative said Wednesday that a Section 301 inquiry found manufacturing-overcapacity policies in 14 economies, including Taiwan, burden or restrict US commerce. The proposed rate for Taiwan is up to 15 percent, counting duties already in place, or up to 25 percent if Washington judges further talks and Taiwan's commitments insufficient. Written comments run through 5 January 2027. Hearings start 25 January. Vice Premier Cheng Li-chiun said Thursday the 15 percent figure matches the investment memorandum and the Agreement on Reciprocal Trade signed in February, and that Taipei expects that rate to hold without an add-on. She said semiconductor-specific terms under a separate Section 232 inquiry are still not announced, and that Taiwan has been told it has preferential quota treatment, with duty-free imports inside the quotas and 15 percent or less above them. Those are the US proposal and her account of the talks. They are not a new chip-export ban published Thursday. No source tied the tariff paper to the sortie count or to the rocket.

## Russia and Ukraine

Ukraine's Air Force said that from 18:00 on Wednesday, Russia launched Iskander-M, Zircon, and S-400 ballistic missiles from Bryansk and Kursk oblasts, and 113 attack drones, 76 of them jet-powered, of Shahed, Gerbera, and Parodya types. Launch sites named were Oryol, Bryansk, Primorsko-Akhtarsk, Millerovo, occupied Donetsk, and Hvardiiske in occupied Crimea. As of 08:00 Thursday the Air Force said 87 drones had been shot down or jammed, 52 of them jet-powered, with hits at 20 locations and debris at 6. It did not give a missile count or say how many missiles were intercepted. Several drones were still airborne and the attack was ongoing. Those are Ukrainian Air Force figures. The drone wave is larger than Tuesday morning's, which the Air Force had put at 96.

Prime Minister Serhii Koretskyi said the night's strikes hit water facilities, heating networks, shopping centres, and homes in Kyiv and seven oblasts, and that at least 10 people were injured. He said water utilities in Dnipropetrovsk oblast were hit several times, which he called a deliberate attempt to cut water, that shopping centres caught fire in Kryvyi Rih and Pavlohrad, and that heating networks and an industrial water line were hit in Zaporizhzhia. He said that at sea near Odesa, Russia attacked two foreign civilian ships carrying more than 100,000 tonnes of grain. He did not name the ships, the flags, the damage, or the crews. Reuters said Russia says it does not attack civilian targets. That is the standing Russian line the agency recorded. No Russian Defense Ministry statement on these two hulls was found. Reuters also said Kyiv has said grain shipments from the Odesa hub have all but stopped since late July under attacks on terminals and merchant ships. Koretskyi's ship claim is his statement. It is not a hull identified by a second government.

The city counts were still moving. In Kharkiv, regional head Oleh Syniehubov said Thursday morning that strikes over the past day killed four people in the region, two of them in the city and two on a road near Dovzhyk, and injured others in the city and in Zlatopil. Later he and Mayor Ihor Terekhov described Thursday's own strikes: a drone hit a 16-story building in the Novobavarskyi district and killed a married couple, and a jet-powered drone hit a university building in the Kyivskyi district, killing an 86-year-old woman. The injury list on the university strike then rose. Education Minister Andrii Butenko said a drone hit the main building of the National Aerospace University, that fires broke out on two floors, and that four employees were injured. The morning regional toll and the later city tolls do not match, because they cover different strikes and the day was still open. In Bylbasivka, in Donetsk oblast, the State Emergency Service said a drone hit a post office on Wednesday evening, killed a woman, injured three, and set a fire of about 350 square metres. Firefighting stopped because of the threat of more strikes. In Zaporizhzhia, regional police chief Artem Kysko said four investigators from the serious-crimes unit were killed in a Russian strike, three at the scene and their supervisor after more than a day in hospital, and that the force has lost ten officers in November, not on the front line. His account does not fix the strike to a single hour on Thursday. No Russian Defense Ministry statement on the Kharkiv buildings or the post office was found.

On the ground, Ukraine's General Staff said Russian forces fought 167 engagements on Wednesday, launched 1 missile strike and 69 air strikes, dropped 228 guided bombs, used 6,016 kamikaze drones, and conducted 1,876 shellings. The heaviest axis was Pokrovsk, with 21 attacks. Lyman had 11. Huliaipole had 8. Kostiantynivka had 7. Kupiansk and the Dnipro axis recorded no assaults. The engagement count is up from 161 in the 24 November morning report. The General Staff estimated 2,319 Russian personnel losses in the day and about 1,645,899 since 24 February 2022. Ukrinform's headline on the same table listed four aircraft. Those are Ukrainian figures. The 6,016 is the battlefield drone count. It is not the long-range wave above. The four aircraft are a line in the loss table. The General Staff text carried by Ukrinform did not name them.

Ukraine claimed strikes the other way the same night. The General Staff said the navy used a coastal launcher to fire Neptune missiles at the Marinovka airbase in Volgograd oblast, where Su-34s that drop guided bombs are based, and that the damage is still being assessed. It said a strike on the Subkhankulovo oil-pipeline station in Bashkortostan, more than 1,000 kilometres from Ukraine, hit a storage tank and started a fire. The station belongs to Transneft. In Stavropol krai, Governor Vladimir Vladimirov said a large drone attack on the industrial zone of Nevinnomyssk was partly beaten off, that debris damaged manufacturing and energy sites, and that two people were injured. Monitoring channels and the Ukrainian account identify the Nevinnomyssky Azot plant, a EuroChem site that supplies chemicals used in explosives, and the power station beside it. The governor's account is debris from downed drones. The Ukrainian account is a hit on a plant that feeds explosives. They do not describe the same result. Russia's Defense Ministry, as carried by Euronews, said 343 Ukrainian drones were destroyed overnight across 15 regions, the Azov and Black seas, and occupied Crimea. Euronews said it could not verify that claim.

Rystad Energy, as reported by Bloomberg and carried Thursday by Ukrainska Pravda, put Russian refinery runs as of 23 November at 2.6 million to 2.7 million barrels a day, almost half the 30 October level, and below the lows earlier this year. The estimate does not include strikes on two Samara refineries on 25 November. Bloomberg's count was at least 18 Russian refineries attacked since late October. Putin had said on 24 November that Kyiv was still hitting refineries despite a request from President Donald Trump to stop, and that retaliatory measures were inevitable. That was his statement. It is not a published target list for Thursday night.

President Volodymyr Zelensky said Thursday, after a coordination meeting, that Russia is finishing preparations for another massive strike. He said intelligence points again at energy, logistics, and the facilities cities need to function. He said the grid is particularly hard in Dnipro, the Dniprovskyi district, and Pavlohrad, and he named repair work in Odesa, Kharkiv, Konotop, and Sumy. He said he wants a Foreign Ministry report on interceptor supplies for Patriot and NASAMS. He said Commander-in-Chief Mykhailo Drapatyi briefed him on Kostiantynivka and that tasks had been set for the units there. Those are his statements. They are not a launch detected Thursday afternoon.

The bodies moved. Ukraine's Coordination Headquarters said 250 bodies that the Russian side says are Ukrainian service members were brought back on Thursday, and that identity work is still ahead. State Duma deputy Shamsail Saraliyev, carried by the Moscow Times, said Russia received 22 bodies in the same exchange. Ukrainian officials had not, in that Moscow Times account, confirmed the Russian number. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said security services are still finishing lists for a larger prisoner exchange through intermediaries, and that Moscow hopes the work ends soon. He gave no date. Belarus's BelTA, via dpa, reported the same 250 and 22. The exchange is confirmed on the Ukrainian side for the 250. The 22 is the Russian account.

Ukraine's Defence Intelligence said Kim Jong Un has paused military support for Russia. The claim, published Thursday, is a ban on Russian military transports in North Korean airspace, a stop to KN-23 ballistic-missile deliveries, and a stop to the next batch of artillery ammunition. It said Pyongyang is also holding a Russian request to raise the North Korean contingent on Russian territory from 8,500 to 50,000, while an 11th Corps element remains in the second echelon in Kursk oblast and a missile unit in Voronezh oblast has been used for strikes on Ukraine since midsummer. It put North Korean casualties in Kursk at 7,058, of which 2,251 are dead. It tied the friction to an October remark by Deputy Foreign Minister Andrei Rudenko about easing ties between Seoul and Pyongyang, which it said North Korean Foreign Minister Choe Son Hui rejected. That is a Ukrainian intelligence claim. No confirmation from Moscow or Pyongyang was found.

Allied ministers answered last week's sea strikes, not a new hull. At the EU summit, Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni said the war must not spread to the Mediterranean, that Italy will say so to Ukraine, and that she will not tie Italian aid to the Benghazi strike. In Berlin, Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul told his Romanian counterpart that Ukraine's right of self-defence is undisputed and that Germany's support stands, and that he would strongly caution against letting the conflict spread geographically. He said he has no doubt Ukraine is hitting military objectives, including in the Baltic, and that Berlin is in close talks to keep the fighting from threatening shipping and the environment there. Those are their statements. They name the Libya strike and the Sparta II. They do not name Hormuz or Taiwan. French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot's line, already public, is that the Libya strike was self-defence.

A Thursday report looked back at the Sparta II weekend, not at a new Baltic strike. Yle and Dagens Nyheter, as carried by European Pravda, said GPS interference on 21-23 November was heavy around Stockholm, Gotland, Tallinn, and Helsinki. Swedish Transport Agency aviation head Andreas Holmgren called the scale exceptional and the first of its kind over several days. The agency did not name a state. Danish Navy chief Johannes Riber said suspicion most likely falls on Russia. That is his judgment. The interference lines up in time with the 21 November strike on the Sparta II, which Zelensky has confirmed. Swedish authorities have not confirmed a Russian source.

No new Russian object on Polish ground was reported Thursday. The NATO AWACS authorization from Monday still stands. No new public base or arrival date was published.

## Iran war

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said Thursday that two tankers exploded and caught fire after hitting sea mines north of Oman's Khasab, on what the Guards called an unauthorized route through the Strait of Hormuz. One, they said, was a supertanker carrying more than 2 million barrels of crude. Tasnim carried the statement. Reuters reported it. No hull, flag, or casualty was named. A separate Guards statement on Wednesday, also carried by Reuters, had already said two tankers on an unauthorized route hit mines, exploded, and caught fire. The two statements may describe one pair of ships or two. They do not say. No UK Maritime Trade Operations notice found Thursday used the word mine.

What UKMTO did report was projectiles. Warning 223-26 said local authorities reported that an outbound tanker was struck by an unknown projectile about 18 nautical miles east of Khasab while transiting the strait. The crew were accounted for. No environmental impact was reported. The incident time on that warning is 13:00 UTC on 25 November. Reuters said Thursday that UKMTO also had a report of an explosion from an unknown projectile near a tanker about 50 nautical miles west of Dubai. A second write-up of the warnings places that Dubai report on 25 November as well, with the crew safe and no damage recorded. The Guards' mine story and the UKMTO projectile stories do not name the same ships. No CENTCOM confirmation of either account was found.

IRGC spokesman Brigadier General Hossein Mohebbi said Thursday that Iran has the firepower to keep the strait closed, to pressure the US economy, and to defend Iranian territory. He said that firepower is precise and destructive, that vessels involved in fuel smuggling suffer incidents daily, and that Iranian forces have struck fighter jets, refueling planes, and drones escorting what he called failed fuel runs, shooting some down and forcing others to land intact. He said Iran is ahead of the United States in missiles and other weapons, that Washington is short of strategic weapons, and that Iran is not short and will keep raising missile range, accuracy, and destructive power. IRNA carried those lines. They are his claims. They do not mention Taiwan, Poland, or Ukraine, and they name no new strike hour. IRGC Navy commander Rear Admiral Ali Ozmaei, as carried by WANA, said the eastern and western sections of the strait are under the IRGC navy's full control. That is his claim.

The Red Sea side of the same war moved on Wednesday and was still the oil story on Thursday. Yemen's Houthis struck at Saudi ports. Coalition spokesman Turki al-Maliki said a projectile landed at Jazan port, with minor damage and no civilian injuries, and that a drone was intercepted near Jeddah Islamic Port. He said air defences also intercepted ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh. Earlier Wednesday the coalition said it intercepted four drones fired at Ras Tanura, on the Gulf coast, and that some debris fell. The Houthis said they targeted the Ras Tanura refinery, which has a capacity of about 550,000 barrels a day, in answer to strikes on Hodeidah. Saudi Arabia has not confirmed that the refinery was hit on Wednesday. A Houthi statement Wednesday had threatened Jeddah and the other Saudi Red Sea ports if Hodeidah were attacked, and the coalition said it then struck targets at Hodeidah, Saada, and Amran. Those are the two sides' accounts. Reuters described the Houthis as Iran-aligned. That is the agency's label. It is not a document of an order from Tehran dated Thursday.

Brent rose with those claims. Reuters put Brent at $94.96 a barrel at 12:11 GMT Thursday, up 35 cents. There was no WTI settlement because of the US Thanksgiving holiday. Reuters tied the move to the Houthi claim on Ras Tanura and to the Guards' mine claim. Traders had a recovery story to set against that: the same piece said Gulf oil flows through Hormuz had been climbing back toward pre-war levels before the latest attacks. A separate Bloomberg report, carried by CNBC-TV18, said the UAE has restricted tankers carrying crude, products, and liquefied gas from waters near some ADNOC export hubs, including Ruwais and Das Island, unless the ship is calling there. That is a reported traffic rule. It is not a count of ships turned away on Thursday.

No new public position for USS George Washington, USS Theodore Roosevelt, or USS George H.W. Bush was published. The last placement still has the Japan-based Washington back with 7th Fleet in the Pacific, and Roosevelt and George H.W. Bush as the two carriers on the Iran war. No source showed a missile stock moving from the Gulf to Europe or the Pacific, or the reverse. No Chinese or Russian military assistance to Iran was announced Thursday.
