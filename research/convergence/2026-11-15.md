# Convergence 2026-11-15

## China and Taiwan

Taiwan's Defense Ministry recorded 1 PLA aircraft sortie, 8 navy ships, and 3 official ships from 6 a.m. Saturday to 6 a.m. Sunday (UTC+8). It said that 1 sortie entered the eastern ADIZ. Taiwan sent aircraft, ships, and coastal missiles. That daily air count is down from 10 sorties in the previous window and 30 on 13 November. The ship count around the island is unchanged. No new public total for the wider unannounced Pacific deployment was published today. On Friday, two Taiwan security officials told Reuters about 70 vessels were still active after an early-November peak an unnamed official put above 100. Taipei calls those drills "silent intimidation" ahead of APEC in Shenzhen. Beijing has called the carrier training routine and not aimed at any country.

China's trade promotion council said Sunday that Xi Jinping and leaders from 11 other APEC economies, including Russia, will attend the APEC CEO Summit in Shenzhen on 17-18 November. The list named Australia, Canada, Chile, Hong Kong, Indonesia, South Korea, Malaysia, Mexico, Russia, Thailand, and Vietnam. It did not name the United States on the leaders' list. US companies are the largest foreign business delegation, about 20 percent of participating firms from outside China. The leaders' meeting itself remains 18-19 November.

Reuters reported Sunday that Trump meets Xi this week with the US still dependent on Chinese rare earths for semiconductors, weapons, and AI, despite federal spending on domestic mines. A senior US official said Friday that China is not delivering rare earths at the pace Washington wants and that tighter rules on advanced American AI chips are under consideration. Those are US statements about policy still under consideration, not a new control that has been issued.

## Russia and Ukraine

Ukraine's Air Force said that from 6 p.m. on 14 November Russia launched 132 attack drones, 78 of them jet-powered Shaheds, plus Gerbera drones and Parody decoys, from Bryansk, Oryol, Kursk, Millerovo, Primorsko-Akhtarsk, occupied Donetsk, and Hvardiiske in Crimea. As of 8 a.m. on 15 November it said 106 had been shot down or jammed, with hits at 20 locations. President Zelensky said Russian forces attacked at least nine regions overnight, targeting energy, civilian, and railway infrastructure, and that at least 16 people were injured. In a later statement he put the past day's civilian toll at least 4 killed and 69 injured. He said Russia had launched more than 2,300 drones, 1,590 guided bombs, and 15 missiles against Ukraine over the previous week.

Ukraine's General Staff reported 171 engagements on 14 November, 17 of them on the Pokrovsk sector, plus 1 missile strike, 104 airstrikes, and 311 guided bombs. It estimated 2,230 Russian personnel losses in that day. Those are Ukrainian figures.

Ukraine's General Staff said it struck the Nizhnekamskneftekhim petrochemical complex and an oil refinery in Nizhnekamsk, Tatarstan, and the Monolit aviation plant in Bryansk. Nizhnekamsk mayor Radmir Belyaev reported damage to an industrial facility and a residential building. Tatarstan health authorities said 3 people were injured there. Rostov officials said at least 1 person was killed and 5 wounded in a separate overnight drone attack. Russia's Defense Ministry claimed it destroyed 533 Ukrainian drones overnight over Russian regions and occupied Crimea. That figure is a Russian claim.

Zelensky said Sunday that a speed cap on Starshield along the front and over occupied territory, which Ukraine puts at 161 km/h, is letting Shahed strikes through onto substations, and that protection of lives and the energy grid before winter depends on lifting it. He told the foreign ministry, defense ministry, and presidential office to press the United States and SpaceX. SpaceX has called the disruption technical. Zelensky said Saturday he does not believe that. No US confirmation of a policy cap was published today.

The prisoner-exchange track from Abu Dhabi and Dublin still has no public date or final number. The diplomatic deadlock on territory is unchanged.

## Iran war

The IRGC Navy said Sunday that two oil tankers on what Tehran calls an unauthorised southern route through the Strait of Hormuz hit naval mines, exploded, and caught fire, and that one of them, a supertanker carrying more than 2 million barrels of crude, sank in the southern Gulf. The statement, carried by IRNA and state television, did not name either ship or its flag. These are Iranian claims. There is no independent identification of a sunk supertanker. IRNA also said five tankers had hit mines in the waterway over the previous 48 hours. The same pattern of unnamed "mine" fires was claimed on 12, 13, and 14 November. The US military says it has cleared the main routes of mines. ISW-CTP wrote that it has not observed indicators that Iran has successfully re-mined the southern route and treats the unnamed mine updates as information operations. That is an analyst judgment.

Separately, UK Maritime Trade Operations published Warning 205-26 on Sunday. It said Indian shipping authorities reported an attack on a tanker transiting eastbound through the Arabian Gulf before entering the Strait of Hormuz. UKMTO said the crew were accounted for and safe, and that damage and environmental impact were unknown. It did not name the ship. It is not established that this is the same incident the IRGC described.

The IRGC said Sunday it launched six drones at a US air-defence radar at Bahrain's Riffa (Al-Riffa) helicopter base, and called it the second phase of its response after Friday's claim. Bahrain's military said its air defences intercepted several Iranian aerial attacks before they reached their targets. Bahrain's Interior Ministry issued two shelter alerts, the first such warnings since September. Bahrain's foreign ministry held Iran "fully responsible" and said no pretext legitimises violations of its sovereignty. Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Jordan, and Egypt condemned the strike. CENTCOM had not commented on Sunday's claim at the time of these reports. On Friday CENTCOM said it shot down two IRGC drones aimed at Bahrain with no injuries and no damage. The two sides' accounts of Friday still do not describe the same result.

The Saudi-led coalition said Sunday it intercepted two Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh and three drones in the Eastern Province. Coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki al-Maliki announced the intercepts. Later the coalition said two more drones were intercepted in the Makkah region. Yemen's Southern Armed Forces said they were withdrawing from Dhubab Airport and the Bab el-Mandeb area west of Taiz to secure Aden. Reuters called that a setback for the six-week Saudi-backed coastal campaign and said it leaves Iran-aligned Houthis with greater leverage over the Red Sea mouth while Iran disrupts Hormuz on the other side of the peninsula.

CENTCOM posted Saturday that more than 1.7 billion barrels of crude have been shipped through the Strait of Hormuz by Gulf partners with US protection. No new public position for USS Theodore Roosevelt was published today. It was last reported through the Strait of Malacca around 8 November with its tracker off. A US official said the group would not enter Central Command's area before mid-November. That window is open, and no arrival has been confirmed.

## Week

Diplomatic convergence grew this week. On 12 November Washington described a leaders' meeting that could touch Iran, denuclearization, and a trilateral with Russia; Beijing's readout of the Rubio-Wang call omitted all three. By 14-15 November the White House and the Kremlin both named 19 November in Shenzhen, Washington put Iran and nuclear cuts on the agenda and Ukraine off it, and Moscow still talks about Ukraine in a Putin-Trump track at the same summit. Beijing listed Russia at the CEO Summit today and has still not confirmed the trilateral date or an Iran agenda.

Military activity did not move together. PLA sorties around Taiwan ran 7 on 12 November, jumped to 30 on 13 November during the "silent intimidation" reporting, then eased to 10 and to 1 as APEC host week opened. The wider unannounced Pacific deployment, which Taipei said peaked above 100 vessels, was last publicly put at about 70 still active on Friday. Ukraine stayed in a high-tempo drone and energy war through the Abu Dhabi-Moscow-Dublin talks, with a promised large prisoner swap still undated. Iran stayed in a maritime and Gulf-base fight: repeated unnamed Hormuz "mine" claims, a UKMTO-logged tanker attack, a second Bahrain drone claim in three days, Houthi missiles at Riyadh, and a government pullback from Bab el-Mandeb. USS Theodore Roosevelt is still on the Pacific-to-Gulf leg, unconfirmed in CENTCOM.

No source this week showed a PLA, Russian, or Iranian military move timed to the others. The named cross-theater facts remain the Shenzhen calendar, the carrier shift, and China's 13 November diplomatic contact with Saudi Arabia on Hormuz and the Houthis.
