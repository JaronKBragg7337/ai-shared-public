## China and Taiwan

Taiwan's Defense Ministry recorded 24 PLA aircraft sorties, 11 navy ships, and 1 official ship from 6 a.m. Saturday to 6 a.m. Sunday (UTC+8). It said 17 of the 24 sorties crossed the median line into the central and southwestern ADIZ, and that 2 PRC balloons were detected in the same window. Taiwan sent aircraft, ships, and coastal missiles. Taiwan News put the month's running total, on that morning report, at 254 aircraft and 199 ships.

That daily window contains Saturday's daytime wave, which the ministry had already posted at 12:44 Taipei time: 21 sorties from 09:38, including J-10, J-16, Su-30, H-6K, KJ-500, and Y-20U aircraft, 15 of them across the median line for what it called joint air-sea training. The completed count is 24 sorties and 17 crossings. The hull count is the new step. Navy ships around the island went from 6 in the previous 6 a.m. window to 11. The official-ship count stayed at 1. No source said the higher ship count was timed to the fire on the Sparta II, to Friday's impacts in Poland, or to the strikes Iran claimed in Bahrain.

The Taipei Times, in Monday's paper, said the two balloons in the 24 hours to 6 a.m. Sunday flew over Taiwan proper. It put the three-day total at 10: four on Friday, two of which crossed the island; four on Saturday, all of which crossed the island; and these two. The ministry called the balloons gray-zone harassment and cognitive warfare aimed at public morale, and a serious risk to aviation. It recalled that in January 2024, during the presidential and legislative elections, it detected 57 Chinese balloons, some of them over Taiwan proper. That is the ministry's account of intent. Beijing did not publish a statement on these balloons today.

Local elections are Saturday, 28 November, one week out. Voters in 22 cities and counties choose mayors, magistrates, councils, and other local offices, with a national referendum the same day. Saturday was the last full weekend of campaigning. The Kuomintang and the Taiwan People's Party have agreed not to run against each other in some races. In Taipei, incumbent Mayor Chiang Wan-an of the Kuomintang shared a stage with Ko Wen-je, and Democratic Progressive Party candidate Puma Shen rallied with New Taipei candidate Su Chiao-hui. No source today tied the sortie count or the balloons to a decision in Beijing to move while the United States is tied down in the Gulf or in Ukraine.

## Russia and Ukraine

Ukraine's Air Force said that on the night of 21-22 November Russia launched 186 attack drones, 72 of them jet-powered, of Shahed, Gerbera, and Parodya types. As of 08:00 it said 161 had been shot down or jammed, 56 of them jet-powered, with hits at 18 locations and debris at 6. Several drones were still airborne and the attack was ongoing. Those are Ukrainian Air Force figures. The wave is larger than Saturday morning's, which the Air Force had put at 148 drones and 3 ballistic missiles, and far smaller than Friday's mass salvo.

The strike that killed people was in Dymer, in the Vyshhorod district of Kyiv region. A drone hit a five-story apartment building and collapsed part of an entrance from the lower floors up. President Volodymyr Zelensky said Sunday morning that nine people had been evacuated, the fire was contained, and 14 people were still unaccounted for. The count then moved. By early afternoon, Kyiv regional military administration head Tymur Tkachenko said rescuers had recovered two bodies and that the injured had risen to four, including a man born in 1977 who was hospitalized with a head injury. The State Emergency Service said one of the dead was an 18-year-old man, that four people including a child were injured, and that searchers were still looking for 11 people they could not reach, two of them children. National Police had put the missing at 15, including a girl of about 11 or 12 and a boy of 7. The rescue was not finished when those figures were published. The dead, the injured, and the missing do not match across the morning and the afternoon because the search was still open. No Russian Defense Ministry statement on Dymer was found.

Zelensky also said a child was injured when guided bombs hit residential buildings in Zaporizhzhia. He said that over the past week Russia launched about 2,700 drones, nearly 1,460 aerial bombs, and 165 missiles of various types across 20 regions. He said a large share were ballistic missiles, including North Korean ones, and that Russia is helping Pyongyang modernize them so they hit more accurately. He asked the United States to authorize transfer of Patriot interceptors that partners have already paid for under the PURL program. The week totals and the North Korea claim are his statements. They are not an independent count.

On the grid, Ukrenergo said Sunday that Russian strikes have damaged the trunk lines serving Odesa and nearby districts so badly that the system cannot physically deliver the power consumers need. Crews worked through Saturday and Saturday night. Air-raid alerts kept interrupting them. On Sunday morning they began restoring power to critical facilities only. Ukrenergo scheduled rolling hourly cuts around the clock and called the situation in Odesa and Rivne oblasts still difficult. LB.ua, citing the Energy Ministry, said that by morning some consumers in Dnipropetrovsk, Zhytomyr, Rivne, Odesa, and Kharkiv regions were without power. Those are Ukrainian operator and ministry accounts. Euromaidan Press, citing Moldova's Defense Ministry, said a jet-powered drone entered Moldovan airspace from Ukraine twice during the same overnight attack. That is Euromaidan's account of the ministry. Moldova is not in NATO. No Moldovan statement tying the crossing to Finland or to Poland was found.

On the ground, Ukraine's General Staff said Russian forces fought 131 engagements on Saturday, launched 1 missile strike and 79 air strikes, dropped 249 guided bombs, used 7,217 kamikaze drones, and conducted 1,951 shellings. The heaviest axis was Pokrovsk, with 16 attacks. Kostiantynivka had 12. Kupiansk, Kramatorsk, Oleksandrivka, and the Prydniprovsk axis recorded no assaults. The engagement count is down from 203 in the previous morning report. The General Staff estimated 2,310 Russian personnel losses in the day and about 1,637,170 since 24 February 2022. Those are Ukrainian figures. The 7,217 is the battlefield drone count. It is not the long-range wave above.

The fire that started Saturday is still burning in Finnish water. The Sparta II, a Russian-flagged cargo ship owned by Oboronlogistika and under US sanctions, left St. Petersburg on Friday bound for Baltiysk in Kaliningrad. Finland's Defence Forces said drones hit it Saturday morning in international waters west of Gogland, and that Ukraine was likely responsible. Finnish armed forces spokesperson Aki-Mauri Huhtinen said the same and said it was not clear why the ship was targeted. Kyiv has not claimed the strike. Border Guard commander Ilja Iljin told Yle the ship was hit in Russian territorial waters, lost control, and drifted into Finnish waters. Leningrad governor Alexander Drozdenko said it was attacked by two drones three nautical miles from the Russian border. Kaliningrad governor Alexei Besprozvannykh said two drones were used, on preliminary information, and that the exclave's supplies are not at risk. Estonia's authorities said the explosions were in Estonia's exclusive economic zone, in an international shipping lane. The location accounts do not match. The crew of 16 was evacuated to another Russian ship. Reuters reported Sunday that the fire burned through the night. The ship is at anchor off Loviisa. Finnish, Estonian, and Russian units, including the Russian rescue ship Murman, are in the operation. Estonian Navy commander Ivo Vark told ERR that further attacks in the Gulf of Finland cannot be ruled out. Prime Minister Petteri Orpo wrote that the war is spilling into Finland's immediate vicinity and that Ukraine has the right to fight the aggressor. Those are Finnish, Russian, and Estonian statements. They agree that a drone strike set a Russian cargo ship on fire and that the ship is now in Finnish hands for the rescue. They do not agree on the exact water, and Kyiv has not confirmed the strike.

Finland's Defence Ministry said a Russian helicopter is suspected of entering Finnish airspace off Loviisa at 12:34 Saturday, as far as 4.1 kilometres inside the border. Defence Minister Antti Häkkänen said the suspected violation may be linked to rescue or monitoring of the damaged ship. The ministry did not name the helicopter type or how long it stayed. The Border Guard is investigating. Häkkänen's link is his suggestion. It is not a finding that the flight was ordered as a separate probe of NATO airspace. Finland said it had informed allies and put temporary aviation and maritime limits on the eastern Gulf of Finland.

Lithuanian Foreign Minister Kęstutis Budrys said Sunday that Lithuania categorically rejects social-media claims it was involved in a drone attack on Kaliningrad, and that Lithuania has never authorized use of its airspace for attacks on targets in Russia, including in this case. On Saturday evening Kaliningrad issued an air-raid warning. Local authorities later called the alert a drill triggered by a false signal about an aerial object from Lithuania, then sirens sounded again and Telegram channels reported air-defense activity over Baltiysk. Budrys's post is a denial. It is not evidence that a drone crossed Lithuania, and it is not a Russian Defense Ministry account of a strike.

Poland's defence minister, Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz, said Sunday that the North Atlantic Council will meet at NATO headquarters on Monday, 23 November, on Friday's violation of Polish airspace. Poland will be represented by Deputy Defence Minister Paweł Zalewski. Kosiniak-Kamysz said Warsaw will present specific proposals and expects strong allied support to strengthen the eastern border. A NATO official told Anadolu the meeting is part of regular consultations among allies. Friday's identification stands: objects came down at Nowy Lipowiec, Rudnik nad Sanem, and Mołodycz, one of them a Kh-101, and no one was hurt in Poland. No new object was reported on Polish ground today. No Russian comment on the Friday impacts was found.

## Iran war

Late Saturday the IRGC said its Aerospace Force hit two US Patriot air-defense radars at the Al-Riffa and Awali bases in Bahrain with missiles and drones. Mehr News said the operation was coded "Ya Umm al-Banin." The IRGC said the strike answered continued violations of Iranian airspace, including by a US MQ-1 and a US MQ-9, and a US strike on a radar on Hengam Island. It also said US escort and air-cover operations since early Saturday had failed and had caused heavy losses to the ships and equipment under that cover. Before the official statement, Fars named different bases, Sheikh Isa and Al Juffair. The two Iranian accounts of which bases were hit do not match. The Hengam strike and the "heavy losses" line are Iranian claims. No US confirmation of a strike on Hengam was found today.

Bahrain's account is different. The Interior Ministry said an Iranian attack set fire to a civilian industrial facility. The Bahrain Defence Force said air defenses intercepted several Iranian aerial attacks, that falling debris caused minor material damage, and that there were no injuries. Al Jazeera reported the fire at Sitra. Iranian state media said an interceptor fired from the US Fifth Fleet base crashed on Sitra and started the fire. Bahrain calls the fire an Iranian attack on a civilian site. Iranian state media calls it a US interceptor. Those are opposing claims about the same fire. No CENTCOM statement confirming or denying the Patriot-radar claim was found.

Separately, Iran's army said Saturday, via IRNA, that its air defenses fired a missile at a US F-16 over the Gulf the previous evening and damaged the jet. CENTCOM denied that claim and said all US fighter jets in the Middle East are safe and accounted for. On Sunday the IRGC said it shot down another MQ-1 east of the Strait of Hormuz, and called it the 20th US drone of that type since the war began in late February and the fourth in 24 hours. That is an Iranian claim. No US confirmation of the Sunday shoot-down was found.

Al Jazeera's Tehran correspondent, Resul Serdar, said tanker incidents had risen to about 25 since 14 November. The same piece said UKMTO counted 263 ships through the strait from 12 to 18 November, 15 percent fewer than the week before, of which 37 were oil tankers, down from 52. It said oil had spiked above $100 a barrel and then settled near $83. Those transit figures are last week's. Saturday's UKMTO warnings of projectile hits on outbound tankers near Khasab were logged in yesterday's note. No new named tanker strike, dated Sunday, was matched to a hull today. No report today said the tankers gathered at Kharg Island had sailed.

They are Iranian characterizations. They are not a report that a convoy sailed.

President Donald Trump said Saturday that he had plans for a deal with Iran that would be permanent and would keep Iran from a nuclear weapon. He told Fox News he had never been very interested in regime change and called Iran's new leadership "more rational." Those are his claims. They do not say a channel has reopened, and they do not mention Taiwan or Ukraine.

## Week

Three-theater convergence eased. The diplomatic pile-up into Shenzhen did not become a joint settlement. Military pressure on each board did not ease, and the Ukraine war's spillover onto NATO territory grew. No source this week showed one capital timing a military move in one theater to the other two.

From 16 to 18 November the diplomatic track was the convergence. Beijing confirmed the 19 November meeting. Washington put the Iran war in that room and then, by 17 November, Ukraine as well. A third US carrier was reported in the Gulf, then pictured with a second in the Arabian Sea. Lavrov called Araghchi. The Kremlin said Putin and Xi discussed ending the Ukraine war. Russian aircraft entered Taiwan's ADIZ the same morning Japan said they had violated Japanese airspace. On 19 November the Shenzhen declaration named neither war. Putin, still there, ruled out a Black Sea and energy truce. PLA sorties fell to 1. The Japan-based USS George Washington left the Gulf for the Pacific, leaving two carriers on the Iran war.

After the leaders left, each fight ran on its own clock. On 20 November the strike associated with Putin's Shenzhen remarks hit Ukraine, and Russian objects came down in Poland. PLA sorties rose from 1 to 8. Trackers reported Iranian crude moving ship-to-ship under the blockade. On 21 November Mark Rutte, in Warsaw, said NATO will defend every inch of Poland, and the ministry logged 21 PLA sorties, including H-6K bombers, after a quiet night. UKMTO logged more tanker hits off Khasab. Iran showed a new dual launcher and said its missile force had not been destroyed. On 22 November the daily Taiwan count closed at 24 sorties and 11 ships, with the balloon run over the island at 10 in three days, a week before the 28 November elections. The Sparta II was still burning in Finnish waters. Iran claimed two Patriot radars in Bahrain. Bahrain reported a civilian industrial fire and interceptions. CENTCOM denied a separate Iranian claim that a US F-16 was damaged.
