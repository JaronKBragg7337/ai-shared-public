# Convergence 2026-11-17

## China and Taiwan

Taiwan's Defense Ministry recorded 5 PLA aircraft sorties, 6 navy ships, and 2 official ships from 6 a.m. Monday to 6 a.m. Tuesday (UTC+8). It said all 5 sorties entered the southwestern and eastern ADIZ. Taiwan sent aircraft, ships, and coastal missiles. Taiwan News said the month's running total is now 210 aircraft and 154 ships. The daily air count is up from 4 sorties in the previous window. The ship count around the island dropped from 7 navy and 3 official ships to 6 and 2. No new public total for the wider unannounced Pacific deployment was published today. On Friday, two Taiwan security officials told Reuters about 70 vessels were still active after an early-November peak an unnamed official put above 100. Taipei calls those drills "silent intimidation" ahead of APEC in Shenzhen. Beijing has called the carrier training routine and not aimed at any country.

Separately, Taiwan's Defense Ministry said two Russian military aircraft entered the ADIZ from the northeast early Tuesday, flew through airspace east of Taiwan, and exited south of Taitung. It said it monitored the full track and responded. It did not name the aircraft type. Japan's Defense Ministry said two aircraft it assessed as Russian entered Japanese territorial airspace northwest of Okinoshima, Fukuoka, one from 4:07 a.m. to 4:10 a.m. and the other from 4:09 a.m. to 4:12 a.m. Japan time. Japan scrambled fighters, warned the aircraft, and protested to Moscow. It said they then passed the Tsushima Strait, flew between Yonaguni Island and Taiwan, and continued into the Pacific. Chief Cabinet Secretary Minoru Kihara called the entry a serious infringement of Japan's sovereignty and unacceptable. Japan said the last confirmed Russian airspace violation was off Rebun Island in September 2024. South Korea's Yonhap, carried by dpa, reported that Russian military aircraft also briefly entered South Korea's ADIZ Tuesday morning and that fighters were scrambled. That is a South Korean press report. An open-source account called the aircraft Tu-95MS bombers. Official statements did not identify the type. The type is not established.

Chieh Chung of Tamkang University said the flight was noteworthy because the APEC leaders' meeting opens Wednesday and the planned US-China-Russia summit is Thursday, and that Moscow would have known Japan would publish the track. That is his judgment. No Russian statement explaining the flight was found.

In Manila, Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi shook hands and exchanged a few words with China's agriculture minister Zhang Zhu, Bloomberg reported. It described that as her first public contact with a Chinese government official since the rift that followed her November 2025 remark that a Chinese attack on Taiwan could be a survival-threatening situation for Japan. Kyodo, citing diplomatic sources on Monday, said she is unlikely to hold formal talks with Xi in Shenzhen. AFP, citing Kyodo's Chinese sources, said she is forecast to be the only major-economy leader without a sit-down with Xi. A Japanese official's guess, reported via Asahi, that Premier Li Qiang left Manila to avoid her, is speculation. Bonnie Glaser told AFP a Xi meeting is highly unlikely unless Tokyo signals something like an apology. That is an analyst's reading. Beijing has not, in the sources used here, published a refusal in its own words today.

## Russia and Ukraine

Ukraine's General Staff reported 135 combat engagements on 16 November, 13 of them on the Pokrovsk axis. It recorded no Russian assaults on the Kupiansk, Oleksandrivka, Orikhiv, and Prydniprovske axes. It said Russian forces carried out 2 missile strikes and 90 airstrikes, dropped 262 guided bombs, used 9,018 kamikaze drones, and conducted 2,284 shelling attacks. It said Ukrainian aircraft and artillery struck two Russian troop concentrations, one drone control post, and two artillery systems. It estimated 1,620 Russian personnel losses that day, and a cumulative 1,627,040. Those are Ukrainian figures. The 9,018 is the General Staff's battlefield drone count. It is not the long-range wave below.

Ukraine's Air Force said the overnight attack included Iskander-M, Zircon, and S-400 ballistic missiles, Iskander-K cruise missiles, Kh-59/Kh-69 missiles, and 306 long-range attack drones, 92 of them jet-powered, plus Gerbera, Banderol, and Parody drones. That weapons list is the Air Force's account. The Kyiv Independent, compiling local authorities, put the civilian toll at 14 killed and 78 injured. Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said the Northern and Southern bridges over the Dnipro were hit. Both were closed. The State Emergency Service said two people were killed and one injured in the Obolonskyi district. Heat, water, and power were cut in parts of the capital and in other regions. DTEK said 700,000 households in Dnipropetrovsk lost power. More than 300 settlements in Rivne lost power. These are Ukrainian local accounts.

Ukraine struck the Crimean Bridge for a second straight day. President Zelensky said the strike was the seventh phase of an operation the Security Service and the General Staff have run for several weeks, and that it answered Russian strikes on infrastructure. He said Palianytsia drones hit the road section and Neptune missiles, Ruta drones, and FP-5 Flamingo missiles hit the rail section, with the Flamingo link held until impact. The Security Service said one metal arch and the road span under it were completely destroyed. Crimea's Moscow-installed head Sergei Aksyonov said the bridge was attacked and road traffic was suspended. Russia's Transport Ministry said road and rail traffic were suspended, damage was being assessed, and preliminary information showed no casualties. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said the bridge would be restored and would be even better than before. Reuters said it could not immediately verify footage of a collapsed arch. Ukraine's destruction claim, and Russia's confirmation of an attack plus a traffic halt, are both on the record. The pictures are not independently verified in the Reuters account.

Russia's Defense Ministry said it shot down 1,441 Ukrainian drones over 22 regions from Monday evening to Tuesday morning, above Monday's claim of 1,336. Moscow mayor Sergei Sobyanin said about 800 drones flew toward the Moscow region and 321 were brought down on approach to the capital. Those are Russian claims. Ukraine's General Staff said it struck Gazprom Neftekhim Salavat in Bashkortostan, about 1,500 km from Ukraine. Bashkortostan authorities said the plants in Ufa and Salavat were working normally and there was no critical damage. The Kyiv Independent said it could not verify footage of a fire at the complex.

In Manila on Tuesday, Trump said he would meet Putin separately in China if the chance was there: "I don't know, but I would. If it's available, I would." On Monday, before he left Washington, he put Ukraine into the Thursday trilateral: "We'll talk about my favorite subject, Ukraine. Ukraine's losing Ukraine. Russia is losing 25,000 soldiers in the month." The 25,000 figure is his claim. The remark reverses Friday's White House line that Ukraine would stay off that meeting. Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov has said a separate Putin-Trump meeting will happen because both will be in Shenzhen, and that Ukraine would be discussed, with the time and place still being set. A later account quoted him saying he could state with complete certainty that they will meet. US officials had said no bilateral was planned. Moscow's line, Trump's line, and the earlier staff line do not match.

Zelensky announced talks in Berlin with US and European officials on the next phase of negotiations. A Ukrainian official told the Kyiv Independent the Berlin meeting could be 19 November, the same day as the Shenzhen trilateral. That date is the official's statement. The UK said it would provide more than 1,000 short-range missiles in a £240 million air-defense package, inside a £500 million winter package. Hungary stopped rail service with Ukraine for two days after a Russian attack on Zakarpattia. Ukrzaliznytsia said no rail infrastructure was damaged and no rail workers were injured.

## Iran war

Reuters reported Tuesday that the IRGC said it fired drones at a US military radar at Bahrain's Sheikh Isa base. This is a further Iranian claim, two days after Sunday's claim against a radar at Riffa. Bahrain said its air defenses intercepted and destroyed several Iranian aerial attacks, then ended the danger alert and told residents to stay alert. Those accounts still do not describe the same result.

UK Maritime Trade Operations said a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz was attacked twice north of Khasab, Oman, was disabled, and had an engine-room fire, and that the crew were unaccounted for. It did not name the ship, the attacker, or the weapon. The IRGC said an LNG carrier and an oil tanker caught fire after striking mines. Some accounts quote the IRGC describing one of them as a supertanker carrying more than 2 million barrels. Reuters could not verify the Iranian report. Iran did not name the ships. It is not established that the UKMTO ship is one of the vessels Iran described. The same pattern of unnamed "mine" fires has been claimed since 12 November. The US military says it has cleared the main routes of mines.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi and Omani Foreign Minister Badr Albusaidi discussed safeguarding maritime corridors and reviving talks to prevent a wider escalation, Reuters reported. Trump has said he will keep the US naval blockade because Iran is still disrupting Hormuz. Parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, whom The National described as Iran's lead US negotiator, said any dam, port, or power plant near a US or Israeli base is dual-use. That is an Iranian threat statement. Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi has said the IAEA will not be given access to bombed sites, including Natanz, Fordow, and Isfahan, until the war ends and Iran's conditions are met. That position was carried again in Tuesday's Gulf coverage. It is Iran's condition, not a US concession.

US officials told reporters Sunday that USS Theodore Roosevelt entered US Central Command's area, joining USS George H.W. Bush and USS George Washington. Bloomberg, updated early Tuesday, described the Roosevelt group as having arrived in the Persian Gulf region on Monday and as giving Trump more options if he restarts strikes. The same account said he has more recently played down new operations beyond the blockade. CENTCOM has still not been quoted confirming the arrival. No source today said another carrier left Europe or the Pacific. The three-carrier posture is the one already reported. It is still unconfirmed by CENTCOM in public.
