## China and Taiwan

Taiwan's Defense Ministry recorded 6 PLA aircraft sorties and 12 navy ships from 6 a.m. Sunday to 6 a.m. Monday (UTC+8). It said 5 of the 6 sorties crossed the median line into the southwestern and eastern ADIZ. The ministry text for this window does not list an official ship and does not list balloons. Taiwan sent aircraft, ships, and coastal missiles. Taiwan News put the month's running total, on that morning report, at 260 aircraft and 211 ships. The air count is down from 24 sorties and 17 crossings in the previous window. The navy count around the island is up from 11 to 12. No source said the drop in aircraft, or the one-ship rise, was timed to the strikes Ukraine claimed off Libya, to the objects on Polish ground, or to the Hormuz incidents.

Monday's Taipei Times carried the balloon count already logged yesterday: 10 Chinese balloons over three days through 6 a.m. Sunday, two of them in that last window over Taiwan proper, days before Saturday's local elections. That story describes the previous window. It is not a new balloon detection for the window that ended 6 a.m. Monday.

Local elections are Saturday, 28 November, five days out. Mainland Affairs Council deputy head Shen Yu-chung said Monday that China's methods of interfering in those elections are growing more sophisticated with artificial intelligence and facial recognition, and that the threat has become a serious national-security issue. He cited alleged financing of candidates, cognitive warfare, and money moved through underground betting. The case he highlighted is the 20 November indictment of Taiwan People's Party Taoyuan council candidate Chou Hsiao-yun. Prosecutors say she took Chinese campaign money, including US$10,000 in December 2025 and NT$300,000 a month in January and February 2026, and that she supplied photographs for about 20,000 fake Facebook accounts run by a Chinese online team she met in Xiamen. They are seeking three years and six months. Chou wrote Friday that she never took instructions or foreign funds, that an indictment is not a conviction, and that charging her eight days before the vote is unfair. TPP chairman Huang Kuo-chang said Saturday that candidates must address the allegations. Those are the prosecutors' allegations and Chou's denial. No Beijing statement on the case was found today.

On the 19 November meeting in Shenzhen, Xinhua's account, examined again at a Taipei seminar Monday, attributes to Donald Trump the sentence that the United States "does not support Taiwan independence." After the May and September meetings, Xinhua quoted Xi pressing Washington. This readout puts the sentence in Trump's mouth. The White House had neither confirmed nor denied it as of Monday, and it has not issued a fact sheet on those bilateral talks. Mainland Affairs Council head Chiu Chui-cheng said Friday, after a check with the foreign ministry, that the Chinese account was not factual, and he asked Washington to clarify. He did not say whether Trump spoke the words. Scholars at Monday's seminar said the phrase is Washington's formulation since 1998, and that Beijing's preferred word is "opposes." They said they do not read the Xinhua line as a US policy shift. That is their reading. It is not a White House confirmation. The seminar did not mention Hormuz or Ukraine.

No new US chip-export rule was published today. The Taiex closed up 778.60 points, or 2.16 percent, at 36,811.89. Dealers tied the bounce to a Truth Social post in which Trump said he hopes Nvidia's board rehires Jensen Huang, and to Saturday's tighter short-sale rules. Foreign investors still sold a net NT$28.95 billion. That is a market move. It is not a change in export controls, and no source tied it to the sortie count.

## Russia and Ukraine

Ukraine's Air Force said that from 18:00 on Sunday, Russia launched 153 attack drones, 60 of them jet-powered, of Shahed, Gerbera, and Parodya types, from Oryol, Kursk, Millerovo, Shatalovo, Primorsko-Akhtarsk, occupied Donetsk, and Hvardiiske and Chauda in occupied Crimea. As of 08:00 Monday it said 134 had been shot down or jammed, 42 of them jet-powered, with hits at 11 locations and debris at 7. Several drones were still airborne and the attack was ongoing. Those are Ukrainian Air Force figures. The wave is smaller than Sunday morning's, which the Air Force had put at 186 drones.

The toll that rose overnight was from Sunday's strike on a five-story building in Dymer, in the Vyshhorod district of Kyiv region. Kyiv regional police said Monday morning that 11 people were dead, including a young girl, that two people were still missing, and that seven were injured, two of them children. President Volodymyr Zelensky said the bodies of seven more people were recovered overnight, that the toll from this one strike was 11, and that the fate of two people was still unknown. He said searchers had to stop because of the threat of further strikes and of Shahed drones. Rescuers pulled a seven-year-old boy, Davyd, out alive on Sunday after about 14 hours, and a 79-year-old woman, Valentyna, after about 22 hours. Zelensky said the boy was in hospital with his parents, and that four other injured people were in hospital, including the woman. The police injury count and Zelensky's hospital count do not match. The dead and the missing are the same in the police account and in his. No Russian Defense Ministry statement on Dymer was found.

Separate strikes in the same region, as reported by Kyiv Post from local officials, killed three people at a supermarket in Vyshneve on Sunday evening, with 15 injured, including three children, according to Mayor Illia Dikov, and killed one person in a private home in the Bila Tserkva district. Kyiv Post's regional total of at least 15 dead adds those strikes to the 11 in Dymer. In Lozova, in Kharkiv region, Mayor Serhii Zelensky said a mass strike injured 12 people and damaged six apartment buildings, houses, and heating equipment. Those are Ukrainian local accounts.

On the ground, Ukraine's General Staff said Russian forces fought 149 engagements on Sunday, launched 72 air strikes, dropped 254 guided bombs, used 7,964 kamikaze drones, and conducted 2,250 shellings, 25 of them with multiple-launch rockets. The heaviest axis was Kostiantynivka, with 17 attacks. Pokrovsk had 15. Kramatorsk recorded none. The engagement count is up from 131 in the previous morning report. The General Staff estimated 2,471 Russian personnel losses in the day and about 1,639,641 since 24 February 2022. Those are Ukrainian figures. The 7,964 is the battlefield drone count. It is not the long-range wave above.

The fire in Finnish water is off Finland's hands. Reuters reported Sunday that the Russian rescue ship Murman had begun towing the Sparta II out of Finnish waters after two days of firefighting, and that rescue responsibility would pass to Russia once the ship left. The Finnish military has said the Saturday drone strike was likely Ukraine's. Daily Finland, citing a Border Guard release and updated Monday, said the ship left Finland on Sunday evening. A Baltic daily digest for Monday said the sea and air restrictions were lifted and that no new strike in the Gulf of Finland was reported in the overnight window. No Kremlin statement on the ship was found.

Poland's identification of Friday's impacts changed. Lublin prosecutors said all three objects that came down in the southeast were Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles, made in the fourth quarter of 2026 at Dubna, in Moscow oblast. The first public account was one confirmed Kh-101 and two other objects, described as a suspected missile and a suspected jet-powered drone. The wreckage exam is what the prosecutors are relying on. No one was hurt in Poland. No new object was reported on Polish ground today. No Russian comment on the identification was found.

The North Atlantic Council was due to meet in Brussels on Monday on those impacts. Poland's defence minister had said Warsaw would bring concrete proposals and expected allied support for the eastern border. Deputy Defence Minister Paweł Zalewski was to represent Poland. A NATO official had described the session on Sunday as part of regular consultations, and had said it was not a special session. No public decision from Monday's council was found. In Berlin, at the NATO Parliamentary Assembly, Secretary General Mark Rutte said the incident is clearly dangerous and that Poland and the allies are still deciding whether the crossings were intentional. He said he would not speculate. German Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul said Germany will defend every centimeter of NATO territory, and told Vladimir Putin that a defensive alliance is prepared to defend itself. Poland's foreign ministry said it intends to summon the Russian ambassador after the security services and the military finish their work. Foreign Minister Radosław Sikorski had said Saturday that Warsaw would convey its strongest displeasure. Tusk's "intentional provocation" remains his characterization. Rutte's line on Monday is that intent is not yet a finding.

Zelensky told the same assembly, by video, that a four-party meeting of Ukraine, Europe, the United States, and Russia should happen as soon as possible. He said partners want to try before Christmas, and that Kyiv is ready tomorrow. That is his proposal. It is not a date.

The same morning he confirmed a strike far from that front. He said seven Ukrainian-made naval drones sank the Russian corvette Merkury off Libya on Sunday, that the ship was commissioned in 2023, and that a Ka-27 helicopter was destroyed with it. He said a second target, the dry cargo ship MV Angara, was hit, and that Russia used it to carry weapons. The SBU said the drones launched from a mothership, that the corvette was struck at its berth in Benghazi, and that Russian naval logistics have no safe route even beyond the Black Sea. The SBU describes Angara as a sanctioned ship on the routes it calls the Syrian Express and the Libyan Express, and says Merkury was escorting it. Reuters said it verified footage of fire and smoke on the ships, and that Libya's eastern-based government said the corvette had been docked at the Benghazi naval base. Vantor satellite imagery published with that reporting shows a corvette capsized by the stern beside a damaged pier. Osama Hamad, prime minister of that eastern government, said he had asked the United Nations to investigate suspicions that the boats were launched from western Libya and to check for Ukrainian military personnel there. Reuters, citing local medical sources, reported ten injuries, including Russians and Libyans. The sinking, the helicopter, and the weapons cargo are Ukrainian claims. The docking and the request for a UN inquiry are the eastern Libyan government's statements. No Russian Defense Ministry confirmation of the sinking was found. Zelensky called the operation a response to Russian aggression. He did not mention Iran, China, or Taiwan.

German public broadcasters NDR and WDR, and the newspaper Süddeutsche Zeitung, reported Monday that Western intelligence services believe Russia intends to place a nuclear-armed satellite in orbit by the end of this year, and that the assessment was shared among NATO members. A Bundeswehr spokesman declined to comment on that report. He said Russia has the capability, and that a nuclear blast in space would wreck satellite communications. The German defence ministry said it watches developments in space. The Russian embassy in Berlin said it had no information on such plans. A US Defense Intelligence Agency warning from October, that Russia was developing a satellite able to carry a nuclear weapon, is the earlier public marker the German report cites. None of this is a launch. No source tied the report to the PLA count, to Hormuz, or to the Libya strike.

## Iran war

Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said Monday that Tehran will not tolerate the US naval blockade, that Tehran calls the blockade an act of military aggression, and that Iran will use political, economic, and military tools against it, at a time its own authorities choose. He said the Strait of Hormuz could be restored quickly if Washington met Iran's conditions, and that Tehran would not compromise on those conditions. Asked about reports that the White House had requested Pentagon and intelligence assessments of how Iran would answer possible US attack options, including options meant to avoid retaliation against Gulf oil and gas sites, he said American experts would get their answer from Brig. Gen. Seyyed Majid Mousavi, commander of the Revolutionary Guard aerospace force, "in a neat and elegant package." He also said the best course for the United States is to leave the region. Those are his statements. They are not a report that a missile was fired on Monday, and they do not mention Taiwan or Ukraine.

President Donald Trump's lines from Sunday are the claims Baghaei was answering. He told Fox News he is inclined to resume strikes on Iran after the New Year if there is no deal, and that tankers trying to run the blockade would end "at the bottom of the sea like the rest of the navy." On Truth Social he wrote that Iran launched more drones and missiles in the last week than at any time in more than a year, and that they were all knocked down and did no damage. He gave no numbers. Those are his claims. They do not say a channel has produced a text.

UK Maritime Trade Operations said on Sunday evening that it had a report of an incident involving a tanker and military forces in the Gulf of Oman, near Chabahar, and that it was still investigating. It did not name the ship or the forces. A maritime security analyst wrote that US forces struck a tanker exporting Iranian oil as part of the blockade. GlobalSecurity's day log says neither Washington nor Tehran confirmed that, and that no CENTCOM statement on the incident was found. Separately, the IRGC navy said two supertankers on what it called unauthorized routes hit mines, one north of Ghanam Island and one east of the strait, and stopped. That is an Iranian claim. It is not established that those hulls are the UKMTO case.

Tehran's Hamshahri reported 14 tankers preparing at Kharg Island. No breakout sailing was confirmed on Monday. Those figures describe the blockade. They are not a new convoy.

The Foreign Ministry rejected the IRGC claim that the targets were US Patriot radars at Al-Riffa and Awali, called the target exclusively civilian, and said Bahrain reserves the right of self-defense under Article 51 of the UN Charter. Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and Kuwait condemned the attack. The IRGC claim and Bahrain's claim still describe different targets. No CENTCOM confirmation of a destroyed Patriot radar was found.

Houthi media said a Saudi strike on Hodeidah on Monday killed 10 people. Riyadh did not confirm that toll. The figures were not verified.
