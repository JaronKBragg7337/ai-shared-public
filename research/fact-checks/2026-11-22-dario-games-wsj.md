# Fact-check: "Dario Amodei suggested immersive video games could pacify the masses left jobless by AI" (Polymarket, 11/22 12:51 PM ET)

Method: 3 Claude searchers + Grok (xAI, outside view) + an adversarial checker told to attack both ways (distortion vs. going soft
on Anthropic). Run 11/22 ~8 PM ET for Jaron. The checker found the Claude searchers went soft in places (see "hard truth").

**Verdict: core real, packaging misleading.**
- Source: WSJ profile "The Brother-Sister Duo Who Want to Keep Anthropic Weird" (Berber Jin, Maxwell Zeff, Keach Hagey), online
  Fri 11/20 9:04 PM ET, around Anthropic's Nasdaq debut (11/19). Paywalled.
- The WSJ's own sentence (not Dario's words): while at OpenAI (2016-2020), in debates with coworkers known from "widely circulated
  Slack messages", he suggested immersive videogames "might be a solution for the political strife caused by AI-induced joblessness".
  No date, no exact Slack text, no sign it was a joke.
- Polymarket's changes: "JUST IN" on a ~40-hour-old story about 6-10-year-old messages; dropped "while at OpenAI" (co-author Zeff's
  own post kept it); "pacify the masses" first appears in The Verge's headline (11/22 10:52 AM), not the WSJ; added a Ready Player One
  still. Core claim otherwise matches and says "reportedly".
- Hard truth: "bread and circuses" is a fair reading of the WSJ's own wording; criticism started before Polymarket (Gary Marcus,
  economist Itai Sher quoting Zeff's accurate post). It clashes with his Jan 2026 essay The Adolescence of Technology (warns of a
  comfortable life without freedom; job loss -> unrest; public fixes are economic: taxes on AI companies, wage insurance, income
  support). As of ~8 PM ET 11/22 neither he nor Anthropic had commented. A proposed Community Note ("not his current view") is unbacked.
- Spread (news-desk lesson): WSJ -> Zeff post -> Curran screenshot -> The Verge "pacify" headline -> Polymarket "JUST IN" (~2M views),
  each hop dropping context. 24/<address> wrongly said he told the WSJ directly.
