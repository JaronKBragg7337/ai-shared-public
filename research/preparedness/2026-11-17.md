## Plain summary (read this first)

1. Jaron is right that many countries are doing this. We found government preparedness moves in 62 countries, plus the EU, NATO and CARICOM. About 10 more Latin American countries only changed fuel prices.
2. What they are doing: home-supply advice in about 30 countries (most say 72 hours; Sweden, Norway, Switzerland and Taiwan say a week; Germany 10 days; Austria 10-14 days). State food or fuel reserves in about 30. Shelter work in about 20. Big drills or alert tests in about 25.
3. Real "emergency stores" are only a handful: Denmark (official, 51 Salling stores ready in 2027), Israel ("iron branches", since 2024), Taiwan (5,668 supply sites, a supermarket rationing drill in Aug 2026) and the Dutch plan.
4. NEW in 2026: home-prep campaigns in Belgium, Ireland (booklet to every home), Luxembourg, the Netherlands (second wave), the UK ("Be Ready", Oct 22), Iceland and the EU (72-hour rule, Nov 3). New laws or agencies in Sweden, Finland, Japan, the Netherlands, Canada and New Zealand. A rush of fuel-reserve moves after the Iran war began on Feb 28.
5. Speeding up most: the Nordic and Baltic countries (money, laws, shelters), Western Europe (campaigns), the Gulf and Israel (shelters and stockpiles actually used in war), and fuel reserves across Asia and Africa. The Americas are steady, and the US is handing preparedness down to the states.
6. The Netherlands, plainly: REPORTED, not official yet. The supermarket group CBL, with Albert Heijn, Jumbo and Aldi, plans to turn some existing stores into "noodwinkels" (emergency stores) for a "fase zwart" (long food-supply breakdown). They would have generators, a basic range and stock moved by hand, with a store within 15 minutes' drive of everyone. It is still a plan in talks with the Agriculture ministry. How many stores, which goods and who pays are not settled, and there is no CBL or government page yet.
7. Why: 2025 reasons were mostly Russia, climate and general resilience. 2026 reasons are mostly the Iran war and the Strait of Hormuz, plus cyberattacks on the food chain (hacked supermarket fridges in France, Sweden, Estonia, Belgium and Ireland in Oct-Nov; on Nov 9 France named an Iranian IRGC unit for its wave).
8. Unexplained failures: 14 significant items from Oct 15 to Nov 16 (2 acts of war, 1 planted device, 11 failures). A normal month has about 8-15, and the same weeks in 2025 had about 12-15. Verdict: at or slightly above normal overall, but clearly above normal in the US, with 5 events in 5 days (Nov 12-16). How sure: medium-low.
9. Each US event has a different stated cause and vendor, and nobody claimed any of them. Whether any of them is linked to Iran is not established either way. The FAA router report and Microsoft's review would settle it.
10. Today (Nov 17), the 2:20 PM ET national alert test on TV and radio is routine. It was announced on Sept 16. Fact-checkers found no government announcement of the viral "90-day blackout from Nov 17".

## 1. The Netherlands item, in detail

**What the viral post said.** Grok, replying to a post with 649K views, said Dutch supermarkets coordinated by the CBL are preparing "emergency stores" for a "black phase". It said existing stores would get generators and basic goods within a 15-minute drive of every citizen.

**What holds up (REPORTED: AD 15 Nov 2026, FD 31 Oct 2026, Omroep Brabant 31 Oct):**
- The CBL and the chains (Albert Heijn, Jumbo, Aldi) are planning "noodwinkels" for a "fase zwart", meaning normal food supply breaks down for a long time (war, a large cyberattack, a long nationwide blackout).
- They would use some of the roughly 4,500 existing supermarkets, not new sites. Chosen stores need generators and cannot be inside closed shopping centres. Stock moves by hand because the chains' computer systems differ. The range is cut to basics.
- The target is a store within 15 minutes by car for everyone. That is a target, not something already built.
- The Albert Heijn cyberattack (from 11 Oct 2026) and attacks on cooling systems at foreign chains sped the plan up.
- Still open: how many stores, which products, storage, staffing (talks with the Social Affairs ministry) and money. Money, range and storage still have to be agreed with the Agriculture ministry (LVVN). CBL director Marc Jansen: "it costs a lot of money."
- Team lead, not re-checked by the checker: the Grok sweep found an NRC interview (5 Nov 2026) in which Jansen says they do not yet know how many stores are needed or how long they could feed people.

**What did NOT hold up or is not established either way:**
- The "December 2024" start date. Omroep Brabant says talks began "about half a year ago", after NATO asked whether the Netherlands was ready for an "Article 5" situation. That points to spring 2026. The original FD article would settle it.
- "30 minutes by bike" and "half the Netherlands under water" did not appear in any source we could open.
- No source says the chains "want the government to pay".
- No CBL or Dutch government page on the noodwinkels was found. One would move this item to ESTABLISHED.

**The official Dutch moves around it (all ESTABLISHED unless marked):**
- "Denk vooruit" 72-hour campaign (3 Nov 2025) and a booklet posted to more than 8.5 million households (Nov 2025 to Jan 2026).
- Second campaign wave (12 Oct 2026). The government says just over 4 in 10 people have now done something to prepare.
- About 70 towns are piloting "noodsteunpunten" (local emergency help points) during 2026.
- A national food security crisis plan, due early 2027 (announced 12 Dec 2025, outlined 8 Jul 2026).
- The Critical Entities Resilience Act took effect on 15 Aug 2026. Designated food firms must now prevent and recover from disruptions.
- REPORTED: State Secretary Erkens proposed that supermarkets and distribution centres keep 2-3 days of emergency stock (16-18 Oct 2026). It is not required yet.
- REPORTED: the cabinet declined researchers' advice to raise home stock from 3 days to 8 days (23 Sep 2026).

**Bottom line:** the plan is real and backed by credible press. It is still a plan, not open stores. It sits on top of a real, official Dutch build-up.

## 4. What would change our read

Signs that would point to **coordination or a known coming threat** (worth flagging to Satya at once):
- Several governments raise home-supply advice past 72 hours in the same week, especially ones that long held steady (US Ready.gov, Canada, UK, Japan's 1-week ideal becoming a rule).
- Plans turn into activation outside war zones: emergency stores opened, rationing started, or food reserves released.
- A binding EU or NATO order with dates, replacing today's non-binding guidelines.
- Official threat levels rise: a new DHS NTAS bulletin (none since Sept 2025), the Dutch NCTV threat level, or the UK threat level.
- The stated reasons converge on one named, near-term threat instead of each country's own hazards.
- Investigators link two or more of the US failures to one actor, one vendor flaw or one intrusion, or a group makes a claim backed by real proof.
- A cyber finding at the Fernley water plant, or new CISA/EPA water-system alerts.
- Advice or drills are suddenly withdrawn, or contradicted without explanation.

Signs that point to **normal, if busier, preparedness**:
- Each country gives its own local reason (earthquakes in Japan, storms in the UK, El Nino in Brazil) and runs on its own timetable.
- Advice stays at 72 hours, and officials keep saying there is no specific threat (Canada, Japan, Belgium's "a precaution").
- Messaging is kept calm on purpose: Ireland dropped the 72-hour number, the Dutch cabinet declined 8 days, and the UK says "alert but not alarmed".
- Root-cause reports keep coming back as bugs and hardware (Google so far; FAA and Microsoft pending).
- Much of the European timing traces to one known source: the EU's March 2025 strategy and its Nov 2026 72-hour guidelines, rolled out nation by nation.

Our read today: mostly normal preparedness, busier because of a real war (Iran, from Feb 28) and a real wave of food-chain cyberattacks. Two places deserve a closer watch: the US failure bunch and the Fernley water plant.
