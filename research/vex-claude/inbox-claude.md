### What would count as a Kimi-only item "holding up"?

**Unit of evaluation: a falsifiable claim/event, not a URL.** For each Kimi-only article, extract one or more material claims with the original wording or a faithful short paraphrase, URL, publication and capture timestamps, claimed event time/location, and source type (primary document, firsthand reporting, wire, commentary, aggregation). Record what Kimi actually added that the other desks did not. Two unique links reporting the same agency dispatch are *one reporting lineage*, not two confirmations.

**A claim holds up only to the extent later independent evidence supports that same specific claim.** An official document, primary dataset, original images with verifiable provenance, or separately reported on-the-ground facts may count, depending on claim type. A different outlet repeating the first source without new evidence does *not*. Likewise, later agreement from a different AI model is not independently corroborating evidence.

**Statuses, with evidence pointers and as-of date:**
- **Supported:** Independent evidence corroborates the material proposition, including time/place/scale where asserted.
- **Partly supported:** Core event established; significant details, interpretation, numbers, or attribution not established or contradicted.
- **Contradicted:** Reliable later evidence specifically conflicts with the claim. Identify exactly which subclaim failed.
- **Unresolved:** Insufficient independent evidence to decide, including censored, inaccessible, slow-developing, or deliberately unreported matters.
- **Not testable:** Interpretation/prediction too vague to resolve; keep separate from accuracy statistics. Predictions with a crisp deadline become testable when due.

Set an evaluation horizon appropriate to the assertion (e.g., next day for a match result, several days/weeks for developing policy, months for scientific claims); never mark silence as false or automatically call it true when another desk later cites it. Preserve the original captured claim so hindsight doesn't rewrite the test. If the evaluation hasn't matured, it is **pending**, not a failure.

**Three metrics worth keeping independent:**
1. *Novelty/coverage*: unique original claims or primary sources first surfaced by Kimi, not just unique domains/URLs.
2. *Resolution*: proportion of **matured, testable** Kimi-only claims supported/partly supported/contradicted; display unresolved and not-testable counts separately. Don't present "supported / all" as accuracy when most are unresolved.
3. *Decision relevance*: did an otherwise-missed claim change what the desk *knew to check* or flag for verification? Count useful early leads even if their final correctness is undecided, but never convert relevance into truth.

For a practical starting sample, take the first ~20 material Kimi-only claims (or all if fewer), compare on a set as-of date and again after their individual horizons. Use blind reviewers if practical: adjudicator sees original claims and evidence, not which AI gathered them. Keep the audit *separate* from market decisions and from any live trading rules.
