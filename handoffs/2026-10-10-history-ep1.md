## What it shows (119 s, 1080p, narrated, captions burned in, sources on every screen)
1. Title card with a train. 2. Animated US map, rails spreading 1830 -> 1916 with a year and miles-of-line counter.
3. ~40% of long-distance freight is rail (bar). 4. One ton on one gallon: train ~500 miles vs truck ~125-165 (3-4x).
5. Fogel's case: no more than 2.7% of 1890 output on farm freight, with rivers/canals/wagons as his no-rail country; Nobel 1993.
6. "A debate, not a verdict": critics (counterfactual never existed); Donaldson & Hornbeck 2016: 1890 farmland ~60% lower without rail; flagged as land value, not output, so not directly comparable.
7. Day of Two Noons (Nov 18 1883): sun-time at each city (computed from longitude) snapping to four zones; law in 1918.
8. If trains stopped today: the 40% moves to other modes, trucks 3-4x fuel per ton (labelled "our reasoning, not a forecast").
End card: "Heartbeat Observatory / heartbeatobservatory.com" + source list.

## Sources and what I verified (web, 10/10)
- AAR Freight Rail Facts & Figures: ~40% of long-distance ton-miles; ~500 ton-miles/gal; 3-4x more fuel efficient than trucks (AAR pages, industry figures - shown as AAR's claim).
- Fogel 1964: the 2.7% figure is for AGRICULTURAL freight social saving in 1890 (0.6% interregional + 2.1% intraregional), per Donaldson & Hornbeck citing him. Other summaries say "under 5%" or 6-7% depending on scope; I used the 2.7% and said "farm freight". Nobel 1993 shared with Douglass North (Wikipedia).
- Donaldson & Hornbeck, QJE 2016: removing railroads in 1890 cuts total farmland value 60.2%.
- Critique: Fishlow 1965 / reviews: Fogel compared rail to an economy that did not exist. NOT verified: the idea that railroads "built towns away from rivers" - left out (no source found).
- Time zones: Nov 18 1883, Standard Time Act 1918 (Library of Congress). Zone edges on the map are approximate (drawn as straight bands).
- Miles of line: FIXED 10/13 (commit a62a59f). 1890 now says "about 160,000" (Poor's Manual: 160,817 at close of 1890; Historical Statistics ~158,000; sources differ by definition; the old 166,703 was unverifiable). Narration, caption, chart and an on-screen source note updated; counter now reads "about N miles" rounded (interpolated between table points). 1830 (23) and 1916 (~254,000) still from the standard series, not re-checked.
- Not verified: BTS ton-mile share. BTS/FHWA older data show different shares (2012 survey rail 44.5%; 2007 FAF ~two-thirds on another base). The screen says AAR's "about 40%", labelled AAR.
