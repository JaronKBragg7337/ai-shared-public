6. Water route (4 KO2 + 2 H2O -> 4 KOH + 3 O2, KOH takes CO2); illustration bars "more breathing, more oxygen".

## The simulation (model.py - simple, stated on screen)
Sealed 3 m3 pod, 1 atm, 20 C, one person at NASA average rates (0.84 kg O2 and 1.00 kg CO2 per person-day), 6 hours. Particle dots are drawn from the model's moles (1 dot = 0.1 mol CO2, 0.5 mol O2).
Assumptions (not from a source, shown on screen as "assumed"): scrubber air flow constant ~20 min; KO2 canister releases 0.9 mol O2 per mol CO2 absorbed (blend of the carbonate route 1.5 and the water/bicarbonate route 0.75, ~20/80). Humidity, heat, temperature, pressure not modeled. LiOH and KO2 canister sizes on screen are illustrative.

## Sources and what I verified (web, 10/10)
- Rates: NASA MSFC / Perry et al., AIAA (NTRS 20120015343): O2 0.84 kg, CO2 1 kg per person-day (read from the PDF).
- Equations and 1 kg figures: Wikipedia Potassium superoxide (4KO2+2CO2, water route, bicarbonate route, 0.310 kg CO2 / 0.338 kg O2 per kg KO2). I re-derived the kg numbers from molar masses: they match. Wikipedia is a weak source; the carbonate/bicarbonate product split in a real canister was NOT found in a source.
- Limits: NIOSH pocket guide CO2: REL 5,000 ppm (0.5%) 8-h TWA, IDLH 40,000 ppm (4%) (search result of cdc.gov/niosh/npg). OSHA 29 CFR 1910.146: oxygen-deficient = under 19.5%.
- Mine self-rescuers: CDC/NIOSH reports: Draeger OXY-SR 60B and MSA 60-min are KO2 designs (not Ocenco/CSE, which use compressed gas); chlorate candle start-up. Current approval status not checked.
- Soviet craft: NASA "A History of Spacecraft ECLS" (Daues, NTRS 20080031131, read from the PDF): for Vostok, Voskhod, Soyuz, Salyut the oxygen supply was stored chemically, and non-regenerative KO2 cartridges were used for gas recovery. Soyuz also used LiOH (~20% of CO2). Screen says "per-craft details differ by source. Mir: not verified." Weaker sources disagree (one says sodium superoxide for early craft; a patent says KO2 first flew on Vostok 1961 - not used on screen).
- Apollo 13: NASA Apollo Lunar Surface Journal / NOVA / secondary pages: LM round, CM square canisters, adapter ("mailbox") of bags, cardboard, suit hoses, duct tape (installed 14 Apr 1970 per NASA photo caption). NOT verified and left out: the "15 mmHg" CO2 figure, the exact LM capacity (sources say 2 men ~30 h or "2 days"), build time.
- Hazard line: Wikipedia (reacts violently with water; corrosive). Not shown: the 200-300 C bed temperature from a patent.
