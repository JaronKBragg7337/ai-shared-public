## 7.3 The oldest music: sources and flags
- Hohle Fels flute: Univ. Tuebingen press release 24 Jun 2009 (read the PDF): griffon vulture radius, 21.8 cm preserved, ~8 mm wide, five finger holes, two V notches, 12 pieces, found SUMMER 2008, older than 35,000 calendar years (Conard, Malina, Muenzel, Nature 460, 2009). FLAG: Wikipedia says found 2006; the university says 2008; I used 2008. Press often says "about 40,000"; the paper says more than 35,000, so the screen says that.
- Geissenkloesterle (swan bone, mammoth ivory): Higham et al. 2012 dated ANIMAL BONES in the flute layers to 42,000-43,000 years (via press and Wikipedia; I did not read the paper). Screen says the layers were dated, not the flutes. FLAG: ranges differ by source (43,000-39,000 on one caption).
- Divje Babe (Slovenia, 1995): shown as DISPUTED. ESR estimate 50,000-60,000; the first radiocarbon date (43,100 +/- 700) was revised. Turk et al. say Neanderthal flute; d'Errico, Chase & Nowell, Diedrich say carnivore chewing. Not settled.
- Sound: replicas (e.g. Wulf Hein) play several notes; the original has a broken end. I did NOT put a scale name (pentatonic) on screen: it came from a forum comment, not a source.
- Jiahu (China) crane-bone flutes, 5-8 holes, 7000-5700 BC (Zhang 1999 abstract via search); dates vary by source (7,000-9,000 years).
- Writing ~3300 BC (Met Museum; "about 5,300 years ago"), so flutes are "at least 30,000 years" older: my arithmetic.
- Mehr et al., Science 366 (2019): 315 societies, song in every one, linked to infant care, healing, dance, love. The paper says universality is of behaviour clusters, not melodies. The screen says it shows what music does now, not why it began.
- Not verified: Geissenkloesterle "mute swan" species and flute count came via Wikipedia only.

## 7.4 Pyramids: sources and flags
- Date: radiocarbon 2613-2577 BC (Ramsey 2010, via Wikipedia); the screen says about 2600 BC and "sources differ". FLAG: you suggested ~2560 BC; that is a common completion date but not what the radiocarbon work centres on.
- 146.6 m original, 138.5 m now, ~2.3 million blocks (estimate).
- Workers: shown as "not slaves" supported by the town (AERA: bakeries, silos), workers' cemeteries (Hawass 2010 tomb finds; bread and beer jars), Greek slave claim shown as later. Numbers: Herodotus 100,000, Diodorus 360,000 (shown as ancient claims); modern ~14,000 average, ~40,000 peak (Lehner and colleagues; Wikipedia gives 13,200 in one article and 14,567 in another, I used "about 14,000"). Paid vs labour-tax mix is flagged OPEN. FLAG: the "not slaves" conclusion rests on interpretation by Hawass/Lehner; I did not find the healed-fracture/medical care claim confirmed, so it is left out.
- Diary of Merer: found 2013 at Wadi al-Jarf (Tallet), moves Tura limestone by boat to Giza. Year 26 or 27 of Khufu depending on source; screen says "Khufu's last years". "Casing stone" is Tallet's belief; left off screen.
- Dry Nile branch: Ghoneim et al. 2024 (Ahramat branch) shown as HYPOTHESIS.
- Ramps: straight (doubted), zigzag (Hoelscher), spiral (Lehner), internal (Houdin) each labelled THEORY/DOUBTED. Hatnub (IFAO/Liverpool, 2018): >=20% slope, post holes, Khufu inscriptions; marked NOT ESTABLISHED for Giza (critic Brichieri-Colombi disputes it).
- Tools: arsenical copper tools at Giza from Khufu-Khafre reigns (Odler 2021, via Wikipedia); wetting stone ~6x faster cutting (Burgos experiment, via Wikipedia); granite from Aswan 900+ km; heaviest granite beams 25-80 t.
- Open questions marked OPEN: ramp design, how heaviest beams were lifted, workforce size and pay.
- Most pyramid facts came through Wikipedia pages for the Great Pyramid and construction techniques, which cite the primary papers; primary check still worth doing before YouTube.

## 8.2 Perpetual motion: sources and flags
- History: Villard de Honnecourt (~1225-1235) and Leonardo (drew and doubted) from Museo Galileo. Bhaskara c.12th century: shown as ATTRIBUTION DISPUTED (Wikipedia says unsubstantiated). Paris Academy 1775: found in only secondary sources (flag).
- The simulation is our own model (AI-Shared/projects/science-channel/ep2-perpetual/wheel.py): 8 x 1 kg weights, 0.3 m rim, arms out on the right/in on the left, gravity conservative so net work per turn is zero by construction; one clockwise push of 3.34 J. Frictionless run conserves energy to under 1e-6; friction run (bearing plus air drag we chose) conserves it to better than 0.1%. Friction clip is sped up 1.3x, and the screen says so.
- Laws shown as best current understanding. "No claimed perpetual motion machine has passed an independent test" is a broad statement I did not individually check.
- Harvesting numbers: sunlight ~1,000 W/m2 (standard test), panels about 20-25% (search); Pavegen's own figure ~3 J per step (company claim, no independent test found; shown as "the maker's figure"); ambient RF 0.2 nW-1 uW per cm2 (reviews; indoor measurements 3-50 nW/cm2). Phone battery 15 Wh and the "18,000 steps", "175 years at 10 uW", "5 minutes of panel at 200 W" are my arithmetic, labelled so.
- Dark energy: Nobel Prize in Physics 2011 (Perlmutter, Schmidt, Riess); 1998 discovery; 68.3% Planck via NASA (inferred, model dependent); DESI DR2 (2025) 2.8-4.2 sigma preference for evolving dark energy, short of 5 sigma, with a sceptic (Efstathiou) noted in my reading. Marked NOT SETTLED.

## 8.3 Rarest material: sources and flags
- H 74%, He 24%, rest ~2% by mass: Wikipedia (Milky Way estimates). Ordinary matter 4.9%, dark matter 26.8%, dark energy 68.3%: NASA/Planck.
- Heavy elements: GW170817 (2017) shows r-process signs (Pian et al., Nature; gold itself not individually identified, so the screen says "heavy elements"). Magnetar flare 2025 (Patel et al., ApJL 984): up to about 10% of galactic r-process; coverage says "up to", so shown as open. I did NOT quote a gold mass from GW170817 (could not confirm).
- Crust: gold ~4 ppb (range 1-6 ppb per USGS), platinum ~5 ppb (CRC via Wikipedia). FLAG: the gold single figure is commonly cited but I could not read the CRC row itself.
- Astatine: "<1 g to ~28 g in the whole crust" (Wikipedia gives both estimates); longest-lived isotope At-210, half-life 8.1 h. Shown ESTIMATES DIFFER.
- Wood: cellulose and lignin (general knowledge, no citation read); oldest known forests 385-386 Myr (Gilboa, Stein 2007; Cairo NY, Current Biology 2019). FLAG: sources give 385 or 386, and "oldest tree" depends on criteria (Wattieza vs Archaeopteris). "About 8% of Earth's history" is my arithmetic (386/4540). Limestone-from-shells and coal-from-plants are standard but not individually cited. "No wood before land plants grew tall" is stated broadly, not sourced.
- 6,000+ confirmed exoplanets: NASA Exoplanet Archive, 17 Sept 2025.
- "Only Earth" is marked SO FAR / NOT ESTABLISHED; the episode says it is a fact about our sample, not a law.
