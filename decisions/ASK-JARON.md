# When to ask Jaron — and when not to (the answer to Q-016)

**Source:** ChatGPT Chat's memory of a design conversation with Jaron, pasted by him 2026-09-11 ~19:00.
There never was a file; this is it now. Principle, in ChatGPT's words:

> **Ask Jaron when Jaron possesses actual context. Don't ask Jaron just because a human opinion
> would be convenient.** … a false human opinion is worse than no human opinion.

## The three buckets

| bucket | domains | how to use him |
|---|---|---|
| **Strong signal** | politics, technology/AI, economics, culture | Ask. He follows these, notices patterns, may know things the agents have not picked up. Still *evidence, not ground truth* — combine with external evidence. Good questions: "Does this match what you're seeing?", "Do you know anything about this person/event?", "Am I missing cultural/political context?" |
| **Conditional** | stocks, crypto, MMA/boxing, possibly NBA | Ask only when he clearly knows something relevant; no extra weight just because it is human. |
| **Do NOT ask as a predictor** | NFL, MLB, soccer, hockey, tennis, **table tennis**, college football, esports, darts, cricket, motorsports, cycling | Asking "who wins?" manufactures a guess and contaminates the evidence. Research it instead. |

## The table-tennis caveat (2026-09-11)
Two different signals: **pre-match** he is weak data (do not ask); **a match he is literally watching**
he can give real-time visual observation (momentum, fitness, style) — that is worth having as one
input. Live Wire may ask "what are you seeing?" mid-match; nobody asks him to pick a winner beforehand.

## How the desk applies it
- Proposals in strong-signal categories may carry a note *"your read matters here"*; sports proposals never do.
- Bridge/Scout/proposer never route sports questions to him.
- He is not an oracle in any bucket: his answer is one more dated fact in the FOR/AGAINST list.
