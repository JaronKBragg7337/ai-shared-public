## Remaining issues

- **WRONG — [s4]** “And the experiment was not designed to say which reading is right.” The Amboseli experiment *did* test whether elephants showed greater interest in elephant remains generally or in remains of relatives specifically; it found greater interest in elephant skulls and ivory, but no special preference for relatives. That result bears on the two readings, though it cannot establish grief or understanding of death. [McComb, Baker & Moss (2006)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17148317/)

- **WRONG — [s5]** “Her group inspected and groomed her body, some stayed close overnight.” The wording implies post-death grooming and suggests multiple chimpanzees stayed overnight. The report says the group groomed Pansy shortly before she died; none groomed her after death, and her daughter Rosie stayed near the body overnight. Suggested correction: “Her group groomed her as she was dying and inspected her after she died. Her daughter stayed near the body overnight.” [Anderson, Gillies & Lock (2010)](https://dspace.stir.ac.uk/handle/1893/2834)

- **UNSURE — [s3]** “Some seen directly and some tracked by GPS collars.” This risks implying the collars directly recorded visits to Eleanor’s body. The paper combined direct observations with long-term association and GPS-location data, so some approaches were inferred from proximity. Suggested correction: “Some were seen directly; others were inferred from GPS locations.” [Douglas-Hamilton et al. (2006)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0168159106001018)

I found no remaining misrepresentation of the scientific disagreement: the script distinguishes observed behavior from interpretations, and accurately attributes the 2019 review’s conclusion of “implicit awareness” while noting that researchers disagree about what the evidence supports.
