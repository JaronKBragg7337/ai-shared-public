The Universe

Space You Land is a persistent 3D universe where leadership is temporary, stability is earned, and any player—regardless of wealth or status—can reshape history. The galaxy operates on neutral, measurable dynamics: economy, activity, cooperation, and instability. No faction, player, or alliance can claim permanent victory. The universe itself ensures that power remains fluid.

⸻

Core Principles

Server Authority
All economy, ownership, territorial expansion, and enforcement exist as authoritative server state. The client renders; the server decides truth.

AI as Advisor, Not Authority
The AI Director proposes actions based on universe snapshots. The server validates, clamps, and logs every proposal. AI cannot bypass limits, execute raw code, or alter history.

No Pay-to-Win
Monetization funds infrastructure and personalization—never power. Paid features provide cosmetic options, storage expansion, and enhanced narration. Combat stats, expansion shares, enforcement immunity, and intel access remain completely independent of payment.

Anyone Can Matter
Systems are designed so that players without wealth, rank, or status can create meaningful change. Rebellion, espionage, solo operations, and underdog opportunities are built into the universe’s fabric.

Neutral Enforcement
Consequences are proof-based and measurable. All enforcement actions are recorded in an append-only audit ledger, creating tamper-evident accountability.

Continuity Over Control
The universe survives misuse and maintains fairness across extended timescales. Systems self-stabilize without administrative intervention.

Gameplay Loops

Personal Loop

Fly → dock → trade/missions/combat → earn credits → upgrade ship and equipment → venture into riskier territory.

Faction Loop

Complete missions → participate in votes → engage in diplomacy → enforce embargoes → declare wars → shift momentum → expand territory.

Asset Loop

Own and manage stations or planets → set fees and taxes within hard caps → defend against threats → navigate unrest, strikes, and potential coups.

Shadow Loop

Operate in black markets → trade intelligence → create obligations through bribery → plant spies or become compromised → face enforcement consequences.

Sanctuary Loop

Retreat to Private Sanctuary Instance (PSI) → store valuables and trophies → plan operations in complete privacy → return to the universe carrying only equipped items and ship cargo.

Faction Momentum and Anti-Snowball Systems

Momentum Calculation

Faction momentum (M) is calculated hourly based on:
	•	Trade throughput (risk-weighted)
	•	PvE and PvP outcomes (risk-weighted)
	•	Territory control
	•	Infrastructure stability
	•	Governance approval ratings

Per-Capita Normalization

Player contributions are normalized by faction size:

EffectiveContribution = RawContribution / (ActivePlayers^0.4)

This prevents large factions from dominating purely through numbers.

Leader Pressure and Trailer Support

Leaders accumulate strain:
	•	Increased upkeep costs
	•	Rising unrest probability
	•	Logistics vulnerability
	•	Supply line exposure

Trailers receive opportunities:
	•	Access to more lucrative contracts
	•	Discovery advantages in unexplored space
	•	Preferred logistics lanes
	•	Lower operational friction

Universe Expansion Engine

Expansion Epochs

Every 6-24 hours, new sectors become available for colonization.

Total new sectors = Base + (Scale × Active Players)

Sector generation uses deterministic seeds:
hash(epoch + faction + index + ruleset_version)

This ensures reproducibility and prevents manipulation.

Allocation Rules

Every faction receives at least 5% minimum share of new sectors.

Leader Share:
The dominant faction receives 28-45% of new sectors, scaling with dominance level.

Primary Rival Share:
The faction with the most conflict engagement against the leader (measured over 72 hours) receives 18-35% of new sectors.

Remaining Pool:
Distributed based on relationship weights:
	•	Allied factions: 0.6 weight
	•	Neutral factions: 1.0 weight
	•	Enemy factions: 1.2 weight

Trade-Assist Parity Clause

A leader’s ally can match rival share only if it meaningfully supported the leader’s economy. Trade-assist normalization must reach 0.22 or higher over the assist window.

Expansion Costs

New territory increases:
	•	Defense obligations
	•	Unrest risk exposure
	•	Piracy pressure
	•	Supply line vulnerability

Solo Integrity: Anti-Collusion System

The server monitors combat telemetry:
	•	Damage dealt
	•	Proximity patterns
	•	Kill attribution
	•	Mutual hostility windows

Flagged Patterns

When three “solo” ships engage in combat, and two coordinate suspiciously after one dies, the system flags potential collusion.

Penalty: Captain Walk-Off

Detected collusion triggers:
	•	NPC captain leaves the ship
	•	Cooldown period: 2 hours (first offense) → 12 hours → 48 hours (escalating)
	•	Ghost signature and perks disabled temporarily
	•	Message delivered: “This isn’t solo work.”

	•	Neutral arbitration available during transition
	•	Diplomatic consequences measured and applied

Espionage and Treason

Players conducting operations against their own faction can be classified as hostile operatives based on:
	•	Proven economic sabotage
	•	Intelligence leaks with verified harm
	•	Coordinated actions benefiting rival factions

Classifications use faction-appropriate terminology (no real-world political baggage).

Enforcement Ladder

Minor Offenses:
Warning issued, reputation penalty applied

Repeated Sabotage/Harassment:
15-30 minute detention (in-game time)

Espionage/State-Level Harm:
45-minute detention + deportation from faction

Detention Rules
	•	Detention time persists across logout (must be served as in-game time)
	•	Players in detention retain access to markets, chat, and PSI
	•	After capstone detention: forced faction repick (deportation enforced)

Pirate Cartel, Black Markets, and Intelligence Trading

Pirate Cartel Structure

Pirate leadership includes:
	•	One Supplier Boss per non-pirate faction’s black market
	•	One Internal Operations Boss coordinating pirate activities

Black Markets as Controlled Pressure

Black markets function optimally at 10-30% of total trade volume.
	•	Too low (<10%): Scarcity, stagnation, reduced player options
	•	Optimal (10-30%): Healthy gray economy, manageable corruption
	•	Too high (>30%): Instability spikes, governance breakdown, enforcement strain

Intelligence as Tradeable Asset

Intel items have measurable properties:
	•	Freshness: 72-hour decay timer
	•	Proof Quality: Verification strength rating
	•	Chain of Custody: Tracked through each transaction
	•	Hop Value Reduction: 20-30% loss per resale

High-quality intel can create “compromised early” warnings, giving targets time to react.

Bribery and Obligations

The server tracks:
	•	Outstanding debts
	•	Owed favors
	•	Future promises and contracts

Defaulting on obligations damages reputation and restricts market access.

Solar Storm System: Event-Driven “Sun Heating”

Purpose

A neutral, physics-themed mechanism that triggers only when the universe shows sustained instability, imbalance, or unhealthy patterns. Stable societies can maintain position longer, but rising systemic issues eventually force a shake-up that empowers low-tier and solo players.

The Sun Has a Heat Meter

SUN_HEAT (0.0 → 1.0)

This metric rises based on measurable conditions and falls when stability returns. It is not punishment—it is neutral aggregation of universe health signals.

What Drives Sun Heating (Neutral Definitions)

Instability Signals (I):
	•	Rapid inflation or deflation spikes
	•	Repeated resource shortages
	•	High governance churn (coups, no-confidence votes)
	•	Enforcement escalations (warnings, detentions)
	•	Sabotage incidents (proven)
	•	Supply route interruptions

Cooperation Collapse Signals (C):
	•	Sharp drop in cooperative play participation (escorts, convoys, fleet operations)
	•	Increased solo reliance as percentage of total activity
	•	Reduced “helping behaviors” (repairs, escorts, trade assists) versus baseline

Activity Imbalance Signals (A):
	•	One or more factions fall below minimum activity parity for extended periods
	•	Extreme faction population concentration starving other factions of participation

Heat Gain Model

Each hour (or each epoch tick):

SUN_HEAT += (wI × I_norm) + (wC × C_norm) + (wA × A_norm) - (wS × StabilityRecovery)

With:
	•	Smoothing (rolling time windows)
	•	Hysteresis (prevents rapid oscillation)
	•	Hard clamp [0.0, 1.0]

Result: Storms might occur within weeks during chaos, or not occur for 6-12 months if stability persists.

Warning System: Sun Heating Alerts

When SUN_HEAT crosses thresholds, all faction governments and leaders receive alerts:
	•	0.60: “Solar instability rising.”
	•	0.75: “Solar heating accelerating.”
	•	0.90: “Critical: coronal storm probability imminent.”

Alerts do not explain exact causes. Leaders see:
	•	Sun heating status
	•	Probability bands
	•	Estimated time window

Players learn culturally: sustained conflict, instability, and imbalance correlate with storms.

Trigger Condition

A COMMS FALL Solar Storm triggers when:
	•	SUN_HEAT ≥ 0.95 for a sustained lock period (6-18 hours)

After storm ends:
	•	SUN_HEAT drops sharply (pressure release)
	•	Cooldown prevents immediate retrigger (minimum quiet period enforced)

⸻

COMMS FALL Storm Event

What Breaks During Storm
	•	Long-range communications degrade or fail
	•	Faction-wide coordination tools restricted
	•	Remote governance actions limited to local node range
	•	Market aggregation becomes local and fragmented

Who Keeps Communications

Storm communications use a power signature rule:

Low-power, low-tier comm rigs remain operational.
High-power, high-range, high-tech comm arrays are disrupted.

This naturally benefits:
	•	Small ships
	•	Low-level communication modules
	•	Solo ships using tightbeam local relays
	•	Starter-tier support systems

It’s not moral judgment. It’s signal physics.

Conflict Law Shifts

Storm shifts many areas to “open conflict law,” but:
	•	Starter protections remain active
	•	Repeated kill farming yields diminishing returns plus heat accumulation
	•	Server escalates bounties and enforcement response for pure farming behavior

The Safe Zone: Storm Shadow Corridor

Exactly one safe corridor exists during storms:
	•	Hard “no damage” enforcement
	•	No AoE effects, mines, or ramming
	•	Limited size forces movement and negotiation

Storm Approach Vector

The storm’s closest approach vector biases toward whichever region contributed the highest instability index, cooperation collapse, or activity imbalance in the current heat cycle.

Rebellion Window Amplifier

During storms:
	•	Rebellion initiation becomes easier (slightly lower thresholds)
	•	Defections become more achievable through physical travel
	•	Spy and black market activity increases due to verification system collapse

PSI remains fully private and safe.

But when a region collapses—through mass griefing, endless war, market manipulation, station lockdowns, or simply player absence—the solar system does not “pause.” It adapts.

That adaptation is the Sovereignty Law: empty space gets governed. If players abandon a sector, the universe fills the vacuum with an NPC/AI sovereign force—custodian fleets, interim station commanders, automated trade convoys, and security patrols—so the world remains alive. If players remain present but destabilize the ecosystem, that same force escalates into emergency governance: taxes get capped, access gets restricted, chokepoints get quarantined, and key stations can be reclaimed by the custodians. This is not a punishment mechanic. It is a systemic immune response. The universe refuses to be permanently broken.

Crucially, the AI sovereign is not an “always-on enemy faction.” It is a stabilizer with transparent thresholds. Players can see the region’s Stability Index, Chaos signals, and the current AI Control Stage. The system escalates gradually—observation, containment, martial governance, reclamation, quarantine—so it feels like story and consequence, not developer cheating. And it de-escalates the same way: as players restore order, the custodians pull back.

Reclaiming control is not a switch; it is a campaign. The game tracks Reclaim Progress in each region and unlocks authority in finite seats—Governor, Station Commander, Security Chief, Trade Director—only when humans prove they can run the system without collapsing it again. These seats are not granted because someone is the strongest or richest. They are earned through reconstruction. Space You Land measures Rebuild Credit: verified actions that restore function after damage—repairing stations, reopening trade routes, escorting convoys, delivering scarce supplies, stabilizing elections, and rebuilding infrastructure chains. Rebuild Credit is region-scoped, meaning a player cannot grind somewhere safe and claim leadership somewhere broken. They must rebuild where the damage happened.

This design flips typical sandbox power dynamics. Solo players—the grinders, crafters, haulers, and bridge-traders who move between factions—become the quiet backbone of recovery. They are often the first to restore supply, the first to repair systems, the first to connect enemies through necessity. In Space You Land, that matters. High Rebuild Credit increases eligibility for leadership seats and becomes the legitimacy edge in contested governance. If multiple candidates qualify, the region holds an election; if it’s close, reconstruction breaks the tie. If it remains equal, the system resolves it cleanly—coverage of rebuild categories, then a randomized roll, then a runoff. The message is simple: if two people want power, the one who rebuilt more has the stronger claim.

At the same time, the system remains sharp against bad actors without becoming petty. Each player and group accrues Heat—an actor risk signature based on patterns of harm: repeated low-value kills, blockade behavior, treaty violations, market manipulation, and exploit flags. Heat drives targeted NPC behavior: bounty hunters that track offenders across systems, undercover agents that operate in stations like normal residents, patrols that focus on specific groups, and docking denials that force high-heat players to live on the edge. This adds a constant layer of “the world is watching,” even when no players are nearby. It also makes space feel alive: not every encounter is another player; sometimes it’s the universe responding.

The result is a sandbox that can’t be permanently hijacked by griefers and can’t die when populations shift. Players can start wars, crash markets, raid lanes, and topple governments—but if they push too far, they risk losing the privilege of governing at all. When custodians take a region, enemies often become allies out of necessity. Pirates end up flying escort. Solo traders become diplomats by default. The universe forces cooperation not through moral lectures, but through environmental pressure: stabilize the system together, or live under machine governance until it’s rebuilt.

Space You Land is not a game where power is owned. It is a game where power is leased—by the universe—to whoever proves they can maintain it.
