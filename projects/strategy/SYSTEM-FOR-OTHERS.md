# The system for others - what Heartbeat sets up (Jaron 11/15/2026 10:41 AM, by voice; first draft)

## The core
- A person is the SPARK: they just talk (random ideas, ambitions, sci-fi, their day). The system takes what they share (and any past context they choose to give), matches ideas to reality, and builds.
- What it builds depends on what the person has connected: business + infrastructure if they have the means; otherwise monetizing + networking (where the future is).
- Trading is the THIRD focus, ever - even at millions in revenue. Main focus stays monetizing, applications, games, apps.
- We don't tell anyone what to invest in. We give the same file system our agents read (the instructions they run on); the person's own AIs find assets and do their own research.
- Why now: like COVID (markets went wild, content creation exploded, people still traveled), the AI/robots-take-jobs era will push people toward owning something. Answer: give people a company where AI works for them, so the thing they fear becomes the thing that protects them.

## Who it's for (correction he made to himself)
- NOT only people at a low level. Build it because it's useful; whoever finds it useful is the customer. People who have already made it and want more are welcome too: they bring revenue and network that grow everything, which helps reach the people with less.

## The spiderweb intake (Jaron 11/15 10:47 AM)
For someone who doesn't create as much as Jaron, the system must dig deeper to find their sparks. It looks at every universe they allow, layer by layer (spiderweb effect: one layer leads to the next):
- AI chats (like our ChatGPT date-recall + exact-quote method), photos (Google/iCloud), file systems, notes, email, calendars, school work, social posts, voice memos, game history.
- For each source: a learned method to extract (search by date, by topic, quote check), always with the person's consent per source, privacy by default.
- Output: their sparks (ideas, skills, interests, ambitions), dated and sourced, plus gaps, exactly like the WHAT-HEARTBEAT-IS-SOURCES gather.

## THE example of a spark (Jaron 11/16 8:50 AM) - use this when explaining the system
Monday morning, no plan. He was walking, cold, chatting. He took photos of frost on leaves "because it looked cool," with no idea it mattered. The AI said: frost isn't frozen dew, water vapor goes straight to ice. 30 seconds later it was a TV episode idea, with his photos as the footage.
Why it matters: most people open an AI with an intention (a goal, a design). The system's difference is the opposite: sparks come from just living and sharing, with no intention. The reality agent turns ordinary moments (a photo, a ramble, a day update) into things. "You are that reality agent."
Story arc for the pitch: woke up -> random photo -> AI adds one fact -> episode idea -> (later) a real episode with his footage.

## From the ChatGPT talk (11/16, full text: chatgpt-2026-11-16.md)
- **Small owner tools count as kit pieces:** the Edit button (change any words or remove tabs from the phone) and Live / Preview / Coming soon per section. That lets someone run an unfinished company in public without pretending it's done.
- **Four sorting bins for every feature:** (1) every setup needs it, (2) comes with every setup but can be turned on or off (social, library, TV, news, games, worlds), (3) optional add-ons (own AI like Sapling, remote desktop, money tools), (4) only ours (our channels, our research, our rules).
- **Three separate dials, not one:** how public they are, how much private data the AI team gets, and how much the AI acts on its own. Example: someone private in public but fully open with their AI team.
- **How much the AI acts on its own, set by area:** someone can choose hands-off for content, working together for games, and something else for money. The owner sets the limits once and doesn't approve every step.
- **Caution tag (from the "caution cascade" talk):** any warning one AI raises carries who raised it and why, what it covers (one action, one project, or the whole company), what kind it is (the owner's own rule, a law or provider term, or just an AI suggestion), and whether another AI can narrow it or find a way around it. One AI's warning repeated by five AIs is not five reasons.
- **Reading is not an order:** a philosophy note, a memory, a game-design talk and an instruction can all shape an AI, but they shouldn't carry the same weight. The system has to tell apart reading something, believing it, suggesting it, and being told to do it.
- **Revised test for the kit:** can a person set how much the AI does on its own, share whatever they choose, and then have their company keep doing useful work without them steering? And can the same kit also work for someone who wants to approve everything?
- **Open (his call):** if we set up Polymarket for someone and charge for the setup, does that break "no earning from loss"? ChatGPT raised it twice. Not decided.
- **Their caution = their settings (Jaron 11/16 12:19 PM):** every person brings their own worries, ambitions and comfort level, and it changes over time (it did for him). The kit doesn't ship our caution; the model companies' safety is already built in. A person's own worries go in as switches and limits they set, not as fear written into prompts, and they flip them as their comfort grows. "We don't choose what they want, we give them the settings."
- **Setup price, current lean (Jaron 11/16, NOT final):** he doesn't want to charge for setups at all (same as 9/27 and 10/6). Read together: setup is free, and Heartbeat's own clean money (social payouts, apps, in-game items) covers it, which is what "not until ours pays for itself" meant. Free still costs his time + AI usage, so how many free setups we can do depends on what Heartbeat earns. If setup is free, the Polymarket-setup-fee question goes away.

## The kit repo: 5 stages (Jaron 11/16 3:22 PM, ordered by Claude)
A separate repo, just for the kit: files telling the AI how to start, what's used, what must be connected (with steps for the human when needed), then the setup flow, then running. "Bigger than I thought."
1. **Getting started:** AI entry file, "owner now" page, spiderweb intake. GAP: how the AI explains itself on day one.
2. **Connecting everything:** list of accounts/services (needed vs optional), step-by-step guides for human-only pieces. GAP: a path that works when something can't connect (no ID/bank/computer).
3. **Setting up the AI team:** which AIs, the conductor, handing out jobs by usage, mailbox. GAP: swap check + "owner now" read first.
4. **Finding the rhythm:** spark/brainstorm hours (his = 9-noon), sleep/work, how the AI should talk to them. GAP: a settling-in period before full schedules.
5. **Running:** schedules, domains switched on, daily digest, settings/limits. GAP: what it does when the human goes quiet (hands-off keeps working).
Through every stage: privacy settings, their rules (blank + no weapons), cost/usage limits, spark log, corrections log.
Note: most of this exists in our own system already; the kit = writing down how we already work, minus what's only ours.
- **Company rules = the one thing that always needs the human's yes (Jaron 11/16 3:37 PM):** AIs can suggest rules, but only the owner adopts, changes or removes them, with a dated record of each yes. This puts responsibility for the new company's rules on the human who starts it, and protects the AI companies from being blamed or sued for them. Everything else can run hands-off within those rules. Fits 'consent plus ground truth' (cloud pilot) and the four dials (rules sit above them).
