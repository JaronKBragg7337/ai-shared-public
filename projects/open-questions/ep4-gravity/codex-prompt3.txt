[s2] First, the mismatch. In nineteen thirty-three, Fritz Zwicky found that galaxies in the Coma Cluster move faster than their visible mass can explain. In nineteen seventy, Vera Rubin and Kent Ford measured gas motions in the Andromeda galaxy. Later work, with Norbert Thonnard, extended rotation measurements to many spiral galaxies. Far from the center, speeds often stay roughly flat, instead of falling as the visible mass predicts under Newtonian gravity.
[s3] The first explanation is dark matter: extra mass that gives off no light, forming a halo around each galaxy. Its gravity would keep outer stars moving fast. Dark matter is not one observation. It is a model, called lambda C D M, that has to fit many observations at once. But no dark matter particle has been confirmed. Underground experiments such as Lux Zeppelin have searched for years and reported no confirmed detection. In twenty twenty-six, Lux Zeppelin reported one unexplained candidate event, at about two point six sigma. That is well short of a discovery, and it could be background.
[s4] The second explanation is to change gravity. In nineteen eighty-three, Mordehai Milgrom proposed Mond, modified Newtonian dynamics. Below a very small acceleration, gravity would be stronger than Newton's law says. Using one acceleration constant and the visible matter, Mond reproduces the rotation of many galaxies, including a tight relation between the acceleration galaxies show and the acceleration expected from their visible matter, reported by Stacy McGaugh and colleagues in twenty sixteen. Both Mond and dark matter models have been used to account for that relation, with different explanations, and researchers disagree over which does it better.
[s5] Mond began as a rule, not a full theory of gravity. Later versions tried to be one: Jacob Bekenstein's in two thousand four, and a relativistic theory by Constantinos Skordis and Tom Zlosnik in twenty twenty-one. But galaxy clusters still show a large mass discrepancy under Mond, one that Mond alone has not resolved.
[s6] The best-known test is the Bullet Cluster, two galaxy clusters that collided. Hot gas, which holds most of the ordinary matter, was slowed by the collision and glows in X-rays. In two thousand six, Douglas Clowe and colleagues used gravitational lensing to map the mass, and found most of it apart from the gas, near the galaxies. That is strong evidence for mass that does not follow the gas, and it is hard for gravity-only changes to explain. Mond supporters note that clusters show a remaining discrepancy even in Mond, so those models add some unseen matter, and researchers disagree on what that implies.
[s7] The cosmic microwave background, the oldest light we can see, has a pattern of hot and cold spots. In twenty eighteen, the Planck team reported that standard cosmology with dark matter fits that pattern very precisely, with about five times more dark matter than ordinary matter. Gravity-only alternatives have found it hard to match, though its authors report that the twenty twenty-one relativistic theory agrees with the pattern on large scales. Whether it fits all the data is open. Dark energy, a separate puzzle, is part of the standard model and is not covered here.
[s8] Now a newer test: wide binaries. These are pairs of stars thousands of times farther apart than the Earth and the Sun, orbiting slowly. Their pull on each other is so weak that Mond predicts, at the widest separations, faster orbits than Newtonian gravity says, by an amount that depends on the model. The Gaia space telescope has measured many such pairs.
[s9] In twenty twenty-three, Kyu-Hyun Chae reported an apparent speed excess in Gaia wide binaries, consistent with Mond. In twenty twenty-four, Indranil Banik and colleagues analyzed Gaia pairs too, and found no such effect, favoring Newtonian gravity. The analyses differ in how pairs are selected, how hidden third stars are handled, and how orbits are modeled. A twenty twenty-five paper by Saad and Ting added radial-velocity measurements and reports tension with Mond in its sample. Researchers still disagree. This is open.

Established: visible matter alone cannot explain these motions under standard gravity, and the dark matter model fits many observations, including the microwave background and cluster collisions. A Mond-style rule also matches many galaxy rotation curves. Not established: what dark matter is, whether any modified gravity can fit galaxies, clusters and the microwave background together, and whether wide binaries depart from Newtonian gravity. Scientists disagree on each.
