UNSURE (s1) — “Stars in galaxies move faster than the matter we can see should allow.” This leaves out the key condition: the mismatch is relative to the motions predicted by standard gravity from visible matter alone. “Stars” also narrows a discrepancy observed through several kinds of measurements. Suggested correction: “In many galaxies, observed motions are faster than standard gravity predicts from visible matter alone.” [Rubin & Ford 1970](https://doi.org/10.1086/150317), [Rubin, Ford & Thonnard 1980](https://doi.org/10.1086/158003)

UNSURE (s2) — “Starting in nineteen seventy, Vera Rubin and Kent Ford measured how fast stars and gas orbit in spiral galaxies.” Their 1970 paper studied ionized gas in Andromeda (M31); the broader spiral-galaxy rotation-curve work came later. As written, “starting in 1970” can make the broad survey sound like the 1970 study’s scope. Suggested correction: “In 1970, Rubin and Ford measured gas motions in Andromeda; later work extended rotation-curve measurements to many spiral galaxies.” [Rubin & Ford 1970](https://doi.org/10.1086/150317), [Rubin, Ford & Thonnard 1980](https://doi.org/10.1086/158003)

UNSURE (s2) — “Far from the center, speeds stay roughly flat” is broadly right for many measured rotation curves, but it should not sound like a result established by the 1970 Andromeda paper alone. Attribute the broad flat-curve pattern to later, extended measurements. [Rubin, Ford & Thonnard 1980](https://doi.org/10.1086/158003)

UNSURE (s4) — “a tight link between visible mass and orbital speed reported by Stacy McGaugh and colleagues in twenty sixteen” is imprecise. McGaugh, Lelli and Schombert’s 2016 paper reported the **radial acceleration relation**: observed galaxy acceleration is tightly related to the acceleration expected from the distribution of baryonic matter. It is related to rotation speed, but is not simply a link between total visible mass and orbital speed. Suggested correction: “a tight relation between observed acceleration and the acceleration expected from visible matter.” [McGaugh, Lelli & Schombert 2016](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.117.201101)

UNSURE (s4) — “Dark matter models can also reproduce that link, but researchers disagree on how naturally” is an interpretive comparison without a clear measure of “naturally.” It risks making the competing accounts sound equivalent on this relation without explaining that the comparison depends on galaxy-formation modeling and assumptions about baryonic matter. Either specify what “naturally” means or say that both MOND and ΛCDM have been used to account for the relation, with differing explanations. [McGaugh, Lelli & Schombert 2016](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.117.201101)

UNSURE (s5) — “MOND has trouble with galaxy clusters, which still seem to need extra mass even under MOND” is basically fair, but “extra mass” should be clearly described as a remaining mass discrepancy inferred within MOND, not as established evidence for a particular unseen component. Suggested correction: “MOND reduces the mass discrepancy in galaxies, but clusters still show a substantial discrepancy that MOND alone has not resolved.” [Clowe et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1086/508162)

UNSURE (s6) — “That is what dark matter predicts, and it is hard for gravity-only changes to explain” fairly conveys why the Bullet Cluster is important, but risks implying it rules out modified gravity generally. The lensing mass peaks are offset from the dominant baryonic component, the hot gas; this is strong evidence for mass not following that gas distribution. MOND explanations generally retain a need for some additional unseen mass in clusters, so distinguish “modified gravity alone” from MOND models that include residual unseen matter. [Clowe et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1086/508162)

UNSURE (s7) — “the twenty twenty-one relativistic theory reproduces much of it” understates and blurs what Skordis and Złośnik claimed. Their 2021 paper says their theory agrees with observed CMB and matter-power spectra on **linear cosmological scales**. That is a result within their model, not a demonstration that it fits all cosmological observations. Suggested correction: “The authors showed agreement with key CMB and matter-power-spectrum observations on linear scales; the theory’s broader cosmological adequacy remains open.” [Skordis & Złośnik 2021](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.127.161302)

UNSURE (s8) — “MOND predicts they should orbit somewhat faster” is a reasonable simplification, but the size of the predicted effect depends on separation and the model, including the Galactic external-field effect. For example, Banik et al. give an approximately 20% excess in the asymptotic large-separation limit they study. Consider saying “some MOND models predict a modest speed excess at the widest separations.” [Banik et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/527.3.4573)

UNSURE (s9) — “Chae reported that Gaia wide binaries do move faster, matching MOND” turns a contested paper’s result into a fact in the narration. Chae reported an apparent excess consistent with MOND; Banik et al. disputed its interpretation and reported that their own analysis favored Newtonian gravity. Suggested correction: “Chae reported an apparent speed excess matching MOND; Banik and colleagues’ analysis found no such effect.” [Chae 2023](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/ace101), [Banik et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/527.3.4573)

UNSURE (s9) — “Researchers point to how pairs are selected, to hidden third stars that can fake extra speed, and to how orbits are modeled” is a plausible summary, but risks presenting those as an agreed explanation for the disagreement. The analyses differ in sample selection, uncertainty handling and population modeling; hidden companions are one modeling concern, not a settled cause of the conflicting findings. [Banik et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/527.3.4573), [Saad & Ting, arXiv:2512.19652](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.19652)

UNSURE (s9) — “A late twenty twenty-five paper still describes the results as conflicting” should identify this as a preprint submitted on 22 December 2025 and revised in 2026; its current journal reference is *The Open Journal of Astrophysics*, volume 9 (2026). It reports tension with MOND from its own radial-velocity analysis, rather than simply settling the earlier Gaia disagreement. Suggested correction: “A preprint submitted in late 2025 and revised in 2026 adds another result, finding tension with MOND in its sample.” [Saad & Ting, arXiv:2512.19652](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.19652)

UNSURE (s10) — “Gaia’s next data release, planned for December twenty twenty-six” is supported by ESA’s current plan. Keep “planned” or “expected”: ESA lists DR4 as expected in December 2026, and release dates can change. [ESA Gaia mission page](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Gaia)

UNSURE (s7) — “Dark energy is a separate puzzle” is too loose in this context. Dark energy is a distinct question from the dark-matter-versus-modified-gravity comparison, but ΛCDM includes a cosmological constant, Λ, as its dark-energy component. Suggested correction: “Dark energy is a separate topic, included in the standard ΛCDM model, and is not covered here.” [Planck 2018 results, VI](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.06209)

UNSURE (s11) — “whether any modified gravity can fit clusters and the microwave background” implies that no modified-gravity theory has shown a CMB fit, which conflicts with the 2021 Skordis–Złośnik paper’s stated linear-scale results. The genuinely open issue is whether a theory can give a comprehensive fit across the relevant cosmological and cluster observations while preserving MOND’s galaxy-scale successes. [Skordis & Złośnik 2021](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.127.161302)

**Verdict:** The script is broadly fair and gets the main historical references and scientific debate right. Revise the precision issues around Rubin and Ford, the radial acceleration relation, the interpretation of MOND’s cluster discrepancy and Bullet Cluster, and the scope of the 2021 CMB result. Its main balance risk is presenting contested wide-binary findings and interpretive explanations too definitively.
