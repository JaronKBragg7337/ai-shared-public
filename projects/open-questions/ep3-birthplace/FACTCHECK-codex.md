WRONG — [s7] “Until two thousand five, no fossil chimpanzee had been found at all.” The Kapthurin specimens were discovered in 2004 and reported in 2005; say “reported” rather than “found.” [McBrearty & Jablonski, *Nature*](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature04008)

UNSURE — [s7] “experts expecting an Asian or European origin doubted it for years.” The Taung Child was widely rejected, but this makes the reason too simple and treats “Asian or European” as a single expectation. Scientific objections also concerned the fossil’s anatomy, its juvenile status, and its geological context. [Biology Letters review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11267397/)

UNSURE — [s12] “the Rift preserves and dates fossils unusually well” as an established conclusion. Rift deposits and volcanic ash have made many East African sites exceptionally useful, but “unusually well” is a broad comparison without a defined basis; the fossil record also reflects where researchers have searched. Keep this as a qualified explanation, not an established general fact. [Smithsonian on Olduvai OH 5](https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/human-fossils/fossils/oh-5), [McBrearty & Jablonski, *Nature*](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature04008)

UNSURE — [s2] “Early members of our own species appear at Jebel Irhoud.” Hublin et al. described the fossils as documenting early stages of the *Homo sapiens* clade; calling them members of *Homo sapiens* without qualification makes a debated taxonomic interpretation sound settled. [Hublin et al., *Nature*](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature22336)
