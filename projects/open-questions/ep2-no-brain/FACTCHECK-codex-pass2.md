## Problems

- **WRONG — [s6]** “When a slime mold used to the substance fused with one that was not…” The transfer study used **salt (NaCl)** as the repellent; s5 describes the separate quinine/caffeine habituation study. **Correction:** Name salt here, or say “a repellent.” [Vogel & Dussutour, 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5204175/)

- **UNSURE — [s9]** “This result did not hold up.” Markel’s 2020 attempt failed to find the effect, but the original authors argued that Markel’s setup was unsuitable; Markel published a response disputing that critique. Calling the result simply one that “did not hold up” can make the replication dispute sound settled. **Correction:** Say that the replication attempt did not find the effect, and briefly note the published disagreement over whether it was a valid replication. “So the pea association result is not established” is a fair summary. [Markel, 2020](https://elifesciences.org/articles/57614); [Gagliano et al.’s comment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7556858/); [Markel’s response](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7556859/)

- **UNSURE — [s10]** “It tried a sequence of responses.” *Tried* can imply intention or deliberate problem-solving; the study reports a sequence of observed avoidance responses. **Correction:** “It showed a sequence of responses.” Dexter et al. describe the hierarchy as distinct from habituation or conditioning, so the script’s learning conclusion is appropriately cautious. [Dexter et al., 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0960982219314319)

- **UNSURE — [s11]** “Established: in the slime mold habituation experiments, behavior changed because of earlier experience. That fits the behavioral definition of learning.” The study reports response decline, recovery after the stimulus was withheld, and stimulus specificity; calling this learning follows the authors’ interpretation and the script’s stated behavioral definition. “Established” is stronger than the evidence warrants if viewers take it to mean that the learning interpretation is undisputed. **Correction:** “The slime mold study reported behavior changes consistent with habituation, which fits this behavioral definition of learning.” [Boisseau et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4855389/)

- **UNSURE — [s12]** “It shows that learning, as defined by behavior, may not need [a brain].” This conclusion generalizes from one slime mold habituation study whose learning interpretation is the authors’ framing; the definition alone does not establish that the behavior is learning. **Correction:** “It offers evidence that habituation, under this behavioral definition of learning, may not require a brain.” [Boisseau et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4855389/)

## Saigusa 2008

**CONFIRMED.** The description in [s7] matches the paper: *Physarum* received three unfavorable cold, dry pulses at regular intervals, slowed during them, and later showed spontaneous slowdowns at expected pulse times under favorable conditions. “Anticipating” is accurately attributed to the authors’ interpretation, and the script distinguishes it from the measured slowdown. [Saigusa et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18232821/)
