## Problems and qualifications

**UNSURE — [s2]** “Researchers who study this define learning by behavior only.” That presents one operational definition as universal. The cited studies use behavior change due to experience as a working definition, but definitions and criteria vary. “Scientists disagree about” awareness is also too broad without specifying which scientists or organisms. Source: [Boisseau et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2016.0446); [Markel (2020)](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.57614).

**UNSURE — [s5]** “The researchers took that pattern as habituation, not tiredness.” The researchers interpreted the results as habituation and used stimulus specificity to distinguish it from motor fatigue or sensory adaptation. “Not tiredness” sounds more conclusive than the study’s evidence warrants; consider saying the pattern *supported their interpretation* of habituation. Source: [Boisseau et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2016.0446).

**UNSURE — [s6]** “The learned behavior had transferred through the fusion.” That accurately reports the authors’ conclusion, but could sound as if they identified what physically transferred. The study found a behavioral response transferred after fusion; it did not establish the cellular mechanism. The next sentence helps clarify this. Source: [Vogel & Dussutour (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2016.2382).

**UNSURE — [s8]** “Stopped folding” and “still ignored the drops weeks later” overstate the Mimosa result. The study measured reduced leaf closure after repeated 15 cm drops, and reported that the response persisted at a later test about a month afterward; it did not show that the plants simply stopped folding altogether. The dispute is real: a later critique argued that fatigue could explain much of the data and that habituation remained uncertain. Source: [Gagliano et al. (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00442-013-2873-7); [Biegler (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00442-017-4012-3).

**WRONG — [s10]** “Jennings watched the trumpet-shaped cell *Stentor*” attributes the classic account to the right scientist and organism, but the trumpet-shaped species is *Stentor roeseli* (also written *S. roeselii*), and the later paper’s title and abstract identify the hierarchy as responses to **repeated** stimulation. The narration’s “because it is a change during one event” is therefore too simple: it describes a sequence during ongoing stimulation, but across repeated pulses. Dexter et al. confirmed the hierarchy, which they distinguished from habituation or conditioning. Source: [Jennings (1902)](https://doi.org/10.1152/ajplegacy.1902.8.1.23); [Dexter et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2019.10.059).

**UNSURE — [s11]** “Established: in these experiments, organisms without brains changed their behavior because of earlier experience. That is learning by the behavioral definition.” This overgeneralizes across the episode’s examples: the pea association result failed to replicate, Mimosa habituation is disputed, the maze and rail-network studies are not learning tests, and the Stentor sequence is not generally treated as habituation or conditioning. Narrow this to the specific Physarum habituation evidence, and label the Mimosa evidence disputed. Sources: [Boisseau et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2016.0446); [Biegler (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00442-017-4012-3); [Markel (2020)](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.57614); [Dexter et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2019.10.059).

**UNSURE — [s11]** “People argue about what counts as learning in AI too, and it isn’t settled.” This is vague and outside the cited research: it does not say which AI learning question is disputed or identify a source. Specify the dispute or remove the line.

## Saigusa 2008

**CONFIRMED — [s7]** The claim is accurate as worded: Saigusa and colleagues reported that *Physarum* slowed in response to regularly spaced unfavorable pulses, then slowed again around the time the next pulse would have occurred after the pulses ended. They described this as anticipation. The narration appropriately identifies that as the authors’ interpretation of measured behavior, rather than evidence of conscious prediction. Source: [Saigusa et al. (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.100.018101).

## What is fine

- **[s1]** *Physarum polycephalum* is a giant single cell in its plasmodial stage and has no nervous system. The opening claim about behavior changing with prior experience is consistent with the habituation study, though it should not imply that the maze experiment demonstrated learning. Source: [Nakagaki et al. (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1038/35035159); [Boisseau et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2016.0446).
- **[s3]** The Nature attribution, authors and year are correct. The plasmodium spread through the maze, then withdrew from dead ends and retained a shortest route between the food sources. Source: [Nakagaki et al. (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1038/35035159).
- **[s4]** Tero et al., Science, 2010 is correct. They compared *Physarum* networks with Tokyo-area rail infrastructure using cost, transport efficiency and fault tolerance measures. “Did not plan a railway” is an appropriate clarification that avoids attributing intention. Source: [Tero et al. (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1177894).
- **[s5]** The authors, journal, year, quinine/caffeine stimuli, reduced hesitation with repeated exposure, recovery after rest, and response to a different substance are broadly accurate. Source: [Boisseau et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2016.0446).
- **[s6]** Vogel and Dussutour’s 2016 study reported transfer of the adaptive response after fusion. Source: [Vogel & Dussutour (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2016.2382).
- **[s9]** Gagliano et al. reported pea seedlings growing toward a fan previously paired with light. Markel’s 2020 study found no evidence of associative learning, so “not established” is a fair summary of that dispute. Source: [Gagliano et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep38427); [Markel (2020)](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.57614).
- **[s10]** The Jennings attribution and 1902 date are correct; Dexter, Prabakaran and Gunawardena’s 2019 Current Biology paper did report a hierarchy of avoidance behaviors in *Stentor roeseli*. Source: [Jennings (1902)](https://doi.org/10.1152/ajplegacy.1902.8.1.23); [Dexter et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2019.10.059).
- **[s12]** “Learning, as defined by behavior, may not need” a brain is cautiously framed as an implication. The requests for independent repeats, clearer tests of cellular storage and agreement about learning criteria are reasonable.
- **[s13]** The closing production and website statements do not make claims about the science checked here.
