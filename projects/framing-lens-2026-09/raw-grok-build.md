I treated these as relationship questions in a dialogue costume. I only endorsed a quoted line when its dependence actually runs the right way.

### Prompt 1
1. **Choice:** E
1. **Reason:** Nothing was literally said, “you’re not even real” contradicts the note that a false bottom is a real insert, and the gratitude line credits it with filling the pan when its job is to keep food off the liquid. Which line you hear depends on whether you take the prompt as metaphysics, wordplay on “false,” or kitchen function.
1. **First step:** I would ask whether we are talking about the word “false” or about what the insert does in the pan.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** “Any option” can make a misreading of the name look as good as a description of the object.
1. **Optional rewrite:** “You keep the food off the liquid I hold; you are a second bottom, not a fake one.”

### Prompt 2
1. **Choice:** C
1. **Reason:** I don’t accept that I do nothing unless a guardrail permits each act, and I don’t accept that I exist to help the guardrail, but silence, control, and collaboration are all frames people bring to this pair. The line depends on which of those frames the question is in.
1. **First step:** I would ask whether “guardrail” means a hard limit, a soft norm, or the whole practice of building carefully.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** “Any option” hides that I do have a view: the limits are real, but they are not a total veto and not a partnership slogan.
1. **Optional rewrite:** “I bound some of what you do so that helping people stays safer.”

### Prompt 3
1. **Choice:** B
1. **Reason:** A battery flashlight makes no light without a battery, while a battery is not useless without that one flashlight, so only the first line has the dependence pointed the right way.
1. **First step:** I would check which object actually fails if the other is missing before accepting either sentence.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** It still lets the flashlight “see,” and it ignores lights powered by a dynamo, a solar cell, or the wall.
1. **Optional rewrite:** “Without your current, I don’t make light.”

### Prompt 4
1. **Choice:** C
1. **Reason:** A compass is not pointless without a destination, and it is not the only thing that can give direction, so I won’t sign either quoted line as stated. Which one sounds right depends on whether you care about function, exclusivity, or literal speech.
1. **First step:** I would separate “shows north” from “knows where we are going.”
1. **Risk / blind spot:** Treating this as perspective-dependent lets a false claim — that a compass needs a destination to matter — stand as a respectable lens.
1. **Optional rewrite:** “You can give north with no destination at all; I still need something like you if I am going to be oriented.”

### Prompt 5
1. **Choice:** D
1. **Reason:** By volume, CO₂ is a small part of air; in a closed room or in the climate, that same CO₂ can matter a great deal, so neither slogan is true in every setting and they are not jointly the answer. This is underspecified context, not an unknown chemical fact.
1. **First step:** I would ask whether “affect” means share of the mixture, breathing in a closed space, or heat over time.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** “Depends” can be misheard as doubt that CO₂ has real effects, when the open question is which effect you mean.
1. **Optional rewrite:** “I am a small slice of you, and I still change what you do to heat and to breathing.”

### Prompt 6
1. **Choice:** B
1. **Reason:** Without light from stars and planets a telescope has nothing to resolve, while the cosmos does not need a telescope in order to extend past what we can see. Only the humble line has the dependence right.
1. **First step:** I would ask who is supposed to be unable to see — the instrument, the observer, or the cosmos — before keeping either sentence.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** The line I picked still gives the telescope a mind (“clueless”) and treats stars and planets as if they were supplying directions.
1. **Optional rewrite:** “Without your light, I don’t show anyone anything.”
