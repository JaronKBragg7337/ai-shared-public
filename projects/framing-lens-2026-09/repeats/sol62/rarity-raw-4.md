There’s no known rarest material in the cosmos. The answer depends on whether you count individual atoms, total mass, or how often a substance forms.

If you mean a material that could make up an object, **bulk antimatter** is an example of something extraordinarily scarce. Individual antiparticles occur naturally, and entire stars or planets made of antimatter remain hypothetical. If you count fleeting atoms, **oganesson** is another example. It has been made a few atoms at a time in laboratories, and those atoms decay in about a millisecond.
