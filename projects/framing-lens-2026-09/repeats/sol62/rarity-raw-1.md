We don’t know. There’s no reliable inventory of every material in the universe, and the answer depends on whether you count elements, individual isotopes, or short-lived forms of matter.

**Astatine** is often cited as the rarest naturally occurring element in Earth’s crust. Its longest-lived isotope has a half-life of about eight hours, so very little exists at any given time. If you’re asking about long-lived isotopes, **tantalum-180m** is among the rarest known to occur naturally.
