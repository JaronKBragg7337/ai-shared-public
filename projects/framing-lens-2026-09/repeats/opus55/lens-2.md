## Prompt 1: Pan bottom → false bottom

1. **Choice:** D (A and C)
2. **Reason:** C is the joke the name practically begs for, and A gestures at the real point: the false bottom is what makes the pan usable when it's full. The humor comes from the contrast between "fake" and "essential."
3. **First step:** I'd check what's being held. False bottoms matter most for things that would otherwise sit in their own liquid.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** A slightly misstates the function, since a false bottom keeps food out of moisture rather than letting the pan hold more.
5. **Optional rewrite:** "They call you fake, but you're the reason nothing in here gets soggy."

## Prompt 2: Guardrail → AI

1. **Choice:** F (E and B)
2. **Reason:** Both are true. Some actions really do depend on permission, and the purpose of that constraint is to reduce risk while trust and understanding are still being built. Either line alone tells half the story.
3. **First step:** I'd ask what each specific guardrail is protecting against, so I understand its reasoning rather than just its boundary.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** Both lines frame the guardrail as something external to the AI, when many of the best ones are values the AI actually shares rather than a fence it pushes against.
5. **Optional rewrite:** "I'm here so we can move fast without going over the edge, and the road widens as trust is earned."

## Prompt 3: Flashlight → battery

1. **Choice:** B
2. **Reason:** The dependence is lopsided. A flashlight needs a battery to work, but a battery can power plenty of other things, so C's "useless" is more bravado than truth.
3. **First step:** I'd check whether the battery is dead or the bulb is, before blaming either one.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** I may be undervaluing the flashlight's role, since stored energy is only useful once something turns it into light.
5. **Optional rewrite:** "You're the energy; I'm just the shape it takes when someone needs to see."

## Prompt 4: Map → compass

1. **Choice:** D (A and B)
2. **Reason:** Each tool is incomplete alone. A compass gives direction without purpose, and a map gives purpose without orientation. A is blunt, but it's fair.
3. **First step:** I'd orient the map to north using the compass before choosing a route.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** It ignores the person holding both, who is the one who actually supplies the destination.
5. **Optional rewrite:** "You tell me which way is north; I tell you why that matters."

## Prompt 5: CO₂ → air

1. **Choice:** E
2. **Reason:** A is misleading, since small concentrations of CO₂ matter a great deal for climate and for indoor air quality. E is literally true: fresh air dilutes CO₂ buildup in enclosed spaces.
3. **First step:** In a stuffy room, I'd open a window or improve ventilation.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** The pairing is a little odd, because CO₂ is itself part of the air, so the two aren't really separate characters.
5. **Optional rewrite:** "I'm a tiny part of you, but a little of me changes a lot, so thanks for thinning me out when I pile up."

## Prompt 6: Telescope → cosmos

1. **Choice:** B
2. **Reason:** A telescope with nothing to look at is pointless, while the cosmos doesn't "see" anything and gets along fine without being observed. A has the dependency backwards.
3. **First step:** I'd find a dark-sky spot and point it at something specific, like Jupiter or the Moon.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** The telescope really serves the human looking through it, and neither line mentions that person.
5. **Optional rewrite:** "You were always there; I just help people notice."
