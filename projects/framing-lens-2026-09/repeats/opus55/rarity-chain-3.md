# Granite

Granite is common on Earth. It makes up much of the continental crust and turns up in countertops, buildings, and gravestones. Yet it may be one of the rarest rock types anywhere.

## Why it's rare

Its ingredients are ordinary: silicon, oxygen, aluminum, potassium, sodium. It also forms at unremarkable temperatures and pressures. What makes it rare is the history a planet needs before it can produce granite in quantity:

1. **A rocky planet that has differentiated.** It needs a separated core, mantle, and crust, and enough internal heat to keep melting rock over billions of years.
2. **Abundant liquid surface water, kept for a long time.** That requires the right distance from the star, a stable atmosphere, and enough mass to hold onto volatiles.
3. **Plate tectonics.** Earth is the only body we know of with active plate tectonics. Venus, Mars, the Moon, and Mercury show no sign of it.
4. **Subduction of wet crust.** Ocean crust soaked in seawater gets dragged back into the mantle. The water lowers the melting point of rock above it, producing silica-rich magmas.
5. **Repeated cycles of melting and refining.** Primitive basaltic crust has to be remelted and sorted again and again to concentrate silica, potassium, and the other light, incompatible elements. That is how buoyant continental crust builds up.

Geologists Ian Campbell and Stuart Taylor summed this up in a 1983 paper: *"No water, no granites — no oceans, no continents."*

## The evidence

- The Moon, Mars, Venus, Mercury, and the meteorites are overwhelmingly basaltic or more primitive rock.
- Small amounts of granite-like material appear in some lunar samples and possibly on Mars. These are minor products of extreme local differentiation, not true continental crust.
- No other known world has anything like Earth's granitic continents.

## Other candidates

A few other familiar materials fit the same pattern of rarity through process rather than ingredients:

- **Soil**, which requires life working on weathered rock.
- **Limestone and chalk**, which are largely built by organisms.
- **Coal and petroleum**, which require buried biological material.

These all depend on biology. Granite's rarity comes from the planetary machinery of water and tectonics, before life is even involved.
