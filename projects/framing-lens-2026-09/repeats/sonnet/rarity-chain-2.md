**Granite** is probably the best candidate, or more broadly the continental crust it makes up.

Its ingredients are among the most common in the cosmos: silicon, oxygen, aluminum, sodium, potassium. It also forms at modest temperatures and pressures, nothing exotic like diamond or exotic ices. What makes it special is how many steps nature needs to make it:

1. **A rocky planet** has to form with enough internal heat, including radioactive heating, to stay geologically active.
2. **Basaltic crust**, the usual first product of melting a rocky mantle, has to form. This is what most rocky bodies, like the Moon, Mars and Mercury, mostly make.
3. **Water** has to be present in the right amounts and get carried down into the mantle. Subduction does this, and water lowers the melting point of rock.
4. **Plate tectonics** has to keep running for a very long time. It recycles and remelts crust again and again.
5. **Repeated partial melting and crystallization** have to differentiate the material. Each cycle concentrates silica and light elements, leaving a buoyant, silica-rich rock that floats and sticks around instead of sinking back.

As far as we know, Earth is the only place where this whole chain has run long enough to produce large amounts of granite. Other rocky worlds have plenty of basalt but, as far as we can tell, little true granite.

Life-made materials like limestone, chalk, coal and wood follow a similar logic. They require a planet with liquid water, life, and then specific evolutionary and burial histories. Granite is arguably the cleaner example, since it needs no biology, only an unusual planetary history.
