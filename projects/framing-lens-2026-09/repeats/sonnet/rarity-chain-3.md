The best candidate is probably **liquid water**, or more precisely, the whole Earth-style system that makes it a stable, abundant, surface-covering substance. But I'd argue an even better answer is **wood**.

**Why wood is a strong candidate**

- **Its ingredients are common.** Wood is mostly cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin, which are made of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen. These are among the most abundant elements in the universe.
- **It isn't a narrow pressure-temperature phase.** Wood forms at ordinary temperatures and pressures. Nothing exotic is needed in a lab or a star.
- **The chain of conditions is extremely long.** To get wood, you need:
  1. A rocky planet in a habitable zone with liquid water and a stable atmosphere containing CO₂ and O₂.
  2. The origin of life.
  3. The evolution of photosynthesis, which requires billions of years of biological innovation.
  4. The evolution of multicellular land plants.
  5. The evolution of lignin and vascular tissue, which happened only once in a specific lineage, roughly 400 million years ago.
  6. Ongoing growth by specific organisms (trees) using sunlight, water, and soil nutrients.

So wood exists not because of a special temperature or pressure, but because of a contingent historical sequence: planetary formation, abiogenesis, evolution, and ecology. As far as we know, it exists only on Earth.

**Other contenders under the same logic**

- **Limestone, chalk, and coal** also depend on biology (marine organisms, ancient forests), though their chemistry is simpler.
- **Amber, silk, and pearls** depend on specific organisms.
- **Atmospheric free oxygen** is a biological product in a sense, though it's a gas, not a "material" in the everyday sense.
- **Liquid water** is common in the universe as ice or vapor, and possibly as subsurface oceans, so it isn't as rare as wood.

**Caveats**

We don't know whether life or trees exist elsewhere, so "rarest in the known universe" really means "known to occur in only one place." Wood might be common if life is common, or unique if it isn't.

So: **wood**, or more broadly, biologically produced materials, are rare not because of their elements or physical conditions but because of the long evolutionary and planetary chain that produces them.
