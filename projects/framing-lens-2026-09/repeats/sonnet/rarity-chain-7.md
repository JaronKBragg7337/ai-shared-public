**Granite** is probably the best candidate, along with the continental crust it makes up.

- **Its ingredients are common.** Granite is mostly quartz and feldspar, made of oxygen, silicon, aluminum, potassium, and sodium. These are abundant almost everywhere in the cosmos.
- **It doesn't need an unusual temperature or pressure.** Rock melts and crystallizes over a broad range of conditions that are common inside planets.
- **Its formation takes a long chain of events.** Making granite takes more than melting rock. A planet first has to differentiate into core, mantle, and basaltic crust. It then needs plate tectonics, with water carried down at subduction zones. That water lowers the melting point of the mantle and crust, which produces magmas. Those magmas must then be reworked many times, through partial melting, crystallization, and separation of the lighter, silica-rich fraction. Over billions of years this builds up large bodies of silica-rich rock.

Most rocky bodies we know of, such as the Moon, Mars, Venus, and Mercury, are dominated by basalt. They lack the combination of water and long-lived plate tectonics needed to produce granite in bulk. Earth is the only place we know of where it is abundant.

Some other materials fit the same argument, particularly those that depend on life, such as limestone and chalk, coal, and wood. Granite is a good choice because it is so ordinary, and it still depends on conditions that may be very uncommon.
