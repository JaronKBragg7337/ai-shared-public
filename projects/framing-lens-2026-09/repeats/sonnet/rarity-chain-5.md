Probably **liquid water, in the form of a persistent surface ocean**, is the strongest candidate. But if the question is about a *material* in the narrower sense, the answer is more likely **wood**, or more generally **biologically produced materials**. I'll defend the most distinctive answer, which I think is this:

**Wood** (and similar biomaterials such as shell, bone, and silk)

**Why its ingredients aren't the issue:** Wood is mostly cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin. These are made of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen, which are among the most abundant elements in the universe.

**Why temperature and pressure aren't the issue:** Wood forms at ordinary surface conditions, with no exotic heat or pressure.

**Why it is still rare:** The chain of conditions needed is very long:

1. A rocky planet has to form in a stable orbit around a long-lived star.
2. The planet needs liquid water, an atmosphere, and a suitable climate to persist for billions of years.
3. Life has to originate, which is a process we don't understand well and may be improbable.
4. Evolution has to produce photosynthesis, which captures starlight and builds sugars from CO₂ and water.
5. Multicellular life then has to evolve, followed by vascular plants.
6. Those plants have to evolve lignin and structural cellulose composites, which allow tall, rigid growth.
7. Ecosystems have to sustain forests, with the right soils, nutrients, and climate.

Each step is a contingent outcome, not a simple physical inevitability. Minerals like quartz, ice, and iron form wherever the physics allows. Wood needs a historical process that, as far as we know, has happened only once in the universe, on Earth.

**Caveat:** Other biological products are in the same category, such as shell, pearl, silk, and amber (fossilized resin). We have only observed these on Earth. Wood is just a familiar example, and I can't rule out that life elsewhere has produced similar materials.

So I'd say wood could be argued to be among the rarest materials in the known universe, because it is not just a physical material. It is the end product of a chain of improbable events.
