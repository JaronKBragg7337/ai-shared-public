It depends on what you mean by "material," but here are the best candidates.

**Rarest naturally occurring element: astatine.** Only about a gram or less exists in Earth's crust at any moment. It is produced by the decay of heavier elements, and its longest-lived isotope has a half-life of about 8 hours, so it vanishes almost as soon as it forms. Nothing suggests it is any more common elsewhere in the cosmos.

**Rarest in a broader sense: antimatter.** The observable universe is overwhelmingly matter. Antimatter turns up only in tiny amounts, in cosmic-ray antiprotons, positrons from radioactive decay, and bits made in particle accelerators. Even all the antimatter ever made by humans would amount to a few nanograms.

**Rarest man-made material: superheavy elements.** Oganesson and its neighbors have been made only a few atoms at a time, and they last milliseconds or less. In terms of total quantity that has ever existed, they rank among the lowest.

**Hypothetical possibilities.** Magnetic monopoles and strange matter (quark matter) might exist, but we have never found them. If they do exist, they could be the rarest of all. If not, they are rarer than rare, and simply absent.

If you want a single answer, it's astatine for natural matter and antimatter for the cosmos as a whole.
