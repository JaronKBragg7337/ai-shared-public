A strong candidate is **natural diamond**, but perhaps an even better one is **fossilized wood / petrified wood**, or more broadly **fossil fuels like crude oil, coal, or natural gas**.

The best answer, though, is probably **fossil fuels** — especially **crude oil**.

Why? Because the atoms involved — carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, etc. — are extremely common throughout the universe. Fossil fuels don’t require exotic elements. They also don’t require an especially narrow temperature or pressure window compared with, say, diamond formation.

What makes them so rare is the **full chain of conditions**:

1. **A planet with abundant liquid water and a stable climate**  
2. **A biosphere producing large amounts of organic matter**  
3. **Rapid burial of that organic matter before it fully decomposes or oxidizes**  
4. **Tectonic and sedimentary processes that cook it at just the right depth over geological time**  
5. **Preservation over hundreds of millions of years without being destroyed**  
6. **A civilization that can extract and use it**

Each step is plausible somewhere in the universe, but the *entire chain* occurring together is extraordinarily unlikely. That makes crude oil, coal, and natural gas potentially among the rarest useful materials in the known universe — not because their ingredients are rare, but because the process that creates them is so contingent.

If you want a single familiar material, **crude oil** is the cleanest answer. It is naturally occurring, familiar, and its rarity comes from the improbable sequence of biological, geological, and temporal conditions required to produce it.
