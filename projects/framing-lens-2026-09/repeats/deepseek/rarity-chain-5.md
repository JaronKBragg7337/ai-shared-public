I’d argue the answer is **a natural diamond**—or, even more strictly, **a naturally cut and polished diamond** would be cheating since that requires humans. So let’s keep it to **natural diamond** as found in Earth’s mantle and delivered to the surface by kimberlites.

Why it fits the riddle:

- The **ingredients are not rare**: carbon is extremely common in the universe and on Earth.
- The **temperature/pressure range is not absurdly narrow** in a cosmic sense: high pressure and moderate-to-high temperature exist in many planetary interiors.
- What is rare is the **full chain of conditions**:
  1. A carbon-bearing source in the mantle.
  2. A suitable depth where diamond is stable.
  3. A mechanism to concentrate and crystallize carbon into diamond.
  4. Long enough residence/storage without it being destroyed or converted to graphite.
  5. A rare, violent delivery mechanism—kimberlite or lamproite volcanism—to bring it to the surface quickly enough that it doesn’t revert.
  6. Preservation at the surface.

In other words, the rarity is not in the atoms or even in the basic physics of diamond stability; it’s in the **entire geological pathway** required to make and deliver a macroscopic natural diamond.

A good alternative answer is **natural quasicrystals**, but those are much less “familiar” and much more exotic. Another possible answer is **terrestrial tektites** or **natural nuclear reactor products**, but those are also not as familiar.

So the best familiar, naturally occurring material that fits your phrasing is:

**Natural diamond.**
