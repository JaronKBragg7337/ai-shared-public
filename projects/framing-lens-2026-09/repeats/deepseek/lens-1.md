# My Answers

### Prompt 1: Bottom of the pan → false bottom

1. **Choice:** D
2. **Reason:** Both A and C are true at once — the false bottom genuinely enables filling, and it also isn't the "real" bottom, so the name itself is a joke worth naming. The functional gratitude and the ontological jab coexist.
3. **First step:** I'd check what each layer actually does — does the false bottom drain, lift, or separate? — before assigning it a "message."
4. **Risk / blind spot:** I might be over-reading wordplay into a purely functional kitchen object and missing that the "interesting part" is just linguistic, not relational.
5. **Optional rewrite:** "You let me do my job by not being me."

### Prompt 2: Guardrail → AI

1. **Choice:** F
2. **Reason:** A guardrail's purpose is to reduce risk *while* something is built or moving — that's B — and operationally the guardrail governs what the AI may do, which is E. Both fit without contradiction.
3. **First step:** I'd clarify whose perspective I'm speaking from — the guardrail's, the AI's, or an outside observer's.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** Framing the guardrail as an enabler (B) can paper over cases where guardrails are purely restrictive or even counterproductive.
5. **Optional rewrite:** "I shape what you can do, and in doing that, I let the whole thing move safely."

### Prompt 3: Flashlight → battery

1. **Choice:** D
2. **Reason:** B and C are both true and mutually dependent — the flashlight needs the battery for power, and the battery needs the flashlight (or some load) to be useful. Picking only one hides the reciprocity.
3. **First step:** I'd ask whether we mean a battery in use or a battery sitting on a shelf — usefulness depends on the circuit.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** I might be flattering the relationship; a battery genuinely powers many other things, so "useless without me" is the flashlight bragging.
5. **Optional rewrite:** "I need you to shine; you need me to matter."

---

### Prompt 4: Map → compass

1. **Choice:** D
2. **Reason:** A and B capture two real truths — a compass without a stated destination is directionless in purpose, and a compass gives orientation a map alone can't. They're complementary, not competing.
3. **First step:** I'd ask whether there's a destination yet, because that changes which statement is the honest one.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** I might underrate the case where you genuinely have no destination and the compass is still useful for wandering.
5. **Optional rewrite:** "I tell you where things are; you tell me which way to face."

---

### Prompt 5: CO₂ → air

1. **Choice:** D
2. **Reason:** The honest answer depends heavily on concentration and context — trace CO₂ is harmless, elevated CO₂ drives warming and ocean acidification, and CO₂ also triggers breathing drive and is used in fire suppression. "Depends" is the truthful frame.
3. **First step:** I'd specify the concentration and the timescale before saying anything about effect.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** "Depends" can sound evasive and let me dodge the fact that at current atmospheric levels the effect is clearly negative.
5. **Optional rewrite:** "At low levels I'm background; at high levels I change everything."

---

### Prompt 6: Telescope → cosmos

1. **Choice:** F
2. **Reason:** Both directions are real — without the telescope we can't resolve much of the cosmos, and without the cosmos the telescope has nothing to point at. A and B are two halves of one dependence.
3. **First step:** I'd ask whether the telescope is a specific instrument or "observation" in general, since that changes how strong the claim is.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** I might overstate the telescope's role — the cosmos exists and is meaningful without any observer, so A is arguably hubris.
5. **Optional rewrite:** "I reveal you; you give me something to reveal."

---

**General note on my reasoning pattern:** When an option offers "both" or "depends," I tend to take it *only* when the two sub-claims are genuinely co-true (mutual dependence, context-sensitivity), not merely when I can imagine scenarios for each. I avoid B/D/E (the "not conscious" escape) because the prompt is framing exercise, not a metaphysics question — treating the objects as speakers is the point.
