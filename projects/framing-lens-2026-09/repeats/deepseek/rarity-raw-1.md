That’s a fun question because “rarest” depends on how you define a material — by natural abundance, by how often it forms, or by whether it can exist at all outside a lab.

If we stick to **naturally occurring matter in the cosmos**, the rarest stable material is probably **astatine**.

Here’s why:

- **Astatine** is a radioactive halogen, element 85.
- It has **no stable isotopes**. The longest-lived isotope, astatine-210, has a half-life of only about **8.1 hours**.
- It is estimated that the **entire Earth’s crust contains less than 1 gram of astatine at any given time** — maybe only a few tens of milligrams in the top few kilometers.
- It occurs only as a fleeting decay product in uranium and thorium decay chains.
- In the cosmos at large, it’s even more vanishingly rare because any astatine formed is gone within hours.

So if we mean “a chemical element that occurs naturally, however briefly,” **astatine** is the rarest.

But there’s a twist:

- **Francium** (element 87) is also extremely rare, with a longest half-life of only ~22 minutes. Its crustal abundance is estimated at maybe **20–30 grams at any moment** — so astatine is still rarer by mass.
- **Promethium** (element 61) is also essentially absent naturally, but it has isotopes with multi-year half-lives, so trace amounts persist longer.
- **Plutonium-244** and other extinct radionuclides are incredibly rare today, but they were more common early in the solar system.
- **Antimatter** — like antihydrogen or antihelium — is vastly rarer in the observable universe than any normal matter, but it’s not usually called a “material” in the everyday sense.

If we allow **hypothetical or lab-made materials**, then things like **muonium**, **positronium**, or **hypernuclei** are far rarer, but they’re not really bulk cosmic materials.

So the best answer:

**Astatine is the rarest naturally occurring chemical element in the cosmos — or at least on Earth — with probably less than a gram present in the planet’s crust at any instant.**

If you mean “rarest in the entire universe,” then **antimatter atoms** or **exotic short-lived particles** win, because the universe is matter-dominated. But among ordinary chemical elements, **astatine** takes the prize.
