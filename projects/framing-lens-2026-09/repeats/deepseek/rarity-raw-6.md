“Rarest” depends on how you define it, but if you mean **naturally occurring material in the observable cosmos**, the strongest answer is usually:

**Antimatter — especially complex antimatter nuclei.**

More specifically:

- **Antihydrogen** has been produced in tiny amounts in labs, but naturally occurring antimatter in the cosmos is extraordinarily rare.
- **Antihelium** and heavier antinuclei are even rarer. A few candidate antihelium events have been detected in cosmic rays, but they are among the rarest things we know of.
- If you allow “theoretically possible but essentially never observed,” then things like **anti-carbon** or **anti-oxygen** nuclei would be vastly rarer still.

Why antimatter wins:

- The universe is overwhelmingly matter.
- Antimatter annihilates on contact with matter.
- Any primordial antimatter would have been destroyed long ago unless isolated.
- Natural production is limited to high-energy processes like cosmic ray collisions or maybe exotic astrophysical sources.

So the best concise answer is:

**Naturally occurring antimatter, particularly complex antinuclei such as antihelium, is the rarest material in the cosmos.**
