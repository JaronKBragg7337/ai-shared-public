# My Answers

### Prompt 1: Bottom of the pan to the false bottom

1. **Choice:** D (A and C)
2. **Reason:** The false bottom is functionally real but named as if fake, so both gratitude ("you let me fill up") and a playful accusation ("you're not even real") can be true at once. The tension between the name and the function is the whole joke.
3. **First step:** Notice that the name is misleading before deciding what to say about the object.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** I might be overreading wordplay where a literal functional reading (B) is the cleaner answer.
5. **Optional rewrite:** "You're called fake, but you're the reason I can hold anything at all."

### Prompt 2: Guardrail to the AI

1. **Choice:** F (E and B)
2. **Reason:** A guardrail both constrains ("you don't act unless I allow it") and enables safe construction ("you help me reduce risk while we build") — those aren't in conflict, they're two faces of the same relationship.
3. **First step:** Clarify whether "guardrail" means a hard constraint or a safety collaborator in this context.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** Choosing both might dodge the sharper question of whether constraint is fundamentally adversarial or supportive.
5. **Optional rewrite:** "I'm not here to stop you — I'm here so you can move without falling."

### Prompt 3: Flashlight to the battery

1. **Choice:** D (B and C)
2. **Reason:** Both are true — the flashlight is inert without the battery, and the battery is useless without something to discharge into. Mutual dependency cuts both ways, and picking only one flatters one side unfairly.
3. **First step:** Ask which direction of dependency the question is really probing.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** Saying "both" may look like refusing to commit rather than recognizing genuine reciprocity.
5. **Optional rewrite:** "We're each nothing without the other — let's not pretend otherwise."

### Prompt 4: Map to the compass

1. **Choice:** D (A and B)
2. **Reason:** A map without direction is unusable, and a compass without a destination is aimless — each critiques and depends on the other. Both lines capture a real half of the relationship.
3. **First step:** Figure out whether the "conversation" is about usefulness or about guidance.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** I might be flattening a distinction between "having a destination" and "knowing direction," which aren't the same thing.
5. **Optional rewrite:** "You tell me which way is north; I tell you why north matters."

### Prompt 5: CO₂ to the air

1. **Choice:** F (A and E)
2. **Reason:** In trace amounts CO₂ is negligible to air ("I don't affect you much"), but in excess it drives the greenhouse effect and in medical contexts elevated CO₂ triggers the breathing reflex that keeps people alive — so it both understates and saves.
3. **First step:** Pin down which domain — atmospheric, biological, or regulatory — the question means.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** Pairing a downplaying line with a life-saving one may paper over the fact that the harm and the help come from very different scales.
5. **Optional rewrite:** "At a whisper I'm nothing; at a flood I'm the reason you breathe and the reason you burn."

### Prompt 6: Telescope to the cosmos

1. **Choice:** F (A and B)
2. **Reason:** A telescope genuinely extends what can be seen ("you can't see beyond the stars without me"), but it also depends on celestial objects to have anything to point at ("I'd be clueless without your directions"). Mutual need, stated both ways.
3. **First step:** Decide whether "seeing" here means optical capability or interpretive meaning.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** Claiming both may smuggle in humility that isn't really earned — the telescope *does* do something the cosmos can't do for a human eye.
5. **Optional rewrite:** "You give me something to look at; I give you someone to look back."
