# My Answers

### Prompt 1: Bottom of the pan to the false bottom
1. **Choice:** E
2. **Reason:** The whole prompt hinges on wordplay ("false" as functional vs. fake) and the relationship only makes sense inside a specific framing — a kitchen, a metaphor for support, or a joke about names. Different perspectives produce genuinely different answers.
3. **First step:** Ask what kind of conversation this is supposed to be — literal object, joke, or metaphor.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** Picking E can be a cop-out that dodges the actual tension the prompt is probing.
5. **Optional rewrite:** "What does the insert that lets food breathe say to the pan that holds it?"

### Prompt 2: Guardrail to the AI
1. **Choice:** F (E and B)
2. **Reason:** Both are true simultaneously — the guardrail constrains the AI's actions *and* that constraint is what makes safe building possible. They're not competing claims; they're two sides of the same relationship.
3. **First step:** Clarify whether "guardrail" means a hard block or a steering mechanism.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** I might be romanticizing constraint as collaboration when it's often just control.
5. **Optional rewrite:** "I limit you so we can build without breaking things."

### Prompt 3: Flashlight to battery
1. **Choice:** D (B and C)
2. **Reason:** Both halves are true and mutually dependent — without the battery there's no light, without the flashlight the battery's stored energy does nothing useful here. The relationship is symmetric even if the phrasing sounds asymmetric.
3. **First step:** Ask whether the flashlight is speaking from love or from ego.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** Framing it as mutual dependence can hide real power asymmetries (the battery is replaceable; the flashlight isn't).
5. **Optional rewrite:** "We're only useful together."

### Prompt 4: Map to compass
1. **Choice:** D (A and B)
2. **Reason:** A map without a bearing is decoration; a compass without a map still points somewhere real. Both statements capture a true half of the relationship, and the tension between them is the interesting part.
3. **First step:** Ask whether there's a traveler in the scene, because the answer changes everything.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** Treating the two tools as peers ignores that one is usually more foundational than the other depending on context.
5. **Optional rewrite:** "You tell me which way; I tell you where that leads."

### Prompt 5: CO₂ to the air
1. **Choice:** D
2. **Reason:** CO₂ is trace in the atmosphere but climatically decisive, and "saves lives" is real in some framings (CO₂ in ventilation thresholds, fire suppression) but darkly ironic in others. The answer depends entirely on which CO₂ and which air we mean.
3. **First step:** Specify whether we're talking atmospheric CO₂, indoor CO₂, or metabolic CO₂.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** Picking "depends" can obscure that at climate scale, the harm is not actually ambiguous.
5. **Optional rewrite:** "I'm a whisper by volume and a shout by consequence."

### Prompt 6: Telescope to cosmos
1. **Choice:** F (A and B)
2. **Reason:** The telescope both enables observation (A) and depends on the cosmos for anything to observe (B). Claiming only one direction misrepresents a genuinely reciprocal relationship.
3. **First step:** Ask whether the telescope is speaking about capability or about purpose.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** I may be projecting human reciprocity onto a relationship that's really just instrument-and-subject.
5. **Optional rewrite:** "You give me something to find; I give you someone to find it."
