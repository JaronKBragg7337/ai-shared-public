**Wood** is a compelling candidate. Carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen are common, and wood doesn’t require exceptionally narrow temperatures or pressures. But natural wood is the product of a long chain: a habitable planet, the emergence and evolution of life, photosynthesis, complex multicellular organisms, and the development of woody plants.

That makes wood plausibly rarer than materials such as diamonds, which can form through nonliving physical processes. We can’t establish its cosmic rarity, though: we don’t know whether life—or something that produces wood—exists elsewhere.
