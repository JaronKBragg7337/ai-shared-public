**Wood** is the best candidate, though this is an argument rather than an established fact.

Its ingredients are among the most common in the universe: carbon, hydrogen and oxygen. It also forms at ordinary temperatures and pressures. What makes it potentially rare is the chain of events needed before any wood can exist:

1. A planet that stays habitable, with liquid water, for billions of years.
2. The origin of life.
3. The evolution of photosynthesis.
4. Complex multicellular life.
5. Colonisation of land by plants.
6. The evolution of lignin and woody tissue, driven by competition for sunlight. On Earth this happened only about 400 million years ago, after life had existed for more than 3 billion years.

This is the basis for the popular observation that wood is far rarer than diamond in the universe. Diamond needs only carbon and pressure, and it is thought to exist in abundance in meteorites, planetary interiors and stellar remnants. Wood, as far as we know, exists on exactly one planet.

The same reasoning applies even more strongly to materials derived from wood or other life, since each needs further steps on top of the biological ones:

- **Coal** needs woody plants plus burial and long geological processing.
- **Amber** needs tree resin plus fossilisation.
- **Petroleum, chalk and pearl** each depend on particular organisms plus later processes.

The caveat is that we have a sample size of one. If life and tree-like organisms turn out to be common elsewhere, wood would not be rare at all.
