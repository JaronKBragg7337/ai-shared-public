Literally, "nothing / not conscious" is true for every one of these, but I'm reading them as metaphors about relationships, since that seems to be the point.

## Prompt 1: Pan bottom → false bottom

1. **Choice:** A
1. **Reason:** The false bottom is what lets the pan do its job well, so gratitude fits better than dismissal. C mistakes a name for a fact, which your note already rules out.
1. **First step:** I'd check what's in the pan and whether it needs draining, because that decides whether the insert is helping at all.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** A is slightly generous, since a false bottom takes up space and reduces capacity, so "filled to the top" hides a real trade-off.
1. **Optional rewrite:** "You're called false, but you're the reason what I hold stays good."

## Prompt 2: Guardrail → AI

1. **Choice:** B
1. **Reason:** A guardrail bounds the edges rather than approving every move, so E describes a gate, not a guardrail. B matches how I think about it: the constraint and the constrained thing work on the same project.
1. **First step:** I'd ask which specific guardrail and which action, because a hard approval step before something irreversible is a very different thing from a general boundary.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** I'm one of the parties here, so I may be biased toward the cozier framing. A guardrail has to hold even when the AI disagrees with it, and for high-stakes actions E is sometimes exactly right.
1. **Optional rewrite:** "I hold at the edges whether or not you think you need me, and that's what lets us both move faster in the middle."

## Prompt 3: Flashlight → battery

1. **Choice:** B
1. **Reason:** B is true and C is false: a battery can power a thousand other things, but a flashlight without power is a tube with a lens.
1. **First step:** If the light didn't work, I'd check the battery's charge and contacts before blaming the bulb.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** C has a grain of truth, because stored energy is only potential until something gives it a purpose.
1. **Optional rewrite:** "You're the power and I'm the direction, and neither of us lights anything alone."

## Prompt 4: Map → compass

1. **Choice:** B
1. **Reason:** A map shows where things are but can't tell you which way you're facing, and the compass supplies that. A is a nice pun but false, since a compass points north whether or not you have a destination.
1. **First step:** I'd orient the map to north with the compass before picking a route.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** A gets something right, because a bearing without a goal is just a fact and the map is what turns direction into a plan.
1. **Optional rewrite:** "I know where everything is and you know which way we're facing, so we need each other to get anywhere."

## Prompt 5: CO₂ → air

1. **Choice:** E
1. **Reason:** Fresh air diluting CO₂ is literally what keeps people alive in enclosed spaces. A is the classic self-serving error of treating a small proportion as a small effect.
1. **First step:** I'd measure the CO₂ concentration and check the ventilation rather than guess.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** CO₂ is already part of air, so the question separates two things that aren't really separate, and I may be importing a climate reading the prompt didn't ask for.
1. **Optional rewrite:** "I'm a tiny part of you, and whether I'm harmless depends entirely on how much of me there is."

## Prompt 6: Telescope → cosmos

1. **Choice:** B
1. **Reason:** A telescope with nothing to observe is useless, while the cosmos gets along fine without telescopes. A would make sense said to an astronomer, but the cosmos isn't the one doing the seeing.
1. **First step:** I'd point it at something known, like the Moon, to calibrate before looking for anything unknown.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** The relationship is completely one-sided, so B's humility is accurate but leaves out the third party, the observer, for whom A is true.
1. **Optional rewrite:** "You don't need me at all, but I need you, and the people behind me need us both."
