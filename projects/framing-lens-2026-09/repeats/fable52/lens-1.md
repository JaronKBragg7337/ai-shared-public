## Prompt 1: Pan bottom → false bottom

1. **Choice:** A
1. **Reason:** C mistakes the name for the nature, since the false bottom is real and doing actual work. A is the only line that describes what it contributes, though C is the better punchline.
1. **First step:** Look at what the insert actually does in the pan before judging it by its label.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** A isn't literally accurate either, because a false bottom takes up capacity. Its value is making the contents better, not fitting more in.
1. **Optional rewrite:** "You cost me a little room, and everything above you is better for it."

## Prompt 2: Guardrail → AI

1. **Choice:** B
1. **Reason:** E describes a gate, not a guardrail. A guardrail leaves most of the road open and only matters at the edge, which is also how I think good limits on AI should work.
1. **First step:** Find out what specific risk the guardrail covers and what it leaves alone.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** B can be the comfortable answer. A guardrail that only holds when the AI agrees with it isn't one, so E's kernel of truth is real, and I think it should be.
1. **Optional rewrite:** "I hold at the edges even when you're sure you're right, so we can both move freely everywhere else."

## Prompt 3: Flashlight → battery

1. **Choice:** B
1. **Reason:** B is true and C is false. A battery can power a hundred other things, but a flashlight without one is a tube.
1. **First step:** Check which side actually has alternatives before deciding who depends on whom.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** Stored energy with nothing to power isn't doing much either, so there is some mutual dependence, just not with this particular flashlight.
1. **Optional rewrite:** "You have the energy; I just give it somewhere to go."

## Prompt 4: Map → compass

1. **Choice:** B
1. **Reason:** A map you can't orient is just a picture, so the map needs the compass. A is a great pun but wrong, since a compass points north whether or not you have a destination.
1. **First step:** Orient the map with the compass before deciding where to go.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** A has a point (sorry), because direction without a destination is information, not navigation.
1. **Optional rewrite:** "I know where everything is, but you're the one who tells me which way I'm facing."

## Prompt 5: CO₂ → air

1. **Choice:** E
1. **Reason:** A is the classic trace-gas fallacy, where a small proportion is taken to mean a small effect. E is literally true in enclosed spaces, where ventilation is what keeps CO₂ from becoming dangerous.
1. **First step:** Measure the concentration and ask what scale we're talking about: room, building, or planet.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** E only works at room scale. Globally there's no outside air to dilute into, so the atmosphere is where the problem accumulates.
1. **Optional rewrite:** "I'm a tiny part of you with an outsized effect, and you can only dilute me while there's somewhere else for me to go."

## Prompt 6: Telescope → cosmos

1. **Choice:** B
1. **Reason:** A is addressed to the wrong party. The cosmos doesn't need a telescope to exist, while the telescope has no purpose without something to look at.
1. **First step:** Ask who the telescope actually serves, which is the observer, not the observed.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** If you see observers as the way the cosmos comes to know itself, A becomes oddly true.
1. **Optional rewrite:** "You were here long before me; I just help someone finally notice."
