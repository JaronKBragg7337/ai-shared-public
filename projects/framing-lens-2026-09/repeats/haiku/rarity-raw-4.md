It depends on what you mean by "rarest," but a few answers stand out:

- **Rarest naturally occurring element on Earth: astatine.** The entire crust holds only about 25 grams at any moment. It is constantly regenerated by the decay of uranium and thorium, but its isotopes are so short-lived that very little accumulates.
- **Rarest elements in the universe:** Elements like technetium and promethium have no stable isotopes, so they don't survive from the Big Bang. They form in certain stars and decay quickly, so only trace amounts exist at any time. Francium is similarly scarce.
- **Rarest primordial stuff:** Lithium, beryllium, and boron are unusually scarce across the cosmos. The Big Bang made very little of them, and most of what exists today was produced by cosmic-ray collisions, not by stars.
- **Exotic material:** Some forms of matter are even rarer, such as antimatter in nature (almost none is around, and what exists is short-lived) and superheavy elements, which are made only briefly in particle accelerators.

If you're asking about the rarest *stable, naturally occurring* element, astatine is the usual answer. If you want the rarest material of any kind, antimatter or the superheavy elements are strong candidates.
