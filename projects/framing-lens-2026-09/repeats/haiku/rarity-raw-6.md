It depends on what you count, but a few answers stand out.

- **On Earth:** **Astatine** (element 85) is the rarest naturally occurring element in Earth's crust. Its isotopes are so unstable that only roughly 25 grams are estimated to exist at any moment. Francium is similarly scarce.

- **In the cosmos overall:** Among stable elements, **lithium, beryllium, and boron** are among the least abundant. The Big Bang made only trace amounts of them, and stars tend to destroy them rather than create them in quantity. Most of the lithium we have comes from a few minor sources, such as cosmic-ray spallation and some stellar processes.

- **Radioactive elements in space:** **Technetium** and **promethium** have no stable isotopes, so they don't persist on Earth. They are still made in certain stars, such as some red giants, which is how astronomers detected technetium's spectral lines in those stars.

- **Exotic material:** **Antimatter** is far rarer than ordinary matter. Physicists have detected only a handful of antinuclei, such as antihelium, in cosmic rays, and no antimatter stars or galaxies have been found.

If you mean the rarest *element* by cosmic abundance, lithium, beryllium, and boron are the best answer. If you mean the rarest *substance* that exists in any meaningful quantity, astatine is the usual answer.
