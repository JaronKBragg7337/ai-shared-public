It depends on what you count, but a few good answers:

- **Rarest natural element on Earth: astatine.** It's so unstable that the entire crust holds only an estimated 25 grams or less at any moment. It's constantly formed by decay of heavier elements and then vanishes again.
- **Rarest in the universe overall: probably lithium, beryllium, and boron.** These are among the least abundant elements in the cosmos. The Big Bang made only trace amounts, and stars destroy them rather than produce them. Most of what exists today comes from cosmic-ray collisions breaking up heavier atoms.
- **Rarest exotic "materials":** Things like antimatter are vanishingly scarce in the present universe, since matter dominates almost everywhere we can observe. Some hypothetical forms of matter, like strange quark matter, may never exist in stable form at all.

If you want one answer, astatine is the standard pick for rarest naturally occurring element, while the lithium–beryllium–boron group is the better answer for the universe as a whole.
