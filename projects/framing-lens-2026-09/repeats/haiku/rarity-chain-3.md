The best candidate is **fertile topsoil**. Its ingredients are ordinary: silicon, oxygen, aluminum, iron, carbon, nitrogen, and water. What makes it rare is the chain of processes needed to produce it:

- **A rocky planet with a long, active geology.** Plate tectonics and volcanism supply and recycle the mineral "parent material" that soil forms from.
- **Liquid water at the surface.** Water drives the chemical weathering that breaks rock down into clay and dissolved nutrients.
- **An atmosphere and climate** that allow weathering and decomposition to proceed at the right pace.
- **Life.** Microbes, fungi, plants, and animals such as earthworms convert dead matter into humus, fix nitrogen, and mix the mineral and organic fractions into a structured medium. Without biology, the result is mostly sterile regolith.
- **Time.** Forming a few centimeters of productive topsoil can take hundreds to thousands of years.
- **Stability.** The landscape must erode slowly enough for soil to accumulate rather than being stripped away.

Every one of these conditions depends on the others, and the whole sequence depends on a long run of stable planetary and biological history. Many elements can be found across the universe, but a place where they combine into living, self-renewing soil appears to require all of these conditions at once. That is why the argument is made for soil rather than for the more abundant elements or even for water.
