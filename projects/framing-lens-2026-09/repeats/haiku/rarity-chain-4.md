**Soil, specifically fertile topsoil**, is a strong candidate.

Its ingredients are ordinary: silicates, oxides, carbon, water, nitrogen, and phosphorus are all common in the universe. Its rarity comes from the chain of processes needed to make it:

1. **A rocky planet with liquid water** on its surface, which is uncommon even among rocky worlds.
2. **An atmosphere and climate** that allow rain, freeze-thaw cycles, and chemical weathering to break rock down over long periods.
3. **Geological renewal**, such as tectonics, volcanism, or erosion, to continually expose fresh mineral material.
4. **Life**, especially microbes, fungi, plant roots, and burrowing animals, to fix nitrogen, secrete acids, build organic matter, and mix everything together.
5. **Time**, often centuries to millennia to form a few centimeters of topsoil.
6. **Stability** over long spans, since the soil must survive without being stripped away by erosion, fire, or catastrophe.

Each condition is individually conceivable, but the full sequence has to occur together, in the right order, over geological time. We have found no soil like Earth's anywhere else, and the dusty regolith on Mars or the Moon lacks the biological and chemical history that defines soil. Soil therefore illustrates a material whose rarity lies in its formation history rather than its component parts.
