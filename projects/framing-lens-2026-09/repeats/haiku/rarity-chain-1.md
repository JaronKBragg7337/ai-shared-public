**Soil** is a strong candidate.

Its ingredients are ordinary: silicate minerals, water, air, and organic matter, all common in the universe. Soil also doesn't need an unusual temperature or pressure window. What makes it rare is the chain of processes needed to produce it:

- **A rocky planet with an active geology** to supply and recycle mineral material, through erosion, volcanism, and tectonic uplift.
- **Liquid water cycling across the surface**, which drives chemical weathering and carries dissolved material around.
- **An atmosphere** that supplies oxygen and carbon dioxide and shapes the weathering reactions.
- **Life**, especially microbes, plants, and fungi, which add organic matter, break down rock, and build the structure and nutrient cycling that make soil soil rather than just crushed rock.
- **Time**: in many settings, forming a few centimeters of fertile topsoil takes centuries to millennia.
- **Stable enough conditions over long periods** for these processes to keep running without being reset by catastrophic events.

Each of these has been observed in only one known place, Earth, and the chain depends on all of them operating together over deep time. Most rocky worlds we know of lack at least some links, and a fertile soil layer like Earth's is not something we have seen anywhere else.
