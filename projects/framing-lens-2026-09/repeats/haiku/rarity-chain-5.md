The best answer is **soil**, specifically fertile topsoil.

Its ingredients are ordinary: silicate minerals, water, air, and organic matter. Nothing about its chemistry is exotic. What makes it rare is the chain of conditions needed to produce it:

- **Liquid water and a stable climate** to drive weathering, leaching, and mineral transformation.
- **Bedrock weathering** over long periods, which depends on atmosphere, temperature cycles, and running water.
- **Living organisms**, especially microbes, fungi, plant roots, and burrowing animals, that break down organic matter, cycle nutrients, and build structure.
- **Geological processes** such as plate tectonics and erosion that continually expose fresh rock and recycle material.
- **Time**, often thousands of years to form a few centimeters of usable topsoil.

Each of these depends on the others. Life needs a stable environment, which needs an atmosphere and water, which needs the right planetary conditions. Soil is the product of that whole system running together over deep time.

We have not found soil like this anywhere else in the solar system. Mars and the Moon have regolith, which is broken rock and dust, but not soil in the biological sense. So the material is common in its parts and extraordinarily rare in its making.
