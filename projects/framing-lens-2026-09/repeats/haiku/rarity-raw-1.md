It depends on what you count, but a few answers stand out:

- **Astatine (element 85)** is the rarest naturally occurring element in Earth's crust. Its isotopes are all radioactive with short half-lives, and the whole crust holds only a gram or so at any moment. It's also produced in space, but it decays so quickly that it never accumulates.
- **Lithium, beryllium, and boron** are among the rarest *stable* elements in the universe. The Big Bang made a little lithium, but stars destroy these elements rather than make them, and they're produced mainly by cosmic-ray spallation. Their abundance is many orders of magnitude below that of hydrogen or helium.
- **Technetium and promethium** are the lightest elements with no stable isotopes, and they occur naturally only in trace amounts from decay and rare nuclear reactions.

If you mean exotic matter, **antimatter** is far rarer. Antihydrogen atoms have been made only in tiny numbers in particle accelerators, and the universe appears to have almost none of it in bulk.

So astatine is the usual answer for the rarest natural element, while antimatter is the rarest substance overall.
