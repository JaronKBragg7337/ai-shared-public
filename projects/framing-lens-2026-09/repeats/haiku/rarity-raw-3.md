It depends on what you mean by "rarest," but a few good candidates:

- **Astatine (element 85)** is probably the rarest naturally occurring element on Earth. Its isotopes all decay quickly, and it's only made in trace amounts when uranium and thorium decay. At any moment, the entire crust contains only a few grams or less.
- **Francium** is similarly scarce. Its longest-lived isotope lasts only about 22 minutes, so it barely accumulates anywhere.
- **Technetium and promethium** have no stable isotopes. Technetium has been detected in certain aging stars, where it's freshly produced, but it's essentially absent from the cosmos as a long-lived reservoir.
- **Tantalum-180m** is the rarest naturally occurring isotope that's been found in nature. It makes up only about 0.01% of natural tantalum, and it's so unusual that its half-life is still hard to pin down.

If you're asking about the rarest material in a broader sense, such as exotic matter, nothing in the universe is rarer than the antimatter, dark matter, and other hypothetical particles we've inferred but never seen in bulk. Those are unknown in composition and are not known to form any material at all.
