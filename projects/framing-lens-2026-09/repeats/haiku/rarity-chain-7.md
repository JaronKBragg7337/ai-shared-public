**Soil** is a strong candidate.

Its main ingredients, such as silicate minerals, carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, and water, are common in the universe. What makes soil rare is the chain of conditions needed to turn rock into soil:

- **Liquid water** to drive chemical weathering and carry dissolved material.
- **An atmosphere and climate** that allow rain, freeze-thaw cycles, and erosion.
- **Geological activity** such as tectonic uplift, which exposes fresh rock to weathering.
- **Time**, often millions of years, for rock to break down into fine particles.
- **Life**, especially microbes, fungi, plants, and animals, which add organic matter, fix nitrogen, and build the crumb structure that holds water and air.

Each of these depends on earlier conditions, and the sequence has to hold together over long periods. Mature, fertile topsoil is typically only a few centimeters to a few tens of centimeters thick, and many regions of the planet have none. Soil is therefore a product of the whole Earth system working together, not of any single ingredient or narrow range of conditions.

Coal is a reasonable alternative answer, since it requires land plants, rapid burial, and anoxic conditions over geological time. I'd still favor soil because it depends on more interacting processes, including life, climate, and tectonics.
