**Soil** is a strong candidate.

Its ingredients are ordinary: silicate minerals, water, air, and carbon. Its temperatures and pressures are unremarkable at the surface. What makes it rare is the chain of processes needed to produce it:

- **Rock to begin with:** a planet must generate or retain rock that can be broken down, which depends on geology such as volcanism and tectonics.
- **Liquid water over long periods:** weathering needs water to dissolve, hydrate, and transport minerals.
- **Life:** microbes, fungi, and plants produce organic matter, help break down rock, and build the crumb structure, nutrient cycling, and porosity that make soil function as soil.
- **Time:** good soil often takes hundreds to thousands of years to form a few centimeters, so it requires long stretches of stable conditions.
- **Climate and landscape stability:** erosion and deposition must roughly balance so that soil accumulates rather than being stripped away or buried too deeply.

Soil is the product of geology, chemistry, climate, and biology interacting over deep time. We have found no confirmed soil elsewhere in the universe, though Mars and the Moon have regolith, which is weathered rock but not biologically processed soil in the same sense. So soil is arguably one of the rarest materials we know of, even though it lies under our feet.
