**Wood** is a strong candidate. Its ingredients—carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen—are common, and trees don’t need an exceptionally narrow temperature or pressure range. But making wood requires a long chain: a suitable star and planet, liquid water and sunlight, life that evolves photosynthesis and complex multicellular growth, and organisms that build durable lignified tissue.

That makes wood potentially extraordinarily rare in the universe—not because its atoms are scarce, but because the whole biological history needed to produce it may be. It’s an argument, not a measured ranking: we don’t know how common life or trees are beyond Earth.
