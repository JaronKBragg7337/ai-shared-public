**Coal** is a plausible answer. Its ingredients—carbon-rich plant matter—are common, and coal doesn’t require an especially narrow temperature or pressure range. But making coal takes a long chain: life must evolve land plants, those plants must grow in vast wetlands, their remains must accumulate faster than they decay, and burial and geological change must preserve and transform them into coal.

That makes the *material itself* potentially rare across the universe, even if its ingredients are not. This is an informed argument, not a demonstrated ranking: we don’t know enough about life and geology elsewhere to establish which naturally occurring material is truly rarest.
