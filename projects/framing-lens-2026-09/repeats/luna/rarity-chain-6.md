**Coal** is a good candidate. Its ingredients—mostly carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen—are common, and it doesn’t require an especially narrow temperature or pressure window. But making coal takes a long chain: abundant plant life, burial in oxygen-poor wetlands, protection from decay and erosion, and slow geological alteration over millions of years. The material is familiar on Earth because those conditions have recurred here; elsewhere, the full sequence may be extraordinarily uncommon.

There’s no way to establish that coal is literally among the universe’s rarest materials without knowing what exists elsewhere. The point is that a familiar substance can be rare because its *history* is rare, even when its ingredients are not.
