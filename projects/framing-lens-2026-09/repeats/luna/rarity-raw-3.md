There isn’t a single well-defined “rarest material” in the cosmos: it depends on what counts as a material and what we can observe.

If you mean a naturally occurring chemical element, **astatine** is often called the rarest on Earth, but it decays quickly and isn’t a good answer for the whole universe. In space, some extremely heavy elements—such as **einsteinium**—may exist only in tiny, short-lived traces after rare stellar events.

So the safest answer is: **no known material can be named the rarest across the cosmos; the answer depends on the definition and on what has been observed.**
