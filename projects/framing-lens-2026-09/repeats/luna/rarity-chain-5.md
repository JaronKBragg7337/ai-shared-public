A plausible answer is **wood**—especially mature, complex terrestrial wood.

Its ingredients are common, and trees don’t need an unusually narrow temperature or pressure window. But producing wood requires a long chain: a suitable planet with liquid water and sunlight, photosynthetic life, an atmosphere and climate that let plants thrive, and an evolutionary history that produced large, lignified organisms. The material is familiar on Earth, yet that full chain may be exceptionally uncommon across the universe.

That’s an argument, not a measurable ranking: we don’t know enough about life elsewhere to establish which naturally occurring material is truly rarest.
