• **Wood** is a strong candidate.

  Its ingredients—carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen—are cosmically common, and it forms at ordinary temperatures and pressures. What is extraordinarily demanding is the chain behind it: a suitable planet, liquid water, the origin and persistence of life, photosynthesis, complex multicellular organisms, and eventually vascular plants that manufacture cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin into the intricate structure of wood.

  Diamonds, by contrast, can arise through comparatively straightforward physical processes and may exist on many worlds. Wood requires not merely the right conditions, but billions of years of contingent biological evolution. Since life is confirmed only on Earth, wood could plausibly be rarer than many gemstones—though its actual cosmic abundance is, of course, unknowable until we discover life elsewhere.
