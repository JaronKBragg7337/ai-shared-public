• There’s no rigorously defined single answer, but **antimatter is the best short answer**.

  The observable cosmos consists overwhelmingly of ordinary matter. Antiparticles do occur naturally—in cosmic rays, radioactive decay, and high-energy events—but they quickly annihilate on contact with matter, and no large reservoirs of antimatter are known.

  If you mean **elements**, the answer is less clear: some extremely short-lived isotopes may exist only fleetingly in rare astrophysical events. **Astatine** is often called the rarest naturally occurring element **on Earth**, not necessarily in the entire cosmos.
