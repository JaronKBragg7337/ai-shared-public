• There isn’t a scientifically well-defined single “rarest material,” but **antimatter is the best answer for naturally occurring bulk material**. The observable cosmos is overwhelmingly ordinary matter; antimatter appears mainly as individual particles produced in high-energy processes, with no confirmed stars, planets, or other large reservoirs made of it.

  Other interpretations give different answers:

  - **Rarest element overall:** synthetic superheavy elements such as **oganesson**, of which only a handful of atoms have ever been made, each surviving only briefly.
  - **Rarest naturally occurring element on Earth:** usually **astatine**, with roughly grams or less estimated in Earth’s crust at any moment—but that is not necessarily true throughout the cosmos.

  So the short answer is: **antimatter in bulk; synthetic superheavy elements by atom count.**
