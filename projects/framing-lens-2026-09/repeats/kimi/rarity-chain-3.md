• **Wood** is a strong candidate.

  Its ingredients—carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen—are cosmically common, and trees grow across a fairly broad range of temperatures and pressures. What is extraordinarily demanding is the entire chain leading to wood:

  - a planet with suitable chemistry, liquid water, and long-term environmental stability;
  - the origin and persistence of life;
  - photosynthesis and an oxygenated atmosphere;
  - the evolution of complex multicellular plants;
  - vascular tissue, lignin, and finally trees producing secondary xylem;
  - living cells assembling cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin into wood’s intricate structure.

  Diamonds merely require carbon and suitable geology; wood requires a biosphere and billions of years of evolutionary history. Since life—and therefore trees—is currently confirmed only on Earth, wood has been observed naturally only here.

  That does not establish that wood is literally the universe’s rarest material: we cannot survey most of the universe, and other biospheres might produce analogous materials. But as an argument about the **improbability of the complete natural production process**, rather than scarcity of atoms, wood is an excellent answer.
