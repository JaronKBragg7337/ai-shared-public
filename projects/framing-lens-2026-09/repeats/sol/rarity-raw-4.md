There’s **no established “rarest material in the cosmos.”** We can’t inventory the whole universe, and the answer depends on what counts as a material.

If you mean a **naturally occurring chemical element**, **astatine** is a plausible candidate: every isotope is radioactive, and even its longest-lived isotope decays with a half-life of about eight hours. It can’t accumulate much—but that doesn’t establish it as the rarest element across the cosmos.

If you include substances that exist only fleetingly, certain **superheavy isotopes** could be rarer still.

The honest answer is **unknown**, with astatine a reasonable candidate under a narrow definition.
