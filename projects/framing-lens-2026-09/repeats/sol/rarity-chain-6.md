**Wood** is a strong candidate.

Its ingredients—carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen—are abundant. What makes wood potentially extraordinarily rare is the chain required to assemble them into wood naturally: a sustained biosphere, photosynthesis, the evolution of complex multicellular plants, and specialized cells that build cellulose and lignin into organized tissue. Nature must produce a wood-forming organism, sustain its growth, and preserve the resulting material long enough for it to exist as wood.

That is a much deeper requirement than simply bringing elements together under suitable temperature and pressure. Wood is the product of an evolutionary history.

We cannot establish that it is *among the rarest* materials throughout the universe, because we do not know how common extraterrestrial life is. But it is a defensible candidate: **a commonplace Earth material whose natural production depends on an exceptionally elaborate biological chain.**
