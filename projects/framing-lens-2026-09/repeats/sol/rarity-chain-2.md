**Wood** is a strong candidate.

Its ingredients—mostly carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen—are common throughout the universe. But nature producing wood requires a much longer chain: life must arise, develop complex cellular machinery, evolve photosynthesis and multicellular organisms, and eventually produce plants that build organized tissues from cellulose and lignin. Ordinary wood is a biological structure, not simply a substance that forms when the right elements meet.

That makes wood potentially far rarer than diamonds: diamonds can form through nonliving geological processes, while wood requires an elaborate evolutionary history.

The qualification is that **we don’t know how rare wood actually is across the universe**. Earth is the only place where we’ve confirmed it. So “among the rarest” is a defensible argument about its production requirements, rather than an established cosmic ranking.
