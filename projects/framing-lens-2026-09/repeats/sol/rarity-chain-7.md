**Wood** is a strong candidate.

Its ingredients—mostly carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen—are common in the universe. What makes wood potentially extraordinary is the chain of natural processes needed to produce it: life must arise, evolve complex cells and multicellular organisms, develop photosynthesis, and eventually produce plants with specialized tissues that build cellulose and lignin into woody structures.

Making wood therefore requires more than suitable chemistry, temperature, and pressure. It requires a long biological history within an environment that can sustain it. A tree’s trunk is, in that sense, a material manufactured by evolution.

The caveat is that **we don’t know how rare wood actually is across the universe**. We have no census of extraterrestrial life. But it is reasonable to argue that ordinary wood could be far rarer than diamonds or gold, because its production depends on an elaborate biological chain rather than physical processes alone.
