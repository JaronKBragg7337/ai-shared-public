**Wood** is a strong candidate.

Its ingredients—carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen—are abundant in the universe. But nature producing wood requires an extraordinary chain: a habitable environment, the emergence of life, photosynthesis, complex multicellular organisms, and the evolution of plants that build woody tissue from cellulose and lignin.

Wood is therefore more than a chemical mixture. It is an organized material grown by organisms with a long evolutionary history. Common on Earth, it has never been confirmed anywhere else.

That makes “among the rarest materials in the known universe” a defensible argument, though not a measured ranking: we don’t know how common life or analogous materials are elsewhere.
