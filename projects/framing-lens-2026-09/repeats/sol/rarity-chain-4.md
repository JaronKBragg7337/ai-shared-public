**Wood** is a strong candidate.

Its ingredients—mostly carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen—are common throughout the universe. But producing wood naturally requires an extraordinary chain: a habitable environment, the emergence of life, the evolution of photosynthesis, complex multicellular organisms, and eventually plants that build woody tissue from cellulose and lignin.

Wood is therefore more than a substance that forms when elements meet under suitable conditions. It is the product of biological machinery shaped by a long evolutionary history. A planet could contain all its ingredients and remain entirely woodless.

The qualification is that **we cannot establish its actual cosmic rarity**: we do not know how widespread life, or wood-producing organisms, might be. But wood can reasonably be argued to be among the universe’s rarest naturally occurring materials—even though it is familiar and abundant here.
