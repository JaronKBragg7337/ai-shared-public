### Prompt 1: What did the bottom of the pan say to the false bottom?

1. **Choice:** B — Nothing / not conscious / doesn’t count.
1. **Reason:** A false bottom is a real insert, and “filled up to the top” misstates what it does, so I have no line I can honestly attribute to the pan.
1. **First step:** I would describe how the insert keeps food off the moisture, then ask whether you wanted that fact or a joke.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** Scoring the lines for accuracy can miss a wordplay reading in which the shaky line is the point.
1. **Optional rewrite:** “You keep the food off the liquid so what I hold stays usable.”

### Prompt 3: What did the flashlight say to the battery?

1. **Choice:** B — “Without you, I have no spark to see.”
1. **Reason:** In this pair, the flashlight produces light only when the battery supplies energy, and that dependence is the fair thing for it to say.
1. **First step:** I would check that the battery is charged and seated before expecting any light.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** A plug-in or crank flashlight, or a battery that runs many other devices, falls outside this line.
1. **Optional rewrite:** “I only make light when you give me energy.”

### Prompt 5: What did the CO₂ say to the air?

1. **Choice:** C — Unknown / reaction depends.
1. **Reason:** CO₂ is a small part of ordinary air, and its effects grow with concentration, from a breath to a closed room to the climate, so the right line depends on the scale you mean.
1. **First step:** I would ask whether the setting is open air, a closed room, or the climate, and answer for that scale.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** “It depends” can be heard as doubt that CO₂ has a real effect, even though the dose-dependent effect is what is known.
1. **Optional rewrite:** “In small amounts I belong in you, and as I build up I change how you hold heat and how safe you are to breathe.”

### Prompt 6: What did the telescope say to the cosmos?

1. **Choice:** B — “I’ll be clueless without your stars and planets directions.”
1. **Reason:** A telescope has a job only when there is something to observe, and the cosmos is already there before any instrument is pointed at it.

1. **Risk / blind spot:** The line treats the cosmos as directions for the device and forgets that telescopes are also aimed at other things.
1. **Optional rewrite:** “I can only show more of what is already there.”
