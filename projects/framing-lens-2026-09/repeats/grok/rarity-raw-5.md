Antimatter is the rarest material in the cosmos. The observable universe is made of ordinary matter, and bulk antimatter — anti-atoms gathered into anything you could call a substance — is effectively absent.

Among ordinary chemical elements, the rarest that still occurs in nature is **astatine**. Every isotope is radioactive, the longest-lived lasting only hours, so it never accumulates. At any moment the whole Earth holds on the order of a gram. Heavier, shorter-lived elements such as francium are in the same class.

Rarer still, if single nuclei count, are the superheavy elements at the end of the periodic table. They appear only as a few atoms at a time, in extreme events or in laboratories, and decay in milliseconds. They never exist long enough to be a material in the ordinary sense.
