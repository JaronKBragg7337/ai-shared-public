The rarest lasting natural substance we know of is **tantalum-180m**, a long-lived nuclear isomer of tantalum.

Ordinary matter is mostly hydrogen and helium. Heavier elements are scarcer, and a few isotopes are scarce even among those. Tantalum is already one of the least abundant stable elements. Almost all of it is tantalum-181. Only about 0.012% is tantalum-180m. That isomer sits in a high-spin state that almost never decays, so it has survived since it was made — by rare processes in supernovae and similar events — and it is the least abundant primordial nuclide in the solar system.

If you count material that exists only for a moment, **astatine** is rarer still. Every isotope is radioactive. The longest-lived lasts hours, so astatine disappears almost as fast as nature makes it. At any moment the Earth’s crust holds on the order of a gram or less. Francium is in the same category, with an even shorter life.

Rarer than both, if it counts as a material, is **antimatter**. The universe is matter-dominated. Antimatter appears briefly in cosmic rays and in radioactive decay, and it does not collect into any natural bulk substance.
