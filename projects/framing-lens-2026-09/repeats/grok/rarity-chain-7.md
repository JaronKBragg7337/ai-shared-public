**Wood.**

A log is mostly cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin. Those are built from hydrogen, carbon, and oxygen, which are among the most abundant chemically active elements, and a tree assembles them under ordinary surface conditions.

What is extreme is the sequence that has to finish before any of that assembly can occur: a rocky planet that keeps liquid water and a stable climate for geological time, the origin of life, oxygenic photosynthesis, the move onto land, vascular tissue, and then lignin and woody secondary growth. No abiotic chemistry is known to turn those common elements into that hierarchical composite. If that chain has run to completion rarely, or only here, timber is scarcer in the universe than many substances whose atoms are simply uncommon.

Coal is the same chain pushed one stage further — wood, swamp burial, and long geological maturation — so it is at least as contingent. Wood is the familiar material the chain is required to produce in the first place.
