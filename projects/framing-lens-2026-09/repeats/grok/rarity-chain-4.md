**Wood.**

Carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen are common, and the temperatures and pressures in which trees grow are not exotic. What is extraordinarily specific is the chain required to assemble those elements into wood: a habitable planet, an origin of life, photosynthesis, multicellular land plants, the evolution of vascular tissue and lignin, and a long-lived biosphere in which forests can exist. That composite of cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin is a product of one evolutionary history. If that history is unique, or even merely very rare, wood may occur nowhere else in the known universe, while diamonds, water ice, and most minerals can form by straightforward chemistry under conditions that are not unique to Earth.
