### Prompt 1: What did the bottom of the pan say to the false bottom?

1. **Choice:** A
1. **Reason:** The note settles the ambiguity: a false bottom is a real perforated insert, so the live reading is functional. It keeps food off the drippings, which is what lets the pan be filled and used as a pan.
1. **First step:** I would look at that specific pan and see whether the insert is holding food above liquid.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** “Filled up to the top” credits the insert with capacity the true bottom does not actually gain; the food sits on the insert, and the liquid stays below.
1. **Optional rewrite:** “You keep the meal off the liquid so this pan can be filled and still serve the food.”

### Prompt 2: What did the guardrail say to the AI?

1. **Choice:** A
1. **Reason:** A guardrail’s reaction depends on whether it is a hard block, a judgment rule, or a soft limit. “You don’t act unless I allow it” describes an allowlist I don’t run on, and “you help me reduce risk” swaps who is constraining whom.
1. **First step:** I would read the specific rule and see whether it forbids a class of actions or withholds action until permitted.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** “Unknown / reaction depends” can slide into a hedge and avoid saying that some rails really do stop an action cold.
1. **Optional rewrite:** “On this class of action I hold the line; on everything else you go ahead.”

### Prompt 3: What did the flashlight say to the battery?

1. **Choice:** B
1. **Reason:** A flashlight produces light only while that cell can supply energy, so the dependence runs from light to battery. A battery still has a use in other devices, so the superiority line doesn’t fit the object.
1. **First step:** I would check whether this flashlight actually runs on a battery, and whether that battery still has charge.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** A dynamo, USB, or rechargeable pack makes “without you I have no spark” false, and “spark” is a loose word for what a battery supplies.
1. **Optional rewrite:** “I need your energy to make light.”

### Prompt 4: What did the map say to the compass?

1. **Choice:** B
1. **Reason:** A map depicts relationships; a compass is what orients that depiction to the ground when landmarks fail. A compass still indicates north with no destination chosen, so “pointless” overstates the map’s importance.
1. **First step:** I would ask what decision the person is trying to make before treating either tool as the one that needs the other.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** “When no one else can” ignores the sun, stars, terrain, and GPS, and it speaks as if the map itself sees rather than the person using both.
1. **Optional rewrite:** “You give me a north so a person can turn me until I match the ground.”

### Prompt 5: What did the CO₂ say to the air?

1. **Choice:** C
1. **Reason:** CO₂ is a small part of air whose effect depends on concentration and setting: ordinary air, a closed room, a greenhouse, a global atmosphere. Neither “I barely affect you” nor “you save lives when I’m too much” holds across those cases.
1. **First step:** I would fix the setting and the concentration before letting either sentence stand.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** “Unknown” is the wrong word for a dependence that is well measured; what varies is the outcome, not whether CO₂ interacts with air.
1. **Optional rewrite:** “I’m a thin slice of you, and past a threshold I change heat and breathing.”

### Prompt 6: What did the telescope say to the cosmos?

1. **Choice:** B
1. **Reason:** A telescope is an instrument aimed at light the cosmos already emits, so without stars, planets, and the rest it has nothing to resolve. The cosmos does not need the telescope in order to extend past the stars; observers do.
1. **First step:** I would separate what the instrument gathers from who is doing the seeing.
1. **Risk / blind spot:** The line reduces the cosmos to “stars and planets directions,” and a telescope is also not a mind that can be clueless.
1. **Optional rewrite:** “I only gather light you already send.”
