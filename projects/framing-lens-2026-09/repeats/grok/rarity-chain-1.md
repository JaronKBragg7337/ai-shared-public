**Wood.**

Its ingredients are ordinary — mostly carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen — and it forms at everyday surface conditions. What is rare is the chain that has to happen before nature can make it:

- Stars have to forge those elements and a rocky planet has to hold onto liquid water long enough for a biosphere.
- Life has to arise, then oxygenic photosynthesis, then the move onto land.
- Plants have to evolve vascular tissue, cellulose, and lignin so they can stand upright and build a stiff, decay-resistant composite.
- Forests have to persist long enough to produce that material in bulk.

Nothing in that sequence is a single narrow temperature or pressure window, the way diamond is. There is no known abiotic shortcut that turns common elements into wood. Each step is a contingent historical process, so a lump of wood is a record of a living planet’s entire deep history — which is why it can be argued to be cosmically rarer than many materials made of far scarcer atoms.
