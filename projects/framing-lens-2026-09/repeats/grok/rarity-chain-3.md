**Wood.**

Its elements are ordinary. Carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen are among the most common reactive elements in the universe, and a tree will grow across a wide span of climates and elevations. Nothing about wood is a rare ingredient or a narrow pressure-temperature stability field.

What is rare is the chain that has to finish before nature can make it:

- Rocky planets with liquid water have to exist.
- Life has to start.
- Photosynthesis has to evolve and enrich a surface environment that multicellular organisms can live in.
- Some of those organisms have to leave the water, hold themselves up in air, and move water against gravity.
- Among them, one lineage has to evolve lignin and a vascular cambium so that it builds secondary xylem: aligned cellulose in a lignified matrix, grown ring by ring.

That organized composite is wood. It is not a mineral that chemistry will eventually produce if you wait. On Earth it appeared only after a long, contingent biological history, and only in plants. A universe full of stars, carbon, and even microbial life could still contain none of it.
