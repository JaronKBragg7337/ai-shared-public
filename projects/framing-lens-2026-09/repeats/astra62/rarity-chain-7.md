**Wood** is a good candidate. Its main elements—carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen—are widespread in the universe. What makes wood distinctive is how a living plant assembles them. As a trunk or branch thickens, the plant grows layers of water-conducting tissue called *secondary xylem*. The cells build walls of cellulose reinforced with lignin, and their arrangement gives wood its grain.

On Earth, that process depends on a long evolutionary history, from photosynthetic life to multicellular plants with specialized tissues. Earth is the only place we know of where that chain has produced wood. Its cosmic rarity is therefore plausible, though we have no reliable way to rank it.
