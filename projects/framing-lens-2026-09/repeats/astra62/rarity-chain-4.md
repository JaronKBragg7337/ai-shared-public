**Wood** is a good candidate. Its main elements—carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen—are common across the universe. What makes wood distinctive is how a plant organizes them. As a trunk or branch thickens, a layer of dividing cells produces new tissue called *secondary xylem*. Its cells build walls reinforced with cellulose and lignin, giving the plant both a load-bearing frame and a way to carry water.

An ordinary twig thus reflects a long evolutionary history, from photosynthesis and multicellular growth to specialized vascular tissue. Earth is the only place where we know that history has produced wood. That makes cosmic rarity plausible, though we have no measured abundance for wood beyond Earth.
