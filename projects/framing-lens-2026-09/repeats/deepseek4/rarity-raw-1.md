There’s no single absolute answer without defining “material” (natural vs synthetic, element vs isotope, etc.). But conventionally, the rarest **naturally occurring element** is **astatine (At)**.

Astatine has no stable isotopes; its longest-lived isotope, At-210, has a half-life of only about **8 hours**. It exists only as a transient decay product of heavier elements, so its abundance at any moment is vanishingly small—on Earth, it’s often said that **less than a gram** exists in the crust at any time. Cosmically, it would likewise exist only in trace transient amounts.

If synthetic materials count, then lab-made superheavy elements like **oganesson** are even rarer.
