Wood.

Its ingredients—carbon, hydrogen, oxygen—are cosmically common, and it forms at ordinary surface temperatures and pressures. But wood is not a simple mineral or geochemical product; it is a biological structural material. Producing it requires a long chain of processes: a habitable rocky planet with liquid water, the origin of life, photosynthesis, multicellular plants, the evolution of vascular tissue and lignin/cellulose, and the eventual emergence of trees.

Because we have no evidence that this full chain has occurred anywhere else, ordinary, familiar wood can be argued to be among the rarest materials in the known universe.
