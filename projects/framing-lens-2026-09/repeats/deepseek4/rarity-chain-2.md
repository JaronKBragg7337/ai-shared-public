**Granite.**

It is familiar and common on Earth’s surface, but a case can be made that granite is cosmically very rare. Its main ingredients—silicon, oxygen, aluminum, potassium, sodium—are not rare, and it does not form only in one extremely narrow temperature/pressure window.

The key is the full chain of planetary processes needed: a rocky planet, differentiation, the formation of basaltic crust, plate tectonics, liquid water, subduction of hydrated crust, partial melting, and slow magma evolution/crystallization to produce large volumes of silica-rich rock. So far, Earth is the only known place where this full chain has operated on a large scale.

So the familiar rock of countertops and mountain ranges—**granite**—could be argued to be among the rarest materials in the known universe because of how much natural “processing” it takes to make it.
