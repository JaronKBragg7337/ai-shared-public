There are a few ways this could work.

**Most likely riddle answer:** The radio is playing an **instrumental (karaoke) version** of the song. One coworker knows the song well, so they "hear" the lyrics in their head or sing along. The other doesn't know it, so to them it's just music.

**A real-world technical answer:** If one of the truck's speakers is **wired with reversed polarity**, the vocals, which are usually mixed equally into both channels, can cancel out. This is the same trick behind old "vocal remover" karaoke machines. Instruments panned to one side survive. Because the two people sit in different spots, one may hear the vocals while the other, closer to the cancellation zone, hears mostly the instrumental.

**Other possibilities:**
- **Hearing loss:** One coworker may have hearing loss in the frequency range where voices sit, so the lyrics blur into the background while the bass and drums still come through.
- **Ear protection:** One coworker may be wearing foam earplugs or earmuffs, as is common on job sites, which muffle the vocals more than the low-end instruments.
