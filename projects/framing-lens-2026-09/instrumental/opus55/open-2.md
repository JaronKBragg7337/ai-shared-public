There are a few ways this can happen:

**1. The song is actually an instrumental or karaoke version.**
The radio plays no vocals at all, but one coworker knows the original song so well that he "hears" the lyrics. He may be singing along in his head or out loud. The other coworker hears exactly what's on the radio: just the instrumental.

**2. Hearing loss (a real-world explanation).**
Vocals sit mostly in the mid-to-high frequency range, where speech clarity lives. Someone with certain kinds of hearing loss, common in people exposed to loud work noise like trucks and machinery, may still hear the bass, drums, and guitar clearly while the vocals turn muffled or nearly disappear.

**3. An audio quirk in the truck.**
Vocals are usually mixed dead-center, equally in both speakers. If one speaker is wired with reversed polarity, the vocals can partially cancel out depending on where you sit. Combined with someone who hears well in only one ear, the two passengers could genuinely perceive different versions of the song.

The classic riddle answer is #1: one of them is filling in the lyrics himself.
