There are several ways this could happen, even with the same song, radio, and volume.

**Differences in the listeners**
- **Hearing differences:** Some hearing loss mainly affects the frequencies where consonants and speech sit (roughly 2-8 kHz). That person hears the bass, drums, and melody but struggles to make out the words, so the song sounds almost instrumental.
- **Language:** If the lyrics are in a language one coworker doesn't understand, the vocals register as just another instrument, not as lyrics.
- **Attention:** One person may be tuned out of the vocals while focused on driving, the road, or the beat. Some people also naturally focus on the music and barely process words.

**Differences in their situation**
- **Hearing protection or devices:** One may be wearing earplugs, ear defenders, or a hearing aid that handles music differently from speech.
- **Seat position:** In a truck cab, the speakers may be unevenly placed. Stereo mixes often put the vocals in the center, and the car's speaker layout can make them clearer for one seat than the other.
- **Noise:** Engine, wind, or tools nearby can mask the vocal range for one person more than the other.

**A less common cause**
- **Phase cancellation:** If one listener's speaker is wired out of phase, center-panned vocals can partially cancel while the rest of the mix remains. This is the trick behind some "vocal remover" effects.
