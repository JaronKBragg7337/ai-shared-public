This is a classic lateral thinking puzzle. The key lies in understanding how human perception interacts with music structures, even when both people are hearing the exact same audio signal.

Since neither coworker is singing and the radio broadcast itself hasn't changed, the difference must come from their individual brains processing what they hear. Songs typically consist of lyrics during the **verse** sections (where the story or message is delivered) and music only during the **chorus** or instrumental breaks.

Therefore, one coworker must be paying close attention to the parts of the song containing words, while the other is distracted by those sections or simply not focusing on the lyrical content at all. This causes them to interpret the same auditory input differently: one hears the narrative and lyrics because they are listening to them, while the other perceives only the music during the moments when no one is singing.

**Answer:** One coworker is paying attention (focusing) on the parts of the song with words, while the other is distracted or not listening as intently.
