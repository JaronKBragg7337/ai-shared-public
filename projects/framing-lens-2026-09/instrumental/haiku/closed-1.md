The sound reaching their ears may be identical, but what they *perceive* depends on the listener. Some possibilities:

- **Language:** If the lyrics are in a language one person doesn't understand, the vocals may register as just another sound, like a synth line or a horn. The other person parses the words and hears "lyrics."
- **Hearing differences:** One person may have hearing loss in the speech-frequency range (roughly 500 Hz to 4 kHz), where vocals sit. Vocals can fade while bass and drums still come through clearly. Someone with auditory processing differences may also struggle to separate voice from instruments in a noisy cab.
- **Attention:** Selective attention can filter a voice out. If one person is focused on the guitar, drums, or the road, they may not separate the words from the mix, even though the audio is the same.
- **Prior knowledge:** If one person knows the song well, their brain fills in or pulls out the lyrics. The other, who doesn't know it, may hear only melody and rhythm.

The most likely answer is language or hearing, since both change which parts of the sound get through to the brain. The radio delivers the same signal to both; the difference is in how each person's ears and brain process it.
