The sound waves are the same, but **hearing happens in the brain**, and the brain doesn't process identical input identically. Several things could explain it:

- **Attention:** The person listening for lyrics may be tuned into the vocals, while the other is focused on the drums, the bass line, or the conversation. Attention can filter sound so that a voice "pops out" for one listener and blends into the background for the other.
- **Language:** If the lyrics are in a language one person doesn't understand, they may hear sounds but not words, so the song seems like music without lyrics. Even in a language they know, a mumbled or heavily produced vocal might register as "just singing" to one person and as words to another.
- **Familiarity:** Someone who knows the song may automatically fill in and hear the words, while someone hearing it for the first time only catches the melody.
- **Hearing differences:** One person may have mild high-frequency hearing loss, which blurs consonants and makes lyrics hard to pick out, even at the same volume. Tinnitus or age-related changes can have similar effects.
- **Position:** Even in the same cab, one person may sit closer to a speaker, have a window open toward road noise, or be seated where the vocals are louder or quieter relative to the instruments.
- **Mindset and expectations:** If one person expects a song to have lyrics, their brain may actively look for them, while the other person's brain may not.

So the "same" experience is really two different perceptions of the same sound, shaped by who's listening, what they know, and where they're focusing.
