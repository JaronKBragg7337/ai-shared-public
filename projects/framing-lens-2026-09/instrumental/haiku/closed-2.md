The sound reaching their ears is the same, but **hearing lyrics is more than detecting sound.** It also requires decoding the words, and that depends on the listener. A few possibilities:

1. **Language:** The song might be in a language one of them understands. To the other, the vocals are just another melodic line, like the instruments. This is the most likely answer.
2. **Hearing differences:** One might have mild loss in the speech-frequency range (roughly 1–4 kHz), where consonants and vowels are clearest. Vocals would blur into the music while the bass and drums stay clear. Tinnitus or differing hearing in each ear could have a similar effect.
3. **Auditory processing:** Some people have difficulty separating a voice from background instruments, or converting fast sung syllables into words. Hearing the lyrics takes more effort or simply doesn't happen for them.
4. **Attention:** One person may be tuned into the vocals while the other is focused on the rhythm or conversation, so the words either register or slide past.

Language is the cleanest explanation: the same song can be a full set of lyrics to one listener and pure music to another.
