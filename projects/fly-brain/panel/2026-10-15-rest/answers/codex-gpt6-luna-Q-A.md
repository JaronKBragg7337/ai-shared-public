Keep the rest system for now, but treat it as an experiment, not as an effective energy limit.

The run shows that the budget is only a nudge: after it emptied, Sapling still made 37 consolidations and spent 10,500 seconds against a 3,600-second budget. The stop drive rose only after 20 rests in a row, and even then it was lower than rest, explore, and practice. So the system neither capped compute nor reliably stopped it. Still, it produced observable choices and a stop report, and the measured energy use was modest. Turning it off would remove that evidence without clearly speeding useful learning.

The 4-hour budget change makes a fresh observation worthwhile. I’d keep the current behavior long enough to compare runs, while judging progress by exam results, memory quality, and writing samples—not generation count alone.

I’d change my mind if repeated runs show rest and stopping mostly displace useful work without improving learning or keeping compute use meaningfully aligned with the budget. I’d also reconsider if the owner’s goal changes from observing uncapped behavior to enforcing a daily compute limit.
