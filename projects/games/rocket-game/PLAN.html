A rocket game a kid gets on the first try

The joy of Kerbal Space Program, on a phone. Real physics underneath, almost nothing to learn on top. Built only by Anthropic models, led by Opus.

The table rules

Six rules every screen has to pass

  • 1

    Three choices to start. Nose, tank, engine. More parts come one at a time, as rewards.

  • 2

    One thing to steer. A lean control. No menus during flight.

  • 3

    One goal on screen. Never a list. "Touch the clouds." Then the next.

  • 4

    Real physics, hidden math. Gravity, air, fuel weight and orbits all work like the real thing. The kid feels it; numbers stay off unless a grown-up turns them on.

  • 5

    Crashing is funny, never a punishment. A puff, a wobble, "Try a bigger tank?" and you're back in the hangar in one tap.

  • 6

    It looks real. A real-looking planet, sky, sunrise and flame. Simple to play does not mean blocky.

The four screens

What the kid sees

1 · Hangar

The rocket stands in the middle. Three big buttons under it. Tap "Tank" and it cycles short, medium, tall; the rocket grows right there. One big orange Launch button. The goal sits at the top in one line.

2 · Flight

The camera follows the rocket up through real clouds; the sky darkens to black as the air thins. One lean slider at the bottom. A fuel bar on the side, no numbers. When fuel runs out you hear the engine cough, and you see what happens next.

3 · Map

One tap zooms out to the whole planet with your path drawn as a line. A line that hits the ground means you'll fall back. A line that wraps all the way around means orbit. That one picture teaches orbits better than any number.

4 · Result

Every flight ends on one card: what you reached and what you unlocked. A crash gets a friendly hint instead ("Your rocket ran out of fuel. Try the tall tank?"). One button back to the hangar.

Sitting at the table

Five calls for you to make

Real-size Earth or a smaller planet?

On a real-size Earth, orbit takes a huge rocket and perfect flying. Kerbal shrank its planet to about a tenth of Earth so orbit is reachable while the physics stay real.

My pick: smaller planet, real rules, like Kerbal did

Lean with a slider or by tilting the phone?

Tilt feels magic but is hard in a car or lying down. A slider always works.

My pick: slider first, tilt as an option

Flat path or full 3D flying?

Real-looking 3D everywhere, but the flight path stays on one flat plane, so you only ever lean left or right. Full 3D steering adds a second control.

My pick: 3D look, flat path

A pilot in the capsule?

A real-looking little astronaut who waves at launch and cheers at goals would give it a face, and kids love a crew.
