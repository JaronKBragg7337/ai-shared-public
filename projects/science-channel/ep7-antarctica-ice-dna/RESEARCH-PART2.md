**Watch-outs for the script (read first):**
- **This year's snow does NOT trap this year's air.** Air keeps flowing down through the loose snow for decades, so the air that
  gets sealed is always much *younger* than the ice around it. At Dome C the air is ~2,000 years younger than its ice. [3][4]
- **The latest Mauna Loa CO2 number is 426.07 ppm (September 2026), but that is the seasonal low point.** The 2026 peak was higher
  (June 2026 = 431.44 ppm, Part 1). Say "about 426–431 ppm in 2026", not one number as "today". [10]
- **The 6-million-year Allan Hills air was used for DATING (argon) and the ice for temperature. No CO2 number from it has been
  published that I could find.** Don't say "6-million-year-old CO2 was X". [7][8]
- **The oldest Allan Hills air (2019 study) read over 1,600 ppm CO2, but scientists threw that out:** microbes near the bedrock
  had added CO2. Good story, but it is a fake reading, not ancient air. [9]
- **"100,000-year-old glacier ice" sold to Dubai bars comes from icebergs floating in a Greenland fjord; the age is the company's
  marketing claim, not a measured date.** [16][17]

## 1. How air gets trapped
*Summary: Snow piles up, gets squeezed into "firn" (old packed snow, still full of open air channels), and 50–120 m down it turns
to ice and seals the air into bubbles. Deeper still, the bubbles vanish into a crystal form called air hydrate.*

**Snow → firn → ice**
- Fresh snow is mostly air. As more snow falls on top, it packs into **firn**, a crunchy, porous layer. The top few tens of metres
  of firn are still **open to the sky**: air moves through it. [1]
- At the bottom of the firn (the **firn–ice transition**, usually **50–120 m** down, depending on the site) the pores pinch shut
  and the air is locked into bubbles. [1][3]
- Close-off doesn't happen at one exact line. There is a **"lock-in zone"**: at WAIS Divide (West Antarctica) it is about
  **10 m thick, starting near 67 m** down. [2] Air at a given depth is a blend of a few years: at Law Dome the air in one
  sample spans **10–12 years**. [5]
- Depth differs by place: **~70 m at Summit, Greenland (-30s °C)**, **~120 m at the South Pole (about -50 °C)**. Colder, drier
  sites densify more slowly. [6]

**How long it takes (and why the air is younger than the ice)**
- Where lots of snow falls, firn becomes ice in **a few decades**; where very little falls, it takes **a few thousand years**. [1]
- But air keeps mixing down through the open firn, so the air at the sealing depth is **young**, while the ice around it is old:

| Site | Snow | Ice age at sealing depth | Air age at sealing depth | Gap (air younger than ice) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Law Dome DE08 (coastal East Antarctica) | very high | ~40 years (at 72 m) [5] | ~9 years (mean, bottom of firn) [5] | ~30 years |
| Summit, Greenland | medium | ~220 years (at ~70 m) [6] | ~10 years [6] | ~200 years |
| EPICA Dome C (high East Antarctic plateau) | very low | — | — | **~2,000 years** today-type climate; **~5,500 years** in the last Ice Age [3] |

- This gap is called **Δage** ("delta age"). It is calculated with computer models of how firn packs down, and it is still debated:
  a 2007 study found the model **overestimates** the gap at Dome C during the Ice Age. [3][4]
- At Vostok, about 108,000 years ago, the gap was measured at **5,350 ± 300 years**. [4b]

**Annual layers**
- Summer and winter snow look and behave differently, leaving yearly stripes, like tree rings. In one 19 cm piece of the GISP2
  (Greenland) core from 1,855 m deep, you can count **11 years**. Deeper down, layers thin and blur and other dating tools take over
  (volcanic ash, chemistry, electrical conductivity, flow models). [1]

**Air hydrates (air clathrates)**
- Deeper, the weight of the ice squeezes the bubbles. Eventually the air is forced **into the ice crystal itself**, forming
  **air hydrate** (clathrate): a cage of water molecules holding gas molecules. The bubbles disappear. [1][11]
- Dome Fuji (Antarctica): hydrates first appear at **~600 m**, and **no bubbles at all below ~1,100 m**. GRIP (Greenland):
  hydrates first at **~1,000 m**, last bubbles gone at **~1,500 m**. The overlap is long because the change is very slow. [11]

**Cores that crack, pop and "relax"**
- In the middle depths, bubbles in a fresh core hold air at about **50 bar** (50 times normal air pressure). Small knocks can make
  bubbles burst and the ice crack. Drillers call this the **"brittle zone."** [12]
- Below it, pressure passes ~100 bar, the air goes into hydrates, and the ice becomes strong and easy to handle again. [12]
- Once on the surface, the ice **"relaxes"**: it expands a little and **bubbles re-appear** as hydrates break down, sometimes with
  tiny cracks. Teams store brittle cores for a while and handle them "like eggs." [11][12]
- (Popping sound in a drink: see section 3.)

## 2. What ancient air is made of
*Summary: The same nitrogen and oxygen as today, but much less CO2 and methane. Scientists free it by crushing, melting or
vaporizing the ice inside a vacuum, then measure it.*

**The basics**
- Air today is ~78% nitrogen, ~21% oxygen, ~0.9% argon, plus traces. [GK] Ice cores show oxygen has **fallen only ~0.7%** in
  800,000 years (Part 1, Stolper 2016). So ancient air is, to your lungs, ordinary air. [Part 1 source 23]

**CO2: then vs now**
- **EPICA Dome C, last 800,000 years: about 172–300 ppm.** Low (~180 ppm) in ice ages, ~280–300 ppm in warm spells. [13]
  (The oldest part was corrected in 2015; the overall range holds.) [13]
- **Pre-industrial (around 1750): ~280 ppm.** [GK; consistent with Law Dome record 5]
- **2026: Mauna Loa monthly average 426.07 ppm in September 2026** (424.37 a year earlier); the yearly peak in mid-2026 was
  ~431 ppm. Page updated 9 Oct 2026. [10]
- So today's air has **roughly 1.5 times** the CO2 of the warmest ancient air in the 800,000-year record. [10][13]

**Methane**
- Over the last **650,000 years: about 350 ppb (ice ages) to 800 ppb (warm times)** (EPICA Dome C, Loulergue et al. 2008). [14]
- **2026: global average 1,934.57 ppb (June 2026)**, vs 1,929.17 a year earlier (NOAA). That's **about 2.4 times** the highest
  ancient value. [15]

**What the Allan Hills ice air showed**
- **6 million years old (Shackleton, Higgins et al., PNAS, 28 Oct 2025):** the age was read from the air itself. Earth's atmosphere
  slowly gains **argon-40** (from radioactive potassium in rocks), so older air has slightly less of it. The ice showed
  **~12 ± 2 °C of cooling** in Antarctica since 6 million years ago. The pieces are scattered, not a continuous timeline. [7][8]
  - **CO2 and methane from the 6-million-year air: not published yet** (described as ongoing work). [8]
- **2 million+ years (Yan et al., Nature, Oct 2019, also Allan Hills):** CO2 in the "41,000-year-cycle world" (~1–2 million years
  ago) was **about 204–289 ppm**. Ice-age CO2 back then averaged ~20 ppm higher than in later ice ages. The very oldest samples read
  **over 1,600 ppm**, but that was **contamination: microbes near the bedrock breathed out CO2**. [9]
- **Beyond EPICA (1.2 million years, continuous):** first CO2 results were presented at the European Geosciences Union meeting
  (spring 2026), but only as a talk; **actual numbers not yet published.** [18]

**How they get the air out**
- **Dry extraction (most common for CO2):** crush the ice in a cold vacuum chamber with needles, steel balls, blades, or a
  "cheese grater." Releases only **50–80%** of the air in many devices. [19]
- **Wet extraction (melting in a vacuum):** used for methane. Avoided for CO2 because melting can make **extra CO2** (carbonate dust
  reacting with acid in the meltwater). [19]
- **Sublimation:** turn the ice straight to vapor in a vacuum (no melting), freeing almost all the air. Slower. [19]
- **Newest (2026):** a laser vaporizes the ice and a laser spectrometer reads CO2, methane and nitrous oxide from just **1–2 mL of
  air out of 10–15 grams of ice** (about a tablespoon of ice). Used on Beyond EPICA. [18]

## 3. Could a person breathe it?
*Summary: Yes, and it's harmless: it's normal air with less CO2 and no modern smoke or traffic pollution. People already get a whiff
of it when glacier ice fizzes in a drink. No health benefits are established.*

**Is it safe?**
- Ancient air is ordinary nitrogen-and-oxygen air. Its CO2 (~180–300 ppm) is *lower* than outdoor air today (~426 ppm) and far lower
  than a normal room (often 600–1,000 ppm). The US workplace safety limit for CO2 is **5,000 ppm** over an 8-hour day. [13][10][20]
- It was sealed before cars, factories and modern smoke, so it holds none of today's pollution particles. [GK; inference from
  sealing dates]
- **Caveat:** the ice itself is not perfectly "pure." It holds dust, volcanic ash, sea salt and chemicals from its own time; that's
  exactly what scientists read in it. [1]
- How much air you'd actually get: tiny. A tablespoon of ice holds ~1–2 mL of air. [18] A single breath is about 500 mL. [GK]

**The fizzing ice in drinks**
- Glacier ice in a drink **pops and pings** because the bubbles inside are squeezed under high pressure by hundreds of feet of ice;
  as the ice melts, they burst. (Exploratorium field note from Antarctica, Paul Doherty, Dec 2001.) [21]
- Glaciologist **Paul Mayewski** (University of Maine) told a reporter that after analyzing 10,000-year-old ice samples, his team
  sometimes melts and drinks the leftovers on special occasions. (NPR, Robert Krulwich, Oct 2010.) [22]
- In Alaska, the US National Park Service calls the crackle of melting icebergs **"bergy seltzer."** [23] (Secondhand; the NPS page
  would not load for me.)
- **Products:** A Greenland startup, **Arctic Ice** (founded 2022), ships ice to bars in Dubai and markets it as frozen 100,000+
  years. It actually harvests **free-floating icebergs** in the Nuuk fjord, with a permit for up to 14,000 tonnes a year (first
  delivery Jan 2024). [16][17] Icebergs from Greenland glaciers are genuinely old, but **the specific age of any sold cube is not
  established** (no published dating). Oddly, a co-founder said the ice has **no bubbles**, which would mean no fizz. [16][17]
- Martha Stewart put "a small iceberg" in a cocktail in 2023 and it caused an online stir. [24]

**Asthma (careful: no medical claims)**
- The main outdoor air triggers for asthma, per the US EPA, are **ozone (smog)** and **particle pollution** (smoke, haze, dust).
  Ozone can also make people **more sensitive to allergens** such as pollen. [25]
- CO2 at outdoor levels is not listed as an asthma trigger. [25]
- Whether breathing ancient air would help anyone's asthma: **not established. No study exists.** The honest point is only that
  ancient air lacks today's smoke, ozone and pollen, the things EPA names as triggers. Clean air today (for example after rain, or
  on a good AirNow day) lacks most of them too. [25]

## 4. Air archives: libraries of bottled sky
*Summary: Since 1978, Australia has bottled the cleanest air on Earth at Cape Grim, Tasmania, a few times a year, so future
scientists can test it for things nobody knew to look for.*

**Cape Grim Air Archive (CSIRO + Bureau of Meteorology, Tasmania)**
- Station started 1976; **first archive sample 26 April 1978.** **4–6 cylinders a year** since. [26][27]
- **About 170 cylinders** in storage and usable (Wikipedia: "more than 170" as of 2022; of ~175 collected, 100–150 fully intact). [26][27]
  (One news repost says ~250; not confirmed.)
- **The first cylinders (1978–1988) were repurposed WWII containers** that had held breathing oxygen on US bombers (B-17s and B-24s);
  originally they were beverage containers. **Since the late 1980s:** purpose-built cylinders from Essex Industries (Missouri), with
  electropolished stainless-steel insides. Some early steel ones corroded and damaged their samples. [26][27]
- Air is stored at about **60 atmospheres** (900 psi), at CSIRO's lab in **Aspendale, near Melbourne**. [26][27]
- **Why Cape Grim:** wind arriving from the Southern Ocean has crossed thousands of km of sea, so it's some of the cleanest air on Earth. [26][GK]
- **What it's been used for:** more than **100 trace gases** measured, **100+ scientific papers**, used in UN Ozone Assessments and
  IPCC reports. [26]
- **Discovery:** in 2014, scientists used it (with Greenland firn air) to find **4 ozone-destroying gases nobody knew were in the air**
  (3 CFCs and 1 HCFC). The HCFC-133a record was stretched back to 1978 though direct measurement only began in 2014. [26][28]
- **Older air added:** a diver's old **scuba tank, filled in 1968**, was donated and its air pushed the record back before 1978. [27]
  (Sources disagree slightly on the tank's last fill, 1968 vs 1970.)

**Other air libraries**
- **NOAA (USA):** flask sampling since **1967** (Niwot Ridge, Colorado). Today's network uses **2.5-litre glass flasks** sealed with
  Teflon O-ring valves; samples are meant to stay unchanged for **weeks up to 1.5 years**. [29][30] NOAA also keeps tanks of air
  (some filled at Niwot Ridge decades ago) as calibration standards. [29] A full NOAA "archive inventory": **not found.**
- **Scripps (La Jolla, California):** Keeling started flask CO2 in **1956–57**. Scripps freezes CO2 out of its flask samples and
  **seals it in glass tubes** for an archive, which later let scientists measure radiocarbon in past air. [31][32]
  **When that tube archive started: not established.**
- **Firn air:** scientists also pump air straight out of the open firn; at Law Dome it covers **~65 years** of air. [5]

## 5. Today's air being sealed now
*Summary: Our 2026 air is sinking into the firn right now. At high-snow sites it will be locked into bubbles in roughly a decade
or so; at dry plateau sites the snow it ends up in is thousands of years older. Either way, future scientists will be able to read it.*

- 2026 air soaks down into the open firn like water into sand. [1][2]
- **At a high-snow site (Law Dome):** air at the sealing depth (~72 m) averages **~9 years old**, so **2026 air should be sealing
  into bubbles around the mid-2030s** (spread over about a decade). It will sit in ice from snow that fell **~40 years earlier**. [5]
  (Our own arithmetic from the published numbers.)
- **Summit, Greenland:** air at sealing depth is ~10 years old, in ice ~220 years old. [6]
- **Dry plateau sites (Dome C, Vostok):** the snow takes **thousands of years** to become ice, and the gap between air and ice is
  **~2,000 years** (warm climate). [1][3] **How old the air itself is when it seals there: not established** in what I found.
- **The snow that falls in 2026** will itself become ice in ~40 years (Law Dome), ~200+ years (Summit) or ~2,000+ years (Dome C),
  but it will trap **the air of that future time**, not 2026 air. [1][5][6][3]
- So yes: the 2026 sky, with its ~426–431 ppm CO2 and ~1,935 ppb methane, **is being written into Antarctic and Greenland ice right now.** [10][15]
- Scientists already read the 20th century this way: the Law Dome record runs from **1006 AD to 1978**, overlapping the Mauna Loa
  measurements. [5]

## 6. A home "air time capsule" (family science project)
*Summary: You can bottle air, but home jars leak through their seals over time, so the jar is a symbol. The real time capsule is
the DATA: measure your CO2, note the local air quality, and seal that record with the jar.*

**Containers**
- **Glass itself doesn't let air through; the seal does.** Even NOAA's lab flasks slowly **lose CO2 through their Teflon O-rings**,
  about **0.007 ppm per day** (roughly 2.5 ppm a year). [30] A kitchen jar with a rubber or silicone gasket will leak far more:
  **exact rate not established** (no test data found).
- **Lab-grade:** **electropolished stainless-steel canisters** ("SUMMA" canisters, like Cape Grim's) are what pros use; even so,
  standard US pollution tests only count on them for **~30 days** for many chemicals. Long storage works for stable gases (CO2,
  methane) in purpose-made cylinders like Cape Grim's. [26][33]
- Realistic limit: a home jar **cannot keep 2026 air accurately for decades**. It's a symbol and a conversation piece. [30][33]

**What to measure (cheap and honest)**
- **A real CO2 sensor uses "NDIR" (infrared) or photoacoustic sensing**, e.g. Sensirion SCD40 (accuracy ±50 ppm + 5%) or SCD30
  (±30 ppm + 3%). Cheap "eCO2/TVOC" sensors (CCS811, SGP30) **guess CO2 from other gases** and aren't true CO2 readings. [34]
- Measure outdoors (should read ~420–430 ppm) and indoors (often much higher with people in the room). [10][20]
- Write down that day's **AirNow AQI** (ozone + particles) and the **NOAA Mauna Loa monthly CO2**. [25][10]
- Seal the paper/USB with the jar: "Air of [date], [town]: CO2 ___ ppm outside, ___ inside; AQI ___; world average ___ ppm."
- Optional: fill the jar outside, away from cars and people, and label it with date, time, place and weather.

**Honest limits to say on camera**
- The jar's CO2 will drift; the written numbers won't. [30]
- Home sensors drift too; outdoors-fresh-air calibration (~420 ppm) helps. [34][10]

## Surprising true facts (episode-ready)
1. **The air in an ice core is younger than the ice around it**, by ~30 years on the coast (Law Dome) and ~2,000 years on the high
   plateau (Dome C). [3][5]
2. **Deep ice has no bubbles at all:** below ~1,100 m at Dome Fuji, the air is squeezed into the ice crystals as "air hydrate." [11]
3. **Ice cores can burst on their own:** bubbles at ~50 times air pressure make the "brittle zone" crack, so teams let cores rest
   for days. [12]
4. **Scientists dated 6-million-year-old ice by sniffing its argon**: older air has less argon-40. [7][8]
5. **Scientists can now read ancient CO2 from a tablespoon of ice** (10–15 g, 1–2 mL of air), using lasers. [18]
6. **One "2.7-million-year-old" CO2 reading of 1,600 ppm was fake**, caused by microbes at the bottom of the ice. [9]
7. **Australia has kept bottled air since 1978**, first in **repurposed WWII bomber oxygen tanks**. [26][27]
8. **That bottled air revealed four ozone-eating gases nobody knew existed** (2014). [28]
9. **A diver's 1968 scuba tank** became part of a climate archive. [27]
10. **Even NOAA's lab bottles leak a little CO2** through their seals: ~0.007 ppm a day. [30]
11. **Methane today (~1,935 ppb) is ~2.4 times the highest level in 650,000 years.** [14][15]
12. **2026 air is already sinking into the ice** and should be sealed into bubbles at high-snow sites by around the mid-2030s. [5]
13. **Glaciologists have drunk melted 10,000-year-old ice cores**; one said it tastes "about as clean as anything can taste." [22]

## Common misunderstandings
- **"This year's snow traps this year's air."** No: air flows down through the firn; the trapped air is decades to thousands of years
  younger than its ice. [1][3][5]
- **"Ancient air had less oxygen (or more)."** Oxygen dropped only ~0.7% in 800,000 years. The big change is CO2 and methane. [Part 1 #23][13][14]
- **"The 6-million-year ice told us the CO2 back then."** Not yet: it gave an age and a temperature. [7][8]
- **"Ice cores show CO2 was over 1,000 ppm 2+ million years ago."** That reading was microbial contamination. [9]
- **"Breathing ancient air is healthier / cures asthma."** Not established. It's just clean air. Today's asthma triggers are ozone,
  particles and allergens like pollen. [25]
- **"Glacier ice in a cocktail is guaranteed 100,000 years old."** That's marketing; commercial ice comes from loose icebergs with no
  published dating. [16][17]
- **"Bubbles last all the way down."** Below ~1 km they turn into air hydrates; the bubbles reappear only after the core comes up. [11][12]
- **"A jar of air keeps forever."** Seals leak; even lab flasks drift. Data lasts; the air doesn't. [30]
- **"Today's CO2 is one number."** It swings every year: ~426 ppm in September 2026, ~431 in June 2026. [10]
