Real method behind it: ultrasonic machining (vibrating tool + abrasive slurry) - see 1998 review doi:10.1016/S0890-6955(97)00036-9,
2015 mechanics doi:10.1016/j.ijmachtools.2015.05.006.

## Parts (~$60-100)
- Ultrasonic cleaner, ~40 kHz, small (~$30-60)
- Copper strip or short copper tube (hardware store, ~$5)
- Abrasive: silicon carbide grit (lapidary/rock-tumbler grit, ~$10) or plain quartz sand
- Stone samples: soapstone block (~$10), limestone/marble tile, granite tile offcut (free/cheap at tile shops)
- Digital caliper (~$15), kitchen scale 0.01 g (~$15), a small weight (a few coins in a bag), phone for macro photos

## Runs (same minutes, same weight, same grit each time; 3 runs each)
A. Copper + grit + water, cleaner ON   (vibration)
B. Copper + grit + water, cleaner OFF  (no vibration)
C. Copper + grit, DRY, hand-rubbed same strokes vs D. same, WET  (his "under liquid" question)
## Measure
Groove depth (caliper), mass lost (scale), edge chips/cracks (macro photo), minutes. Write results in results.md.
## Safety, one line
Unplug the cleaner before reaching in; wear eye protection when scraping stone.
