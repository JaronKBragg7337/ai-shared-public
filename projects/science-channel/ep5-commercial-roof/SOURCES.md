# Science 8.5 "How a commercial roof dies" - sources and what each claim rests on

The scene is a simulation (Blender), not footage. Every lifespan and timing is a **typical range** or an **estimate** and is
labelled as one on screen. Generic product types only (exposed-fastener metal, standing seam, TPO/PVC/EPDM membrane, silicone
and acrylic coatings); no brand is named or recommended. Checked with web search 2026-10-13.

## Claims in the narration, with sources

| Claim (as spoken / shown) | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Exposed-fastener metal roof: panels screwed through to the purlins, each screw sealed by a rubber (EPDM/neoprene) washer | Standard metal-building construction; washer sealing described in metal-roofing trade guides (metalroofingsource.com fastener guide; Florida Roofing "Fastener failure in a metal roofing world") | plain description |
| White single-ply membrane, PVC or TPO, heat-welded seams; rooftop units on curbs, drains, hatch, parapet | Standard low-slope assembly (NRCA Roofing Manual: Membrane Roof Systems; manufacturer TPO/PVC data sheets) | plain description |
| "A dark roof in summer can reach 150 degrees" | DOE Energy Saver, Cool Roofs (energy.gov/energysaver/cool-roofs): conventional roofs "can reach temperatures of 150 F or more on a sunny summer afternoon" | confirmed |
| "In an Indiana January the same steel sits below zero" | Fort Wayne record/typical January lows below 0 F (NWS climate normals: Fort Wayne mean Jan low ~18 F; sub-zero nights most winters) | typical, not sourced to a single page |
| "Steel grows and shrinks ... on a 40-foot panel roughly a quarter inch over a summer day, about half an inch across the year" | Linear expansion, ΔL = α L ΔT, steel α = 6.5-6.7 x 10^-6 /F (Construction Canada "Controlling thermal movement in metal panel roofs"; Professional Roofing 2003 article 354). 480 in x 6.5e-6 x 80 F = 0.25 in; x 160 F = 0.50 in. Industry rule of thumb 1/8 in per 10 ft (= 0.5 in per 40 ft). Theoretical values; real movement is partly restrained. | **ESTIMATE (arithmetic), labelled** |
| "A standing-seam roof rides that movement on sliding clips. Most exposed-fastener roofs have no allowance for it" (reworded after Codex: some through-fastened systems use factory-slotted holes) | Construction Canada (two- and three-piece clips let long panels slide); asktoddmiller.com and Rooferscoffeeshop on exposed-fastener products having "no allowance for the metal's natural expansion and contraction, so the holes wallow out" | confirmed |
| "The holes slowly wallow out ... washers bake, dry, and crack" | theroofingcompany.com (why exposed screws fail), greenbuildingadvisor.com (fasteners backing out from thermal movement, enlarged holes), Florida Roofing (washers/grommets dry out and crack) | confirmed (trade sources) |
| "The sun's ultraviolet works on the top layer" (TPO/PVC) | IIBEC Technical Advisory 12 (TPO performance and service life); GAF "Evaluating real-world performance of field-aged TPO" (erosion of the top ply over the scrim = failure point); PVC plasticizer migration and embrittlement (patents US9097018, EP3216831; IIBEC aged-membrane thickness study) | confirmed |
| "Plain zinc-plated screw heads often rust before the panel does; long-life fasteners hold up better" (reworded after Codex; MBCI long-life fastener guidance) | Common field observation in metal-roof restoration guides (pmsilicone.com metal roof coatings: "fasteners rust first"); Galvalume/galvanized panel coatings vs zinc-plated carbon-steel screws (long-life screws are stainless or zinc-aluminum capped). | "Water sits in the low spots for days after a rain. | Membrane roofs designed to 1/4 in per foot slope (on-screen) | IBC 1507 (all membrane roof coverings except coal-tar BUR: minimum design slope 1/4:12); NRCA positive-drainage recommendation | confirmed |

| "A welded seam that was never fully bonded opens into a fishmouth"; punctures from dropped screws and tools; curb flashing cracking | ERDC (US Army) roof moisture surveys: flashing defects around penetrations and walls were the major cause of wet insulation; IIBEC single-ply testing article; general single-ply failure modes (seam voids, punctures from rooftop traffic) | confirmed in outline |
| "Water runs along the deck ribs before it finds a way down" (stain far from the hole) | Standard roof-leak investigation guidance (water travels along deck flutes); ERDC survey reports | plain description |

| "Wet insulation loses much of its insulating value" | PIMA and manufacturer data show small losses for short exposures (polyiso retains ~95 % after a 28-day test); long-term saturation in a roof is far worse and is why wet insulation is cut out (ERDC surveys; manufacturer restoration specs). Said as "much of", with an ESTIMATE tag. | **ESTIMATE, labelled** |
| "The steel deck underneath rusts from the top" | Corrosion of steel decks under wet insulation is the stated reason manufacturers require wet areas removed before restoration (Progressive Materials installation overviews; GAF) | confirmed in outline |

| Restoration steps: power wash, replace loose screws with larger ones, rust-inhibiting primer, fabric and sealant at seams and fastener heads, adhesion test | GAF acrylic/silicone metal restoration 5-step guides and Kymax spec (adhesion 2.0 pli; tighten/replace fasteners and encapsulate; power wash and dry); American WeatherStar and pmsilicone.com metal restoration pages (oversized fasteners); Liquid Rubber / Roof Mate seam-tape and three-course details | confirmed |

## Not shown as fact
- The specific roof, its slope, deck condition, crew and the 15-year clock are the simulation's choices.
- The thermal curves in the cutaway are typical shapes, not measurements; the 5,500 cycles figure is 365 x 15.
- No tax or accounting treatment of restoration vs replacement is claimed.
- Hail, wind uplift and foot-traffic rules of specific manufacturers are not covered.

### What changed after the Codex check (2026-10-13)
- WRONG "an exposed-fastener roof cannot" accommodate movement -> "most exposed-fastener roofs have no allowance for it" (some systems use factory-slotted holes). Also in the heat diagram.
- WRONG "rust ... around every hole" -> "around the holes where the washers have failed"; screw-rust explanation reworded to plain zinc-plated vs long-life fasteners (on screen too).
- WRONG "two choices" / "depends on one thing" -> patch, recover, coat or replace; a coating depends first on moisture, then deck, adhesion, drainage (narration + THE CHOICE label).
- WRONG "every loose screw ... a larger one" -> tighten or replace; oversized screws where holes wore out (narration + RESTORATION label).
- WRONG "each screw head ... fabric" -> heads sealed over; laps, seams and penetrations reinforced, often with fabric.
- WRONG "two coats" -> "one or two coats depending on the product" (narration + COATING label).
- WRONG IBC citation -> 2018 s.1511.3.1 / 2021 s.1512.2.1 (label, end card, this file).
- WRONG 0.55 as "the standard" -> described as a value used by some programs; CRRC sets no minimum (this file; not on screen).
- UNSURE softened: "dozens of feet" -> "well beyond the hole"; "wet for years" -> "wet for a long time" (narration + cutaway); "adds 10-20 yr" -> "carries a 10-20 yr warranty" and the last label is "AFTER RESTORATION", not "YEARS 15-35"; EPDM shown as ~15-30 yr with "sources vary widely"; "usual leak points" -> "common leak points"; acrylic "most need positive drainage".
