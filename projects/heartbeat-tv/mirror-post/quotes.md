# Sourced AI risk numbers and timelines

Checked 12 November 2026. Each row is a public statement with a number or a dated timeline. "Check" says whether the words were read on the source page or post in this pass. Short quotes are the speaker's words. Paraphrase in a news lede is not treated as a quote.

These numbers do not measure the same thing. Some are extinction, some are "things go badly," some are takeover, and some are when a capability arrives. A capability date is not a doom probability.

## Reuse

Nothing in this list is cleared as Creative Commons or as a public-domain government video.

X posts can be quoted in short. The video attached to an X post is still copyrighted. BBC Radio 4, WIRED, Axios, *The Joe Rogan Experience*, TED, and Lex Fridman are copyrighted. A TED talk is often released under CC BY-NC-ND, which does not allow an edited YouTube reuse. That license was not checked talk by talk, so no TED clip is cleared here.

A US Senate or House video produced by Congress would be a US government work. This pass did not find one in which any of these people states a percentage. Links are enough.

## For the video

The spread is the point. Yampolskiy is at 99.999999%. Hendrycks is above 80%. Mostaque is at 50%. Amodei, Bengio, Hinton, Musk, and Buterin sit between about 10% and 25%, and they are not answering the same question. The published-researcher median on extinction is 5%. LeCun says the risk is essentially zero and also says he did not say zero.

Say what each number is. Christiano's 46% is an irreversibly messed-up future within 10 years of powerful AI. His figure for most humans dying in that window is 20%, and his takeover figure is 22%. Amodei's 25% is "things go really, really badly," paired with 75% that they go really well. Hinton's 10%, then 10–20%, is extinction inside roughly 30 years. Musk's October 2024 line is a 10–20% chance it "goes bad." The February 2025 line is about a 20% chance of annihilation and an 80% chance of a good outcome.

Keep capability dates in a separate beat from doom probabilities. Hassabis's 50% is a chance of AGI in five to ten years. Altman's "few thousand days" is superintelligence, with an explicit "it may take longer." Amodei's "as early as 2026" is powerful AI, and the essay is about the upside if it goes right. Huang's five years is doing well on human tests. Son's 2035 is his ASI date. The researcher survey's 10% by 2027 and 50% by 2047 is the chance machines outperform humans at every task, which is a different question from the 5% extinction median in the same survey.

Hinton moved. In October 2023 his own post says 0.1 over 30 years if AI is not strongly regulated. In December 2024 he told the BBC "10% to 20%." Hendrycks says he was near 20% and then above 80%.

LeCun is the clean skeptic quote, in his own posts: the risk is essentially zero, it is smaller than an extinction-level asteroid in the next millennium, it is much less likely than a nuclear holocaust, and he did not say the probability is zero. Andrew Ng, on 31 October 2023, called human-extinction risk overhyped and gave no percentage: https://x.com/AndrewYNg/status/1719378661475017211

Yudkowsky should be used from the September 2026 post. The conditional claim is "Yes" if superintelligence is built by anything like current methods. He says he does not have an equally solid number for whether humanity builds it. A title card that says "Yudkowsky: 95%" would be ahead of what this pass could verify.
