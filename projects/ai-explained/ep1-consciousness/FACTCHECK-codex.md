# Codex fact-check (gpt-6-luna, read-only, high effort)

## Round 1 (script as first written)
## Segment check

**[a1] RIGHT** — The opening presents first-person experience as the starting point for the question; that is framing rather than a disputed study claim. **Suggested wording:** Keep as written.

**[a2] UNSURE** — The “only consciousness anyone observes directly” claim is a philosophical framing. But “mostly from what people say” is too narrow: researchers also use behavior, brain measures, and other physiological evidence, then compare those measures with reports where possible. **Suggested wording:** “We have direct access to our own experience. For others, scientists infer consciousness from reports, behavior and brain activity, and compare those clues where possible.”

**[a3] RIGHT** — PCI uses a brief transcranial magnetic stimulation pulse and EEG to quantify the complexity of the brain’s response. The 2013 study reported high PCI in wakefulness and REM sleep, including cases with reported dreams, and lower values in NREM sleep and anesthesia. **Suggested wording:** “In 2013, Casali, Massimini and colleagues found complex responses in wakefulness and REM sleep, and simpler ones in deep sleep and anesthesia.” [Casali et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.3006294)

**[a4] RIGHT** — The 2016 study used a benchmark group of 150 people whose reports were used to identify conscious and unconscious conditions. The cutoff separated those benchmark conditions with 100% sensitivity and specificity; 36 of 38 minimally conscious patients and 9 of 43 patients diagnosed as vegetative scored above it. The result suggests possible covert consciousness in some of those patients, but does not prove it. **Suggested wording:** “In this study’s benchmark group, the cutoff separated reported conscious and unconscious conditions perfectly. It was above cutoff in 36 of 38 minimally conscious patients and 9 of 43 patients diagnosed as vegetative.” [Gosseries et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5132045/)

**[a5] RIGHT** — PCI measures complexity of the brain’s response, not the contents of experience. Its benchmark relied on reports, including reports made after sleep or anesthesia; “calibrated on people who could report” is fair, though they did not all report while the pulse was being delivered. **Suggested wording:** “PCI is a clue about a level of consciousness, not a readout of what someone experiences.” [Gosseries et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5132045/)

**[a6] RIGHT** — Owen and colleagues’ 2006 report was a single patient diagnosed as vegetative. During fMRI, she followed instructions to imagine playing tennis or moving around her home; the predicted brain regions activated in a manner the paper described as indistinguishable from healthy volunteers. The 2024 study enrolled 353 patients; 60 of the 241 without observable bedside command responses showed cognitive motor dissociation on task-based fMRI or EEG (25%). **Suggested wording:** “In 2006, one patient followed imagery instructions during fMRI. In 2024, 60 of 241 patients without observable bedside command responses showed task-related brain responses on fMRI or EEG.” [Owen et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1130197); [2024 study](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2400645)

**[a7] RIGHT** — The 38% figure is correct: 43 of 112 patients who responded to spoken commands at bedside also showed responses on task-based fMRI or EEG. A failed scan task does not establish absence of consciousness. **Suggested wording:** “Among bedside responders, 43 of 112—38%—also passed a scan or EEG task. A missed signal does not show that a patient lacks awareness.” [2024 study](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2400645)

**[a8] UNSURE** — This is a compressed account of broad theories. Global workspace accounts emphasize broadcasting and ignition, but describing the front of the brain as playing “a key role” can imply that it is the sole or settled location. IIT associates consciousness with a system’s causal structure and predicts a posterior cortical “hot zone”; that is a theoretical proposal, not an established location. **Suggested wording:** “Global workspace theories emphasize widespread broadcasting, often involving frontoparietal networks. IIT links experience to integrated causal structure and predicts a posterior cortical hot zone.”

**[a9] RIGHT** — The Nature paper was published online on 30 April 2025. It reports 256 participants and fMRI, MEG and intracranial EEG. The paper found results that both aligned with some predictions and challenged key tenets: sustained posterior synchronization was lacking, and the predicted ignition at stimulus offset was generally absent. Avoid implying that either result settles the theory; the study tested specific predictions in a particular visual task. **Suggested wording:** “In this visual study, the preregistered results supported some predictions and challenged others: sustained posterior synchronization was lacking, and the predicted ignition at image offset was generally absent.” [Cogitate Consortium, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1)

**[a10] RIGHT** — A letter signed by 124 researchers appeared on PsyArXiv on 15 September 2023 and argued that IIT should be called pseudoscience. Chalmers publicly called that label disproportionate. The 2026 paper is a published **protocol** for planned tests in non-human primates and mice, not a results paper; saying “we found no results yet” is accurate as of this check. **Suggested wording:** “A 2026 published protocol lays out planned tests in non-human primates and mice; it reports the design, not the study’s results.” [Letter, 2023](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/zsr78); [PLOS ONE protocol, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0342770)

**[a11] RIGHT** — Gallup’s 1970 study reported mark-directed behavior in chimpanzees and not in the monkeys tested. The 2008 magpie result and 2019 cleaner-wrasse result were reported as mirror-mark-test passes, but interpretation and replication remain debated. The limitation is broader than the script suggests: the test probes mirror-guided mark-directed behavior, which is often interpreted as self-recognition; it is not a general test of consciousness. **Suggested wording:** “The mirror-mark test probes a narrow kind of mirror-guided behavior. Passing or failing it does not by itself settle whether an animal has conscious experience.” [Gallup, 1970](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.167.3914.86); [Prior et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.0060202); [Kohda et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2019.0129)

**[a12] RIGHT** — The declaration is dated 19 April 2024 and states strong scientific support for conscious experience in mammals and birds, and at least a realistic possibility in all vertebrates and many invertebrates, including cephalopods, decapod crustaceans and insects. It expresses a judgment about evidence and possibility, not a direct measurement. **Suggested wording:** “The declaration summarizes evidence and probability judgments; it is not a consciousness measurement.” [New York Declaration](https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/nydeclaration/declaration)

**[a13] UNSURE** — Butlin, Long and colleagues did derive theory-based indicators and assess AI systems against them in 2023. It is reasonable to say fluent chatbot text alone is weak evidence, but “weak evidence either way” could sound as though behavior has no evidential value; the paper’s method is itself an indirect assessment. **Suggested wording:** “Fluent talk about feelings, on its own, is not a reliable test. In 2023, Butlin, Long and colleagues assessed AI systems using indicators derived from leading theories.” [Butlin et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08708)

**[a14] RIGHT** — The 2023 analysis says it suggests no current AI systems are conscious and no obvious technical barriers to building systems that meet its indicators. The script appropriately qualifies this as theory-dependent. Clarify that this is the authors’ assessment using those indicators, not a definitive finding that AI consciousness is absent. **Suggested wording:** “Using their theory-derived indicators, the authors judged that no current AI systems were conscious, while finding no obvious technical barriers to future systems meeting the indicators.” [Butlin et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08708)

**[a15] WRONG** — “Right now, none can” overstates the case. Tests can provide evidence about consciousness in animals and AI, but there is no universally accepted, decisive test that establishes subjective experience across those cases. **Suggested wording:** “No test can settle the question decisively across fish, octopuses and machines, though some tests provide evidence that can inform judgments.” [New York Declaration](https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/nydeclaration/declaration); [Butlin et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08708)

**[a16] UNSURE** — Tests agreed in advance and measures validated beyond people who can report are reasonable research goals. But “partly, in people, and not yet beyond” is too categorical: animal-consciousness claims already have substantial evidence and evidence-based assessments, even though no direct, conclusive measurement is available. **Suggested wording:** “We can measure some brain signatures associated with levels of consciousness in people. For animals and AI, evidence can inform judgments, but no measure settles the question.”
