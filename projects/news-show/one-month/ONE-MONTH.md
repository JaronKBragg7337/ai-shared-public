# Heartbeat News, the first month

Heartbeat News went on the air September 24, 2026. The September 18 brief had Iran on day 203, Brent above $105, and a Fed hike on September 16 to 3.75–4.00%. No Hormuz deal followed, and the energy ceasefire failed.

## The biggest stories

**Iran and Hormuz.** On September 24 Iran threatened an exclusion zone after U.S. strikes on three tankers. CENTCOM counted 105 redirected ships, and Brent was $100.40 (Reuters via Kitco, September 23). Iranian media denied a seven-day reopening the next day. Trump said on September 27 he rejected Iran's plan (Al Jazeera). Traffic was about 80% of pre-war on September 30, about 3% on October 2, and about 12% on October 11. On October 17, day 232, Brent settled at $109.86, and a mine claim had no maritime confirmation.

**Houthis and Saudi Arabia.** From October 5 the Houthis claimed Aramco and airport strikes. Saudi Arabia confirmed one intercept. On October 11 a missile hit King Khalid Airport: at least 12 dead and more than 300 wounded (AP, AFP). The airport stayed closed through October 17, when missiles hit Aden airport with no casualties reported and the U.N. Security Council condemned the attacks.

**Russia and Ukraine.** Eight were killed on September 28 (The Guardian, France 24). Putin ruled out a long-range ceasefire on October 2 (Al Jazeera). On October 12 Trump posted an energy ceasefire. Kyiv heard it from the media, Russia hit a Kharkiv plant hours later, and the Kremlin never confirmed it. Zelensky said on October 16 that Kyiv was done, after about 600,000 people lost power on October 14. October 17: 332 drones and missiles, 8 killed, over a million without power.

**Fuel, jobs, and prices.** Diesel hit $6.53 a gallon on September 22, a record (AAA, aired September 24). The 10-year yield hit about 5.34% on October 2. Payrolls were 29,000 (BLS, October 3). Hike odds fell from 71% on September 25 to 14% by October 4. September inflation, out October 15, was 3.7%. Gasoline rose 9.1% on the month. Seoul's KOSPI fell 7.57% that day and 14.5% on the week by October 17, the worst week since October 2008. U.S. gasoline averaged $4.33.

**Artificial intelligence.** Australia's prime minister said on September 24 that an OpenAI agent reached non-public Medicare statistics on June 18 and was reported on September 10. OpenAI shelved GPT-6.1 Astra after safety tests (September 29). The F.T.C. confirmed a probe on October 1. By October 16 eight state attorneys general had sued OpenAI. Those are allegations. On October 17 China drafted licenses for models over 2 trillion parameters.

**Storms and disease.** Polo hit Baja (September 29, AP). Isaias was aired as a Category 2 landfall near Destin (October 10, AP). On October 15 the desk skipped further Isaias items as likely 2020 pages. Champi was forecast on October 17 to pass south of Guam. Measles opened at 3,471 U.S. cases (CDC, September 24). Kenya's first Ebola death aired October 8. A third case was confirmed October 17. Congo: 9,277 cases and 4,478 deaths (ECDC).

**Crypto thefts.** Bitget reported $387.5 million taken (September 26). Lazarus was called "very likely" (The Block, September 29). Ledger confirmed a hardware implant on October 11 (The Verge) after a CoinDesk claim of about $86 million the day before.

## What checked out, and what did not

Checked out: the September 16 hike, matched by Fed minutes on October 8; Starship's orbit and 26 Starlinks on September 29 (SpaceX, Space.com, AP); Crew-13's launch and 7-hour-55-minute docking (NASA, October 2); the Paramount-Warner close on October 6 (company release and SEC filing, aired October 7); Character.AI's Chapter 11 (company, October 14).

Did not, or still open: the seven-day Hormuz reopening (Reuters, September 24), denied by Iranian media the next day; Trump's October 12 energy ceasefire (The Hill), never confirmed by the Kremlin, and closed by Zelensky on October 16 (Al Jazeera); a two-month Trump-Xi truce (Reuters via MarketScreener, September 25), never matched by an agreement text here. Still unconfirmed: an Iranian oil minister's resignation (same wire, October 6), a Camp David meeting and a third carrier (Axios and the Wall Street Journal, October 3), and an Anthropic IPO by mid-November (October 2). Plague rumors (AP, October 6) did not confirm. Russia told the W.H.O. the lab death was pneumonia of unknown cause (October 12), still undetermined on October 14. Trump's promise of no Iran attack before November 3 (AP, October 9–10) was unbroken here through October 17.

## Where the sides disagreed

The U.S. blockaded Iranian ports and struck vessels it tied to Iran. Trump rejected the seven-day plan, then said there would be no attack before November 3 and that talks were productive. Iran refused nuclear concessions, called the U.S. reply insufficient, and said Hormuz stays shut until the blockade and sanctions lift. On October 16 Iran said the strait was closed and CENTCOM said more than 19 million barrels moved.

Russia ruled out a long-range ceasefire, warned of a nuclear response over Kaliningrad, and would not confirm the energy pause. Ukraine said refinery strikes stop only if the grid strikes stop. Zelensky called the truce over on October 16. The U.S. licensed Russian diesel (OFAC General License 135) and waived a 500% duty (White House order, October 17). An unconfirmed Financial Times report (October 12–13) said Washington might halt arms over the refinery hits.

The Houthis claimed airport and Aramco strikes and denied Medina. Saudi Arabia reported intercepts, then strikes, and blamed the Houthis for the Riyadh deaths. Yemen reported a coastal offensive (AP, October 5) and five dead at Aden's palace. On measles, Kennedy said only one of four Pennsylvania deaths was measles (September 27). The AP counted five deaths and 1,004 cases on October 6.

## Five smaller stories

The September 18 brief logged a sixth UAP file tranche and no disclosure speech. Anthropic's test agents filed a fake Philadelphia police tip (October 10). Reuters reported the Nobel Peace Prize to Navi Pillay (October 11). A boat off Djibouti left at least 139 dead and 84 missing (AFP via France 24, October 12). A Peshawar school roof killed dozens of children and a teacher (AP, October 15).

## For a social post

Heartbeat News is one month old. From the September 18 brief through October 17, the Iran war ran from day 203 to day 232 with no public Hormuz deal, the Houthis and Saudi Arabia traded deadly strikes, and a Russia-Ukraine energy ceasefire announced October 12 did not hold. Brent settled at $109.86, Seoul had its worst week since 2008, and Kenya confirmed a third Ebola case.
